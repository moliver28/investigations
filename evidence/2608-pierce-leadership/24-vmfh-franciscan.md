## System Snapshot

- **Full name:** Virginia Mason Franciscan Health (VMFH), formerly CHI Franciscan
- **Ownership:** Subsidiary of **CommonSpirit Health** (Chicago, IL) — one of the largest faith-based nonprofit health systems in the US
- **CommonSpirit scale:** ~150,000 employees, 25,000 physicians/APPs, **24 states**, 138 hospitals, ~$40B annual revenue (FY2025)
- **VMFH scale:** 10 hospitals + 300 care sites in WA; ~1,500 hospital beds; ~18,000–20,000 team members; ~5,600 employed/affiliated providers
- **Formed:** January 2021 merger of CHI Franciscan (Tacoma) and Virginia Mason (Seattle)
- **Catholic status:** VMFH is a **Catholic health system**; guided by the **Ethical and Religious Directives for Catholic Health Care Services (ERDs)**. Legacy Virginia Mason facilities remain non-Catholic in name but operate under the Catholic system.
- **HQ (regional):** 1145 Broadway Plaza, Tacoma, WA 98402
- **Founded:** 1891 (St. Joseph Hospital, founded by Sisters of St. Francis of Philadelphia)
- **WSHA member:** Yes — member of Washington State Hospital Association; spans WA legislative districts 5, 11, 23, 25–31, 33–37, 43, 46, 47

## 4. CommonSpirit Ownership Structure

- **CommonSpirit Health** formed **Feb 1, 2019** via alignment of **Catholic Health Initiatives (CHI)** and **Dignity Health** — a ~$29B merger.
- Built on legacy of **17 congregations of women religious** who founded health ministries.
- National office: **Chicago, IL**. ~150,000 employees, 25,000 physicians/APPs, **24 states**, 138 hospitals.
- VMFH is a **subsidiary/joint operating company** of CommonSpirit. Legacy CHI Franciscan and Virginia Mason had **equal board seats** at formation; Marvin O'Quinn (CommonSpirit president/COO) sat on the founding board.
- **Decision-making implication:** Pierce County hospital strategy is set in Chicago, not Tacoma. Local board is advisory/regional within CommonSpirit governance.
- **CommonSpirit finances (FY2025, ended June 30, 2025):** Revenue $40B (+8.5%); **operating loss $225M** (-0.6% margin, improved from FY2024's $875M loss); net income $1.1B (as recorded) / $1.6B (adjusted); EBITDA $1.9B (4.8% margin). Persistent operating losses driven by inflation + unfavorable payer behavior.

## 5. Catholic ERDs — Impact on Care (Key Power Dimension)

VMFH is guided by the **Ethical and Religious Directives for Catholic Health Care Services (ERDs)**. Documented impacts:

**Reproductive health restrictions (Cascade PBS, Dec 2024):**
- VMFH hospitals **do not provide direct abortions or in-vitro fertilization** in any facility.
- After the 2021 merger, employees were told the only care changes would be abortion and medical-aid-in-dying services.
- **Sept 2024:** Employee insurance benefits changed "to align" with CommonSpirit — **abortion covered only in life-threatening situations; vasectomies (male sterilization) no longer covered**; effective 2025.
- VMFH is **self-funded**, so federal ERISA preemption makes these cuts **technically legal** despite WA's strong abortion-coverage protections.
- **Seattle Family Birth Center closed Nov 15, 2024** (only VMFH birth center in Seattle) — 55 RNs displaced; WSNA held vigil. Closest remaining birth units: Burien, Tacoma, Federal Way (nearest ~12 miles).
- **Elective abortion and medical-aid-in-dying are banned** by ERDs — standard post-merger cuts.

**ERD scope:** The ERDs ban abortion (except to save mother's life), direct sterilization, IVF, and medical-aid-in-dying; restrict some end-of-life care. This shapes reproductive and end-of-life access across Pierce County, where VMFH is one of only two major hospital systems.

**Washington context:** WA law requires health plans covering maternity to also cover "substantially equivalent" abortion, but religiously affiliated carriers may opt out (state DOH then pays via Increasing Access to Reproductive Choice program). VMFH's self-funded status bypasses this.

## 7. Controversies & Legal Actions

**1. Charity care lawsuit (AG Bob Ferguson) — St. Joseph Medical Center, Tacoma**
- AG sued St. Joseph in 2017 for violating the state Consumer Protection Act by failing to make charity care accessible to low-income patients (2012–2017).
- **Resolution (April 2019, Pierce County Superior Court):** CHI Franciscan to forgive up to **$20M in debt**, pay **$2.22M in refunds** (5,451+ patients), pay **$2.46M** to AG's office, and rehabilitate patient credit. Total up to **$25M**.
- Findings: St. Joseph demanded payment up to 3x before offering charity care; required multiple income documents; revenue contractor **Conifer** trained staff to "never volunteer information about charity care" and to "avoid phrases that give patients the option to not pay." A 2014 internal email from a senior VP: "I'm afraid this is going to backfire on us."
- Required 5 years of charity care up to **300% of federal poverty level** (above the 200% legal floor).

**2. Antitrust lawsuit (AG Ferguson) — Kitsap County anti-competitive deals**
- AG sued CHI Franciscan in 2017 over acquisition of WestSound Orthopaedics + affiliation with The Doctors Clinic (TDC) in Kitsap County — combining the 3 largest orthopedic providers.
- **Resolution:** CHI Franciscan paid up to **$2.5M**, divested controlling interest in Silverdale ambulatory surgery center, barred from similar deals, required AG notice of future deals.
- Internal emails: CHI CFO Mike Fitzgerald — "I am all for taking advantage of hospital based pricing… drop a couple of million more to our bottom line." TDC physician president — "You can now get your outpatient care in a complex, relatively unsafe, and vastly more expensive location. You are welcome, Kitsap County…"
- Led to **HB 1607 (2019)** requiring 60-day AG notice for hospital mergers/acquisitions.

**3. Labor disputes — St. Joseph Medical Center nurses (WSNA)**
- Nov 1, 2024: 1,250 nurses held informational picket over unsafe staffing, pay, and use of travel nurses; 10-day strike notice from two specialized units.
- **Dec 19, 2024:** Nurses ratified new contract (93.6% approval) after 14 bargaining sessions. WSNA cited increased trauma cases (~100%) while hospital reduced licensed nursing assistants.

**4. Wage & hour class action (nurses)**
- Class action (filed 2019) by >7,000 VMFH employees over unpaid lunch/break time and off-the-clock work (FLSA + state law). **Settled for $5.5M** (~$510 avg per nurse).

**5. Privacy class action**
- **$3.5M settlement** resolving claims VMFH/Virginia Mason disclosed patients' personal/medical data to Facebook and Google via tracking tools on VirginiaMason.org and MyVirginiaMason portal.

**6. Layoffs / WARN notices (2025–2026)**
- **302 workers laid off across 8 WARN notices** (Jun 2025–Jan 2026) in WA, concentrated in Tacoma (Virtual Health Services, Insurance Verification units). Largest: 116 (Jun 5, 2025) and 24 (Sep 8, 2025) in Tacoma.

**7. SEIU Healthcare 1199NW campaign (Feb 2025)**
- Union campaign ("CommonSpirit is failing Washington") citing $24.7M consumer protection fines, executive compensation, and community hospital mission concerns. Union organizing push at VMFH facilities.

**8. 2022 overpayment settlement (UFCW 3000)**
- VMFH negotiated a wage overpayment recoupment plan with union after failing to bargain first.

## 8. Power Analysis — Why This Matters for Pierce County

- **Market duopoly:** VMFH (St. Joseph, St. Anthony, St. Clare, Wellfound) and MultiCare are the two dominant hospital systems in Pierce County. VMFH is MultiCare's **main Catholic competitor**.
- **Chicago control:** Pierce County's second-largest hospital system is owned by a Chicago-based national Catholic chain — local decisions (care, closures, staffing) are made remotely.
- **Faith-based care restrictions:** ERDs restrict reproductive and end-of-life care in a county where VMFH is a primary care provider — a structural limit on healthcare access.
- **Lobbying channel:** VMFH's CEO chaired WSHA (2024–25), giving a Chicago-owned Catholic system the top seat at WA's hospital lobbying association, alongside MultiCare's participation in the same HHFPAC channel.
- **Competitor-collaboration interlock:** VMFH and MultiCare jointly own/operate Wellfound Behavioral Health Hospital — a documented interlock between the county's two rival systems.
- **Board interlocks:** Diann Puls (Weyerhaeuser + Wellfound + Virginia Mason Institute); Chuck Arnold (Seahawks/Lumen Field); Karen Lee (Plymouth Housing + WA state government); John Oppenheimer (Columbia Hospitality + VMFH Foundation).
- **Financial fragility:** Parent CommonSpirit runs persistent operating losses ($225M FY2025) — pressure that drives the layoffs and service consolidation seen in Pierce County.
