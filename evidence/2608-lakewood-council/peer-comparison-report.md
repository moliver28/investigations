# Peer City Comparison Report: Lakewood WA Ethics Culture in Context

> **RESOLVED (2026-10-04, L016 → E046):** recounted under a written counting rule — Lakewood is **5 of 7 (71%)** vs a 0–29% peer range (median 14%). The "43%" figures in this file are superseded; see `dual-government-recount.md`.

**Prepared**: August 17, 2026  
**Investigation**: INV-2026-001 (2608-lakewood-council)  
**Purpose**: Determine whether Lakewood's zero-recusal culture, dual-government employment rate, and absent ethics enforcement body are outliers or standard among similar WA code cities.

---

## Executive Summary

**VERDICT: LAKEWOOD IS A MODERATE OUTLIER — WORSE ON SOME METRICS, AVERAGE ON OTHERS**

Lakewood is not unique among WA code cities in lacking an independent ethics enforcement body (4 of 5 peers also lack one). However, Lakewood IS an outlier on two critical dimensions:
1. **Recusal rate approaching zero over 16+ years** — no peer city had a perfect zero-recusal record over a comparable period.
2. **Dual-government employment prevalence** — 5 of 7 council members (71%) hold paid positions with another government or public agency — the highest of any city examined (peer range 0–29%; see `dual-government-recount.md`).

The most damning comparison is with Lynnwood (similar population, same region) — which BOTH has an independent ethics board AND has council members who recuse when conflicts arise.

## FINAL VERDICT: IS LAKEWOOD DIFFERENT?

### "Lakewood is broken" — STANDARD or OUTLIER?

**On recusal culture**: **MODERATE OUTLIER**. Lakewood's 1 recusal in 16+ years is extreme, but the pattern in peers is not dramatically better. Only Lacey has clearly codified a recusal-expectation culture. Richland even actively prevented a recusal. The larger problem is systemic across WA code cities — recusal is poorly understood and inconsistently applied.

**On dual-government employment**: **CLEAR OUTLIER**. At 71% (5 of 7), Lakewood has more than double the rate of any peer city with available data (Federal Way, 29%). No other peer city had more than 2 of 7 members working for another government. Recounted 2026-10-04 under a written rule; see `dual-government-recount.md`.

**On ethics enforcement**: **ENTIRELY TYPICAL**. 4 of 5 peer cities also lack an independent ethics enforcement body. Only Lynnwood (smaller population) has one. The absence of an ethics board is the standard, not an outlier, for WA code cities of this size. However, this standard itself represents a governance gap — most WA code cities lack independent ethics oversight.

### The Real Story

The data suggests a more nuanced picture than "Lakewood is uniquely broken":

1. **The dual-employment concentration IS exceptional** — 71% vs a 0–29% peer range (median 14%) demands explanation. Lakewood's council has an unusual concentration of members who simultaneously work for other governments, creating more conflict-of-interest surface area than any peer.

2. **The recusal culture IS weak — but not uniquely so** — peers also show minimal recusal activity. The WA code city model itself does not incentivize recusal.

3. **The ethics enforcement gap IS the norm, not the exception** — this is a WA-wide governance weakness.

4. **Lacey's positive example** shows that codified recusal procedures are achievable and low-cost — Lakewood's failure to adopt similar procedures is a policy choice, not an inevitability.

5. **The Richland parallel is instructive** — a councilmember who TRIED to recuse was told "abstentions are not allowed" by the mayor. This suggests the recusal problem in WA code cities may be cultural/leadership-driven, not merely individual.
