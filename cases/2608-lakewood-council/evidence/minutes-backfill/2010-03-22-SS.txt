IT EMS FOR DISCUSSION:

Joint meet ing wit h t he Human Services Funding Advisory Board.

Deputy Mayor Anderson called on Human Services Funding Advisory Board (HSFAB) Chair Mary Green who introduced members of the
Board.

HSFAB Chair Green reviewed the human services funding application process and HSFAB member Julie Washburn asked questions of the
Council relative to funding priorities, two-year funding cycle for human services, filing of applications by all agencies, the outcomes-based
evaluation process, and allocating unspent funds.

Discussion ensued on funding priorities of basic needs, health, education and safety for 2011; recommending agencies to attend
Collaboration meetings; recognizing domestic violence as a funding priority; agencies being accountable for service delivery and
performance; building objective criteria for economic efficiencies; moving toward a two-year human services funding cycle; two-year
funding potentially binding future Council funding priorities; Council formalizing the human services funding priorities by June before the
application is made available; whether all agencies funded with the one percent of the General Fund should go through the outcomesbased evaluation process; and develop a process for redistributing funds that may not get spent throughout the year.

Amended It was the consensus of the Council that the Council’s 2011 human services funding
4/5/10 priorities be provided to the HSFAB in writing prior to the distribution of the 2011 human services funding application.

BRIEFING BY T HE CIT Y MANAGER

City Manager Neiditz reported on the closure of Pacific Highway for bridge repairs.

City Manager Neiditz reported that an item on the Council’s April 5, 2010 agenda will be a contract with the Washington State Patrol
(WSP) which would allow a Lakewood detective to participate in the WSP program.

City Manager Neiditz reported that Governor Christine Gregoire is interested in signing a bill at the Lakewood Police Station on March 29,
2010, at 2:00 p.m.

City Manager Neiditz indicated that a proposed ordinance will be coming before the Council to revise the employee recognition program.

City Manager Neiditz then called on Lt. Steve Mauer about the Community Safety Resource Team (CSRT) program.

CSRT Updat e

Lt. Mauer provided an overview of the CSRT team and introduced Assistant City Attorney Mike Savage and Code Enforcement Officer
Doug Price, members of the team who were in the audience.

Discussion ensued on how “personal” contact and follow-up will be made between the requestor and the person outside the City’s
jurisdiction to whom the requestor must be referred to; what are the boundaries of each of the six districts; how effective and active are
the neighborhood watch groups; marketing the program through the website, utility brochures and City Connections newsletter; how is
the CSRT program being measured for success; and any concerns about confidentiality of the citizens who file complaints, such as drug
tips, etc.

**********

Speaking before the Council was:

Dennis Haugen, Lakewood resident, spoke about too much in-breeding in the CSRT approach, and that the City should seek speakers out
of State to bring new ideas to the CSRT program.
