## 5. South Sound 911 Funding in the 2025-2026 Budget

The 2026 Carry Forward Budget Adjustment packet (Adopt-2026-CFAdj-Complete-Packet.pdf) explicitly references South Sound 911:

> **PD – South Sound 911 Public Authority - Background Examinations, Continuation/Ongoing**
> Add $9,000 in expenditures funded by reimbursements, resulting in a revised budget of $49,000.
> The current contract runs from 01/01/2026 to 12/31/2026.
> The purpose of this contract is to assist South Sound 911's efforts to screen and evaluate employment and vendor applicants by performing the tasks assigned by South Sound 911.

**This is the $49,000 line item referenced in the budget PDF analysis.** The line sits in the Lakewood Police Department budget as a reimbursable contract: the City performs background examinations on behalf of South Sound 911 and is reimbursed. The carry-forward adds $9,000 in revenue and $9,000 in offsetting expenditure, revising the line to $49,000.

**Direction of payment (corrected 2026-10-04):** on this specific line, **South Sound 911 pays the City** — the 2025/2026 Adopted Budget lists "Police-South Sound 911 Background Investigations" among the city's contract-revenue projections (rising to $40,000/year), grouped with the city's other service contracts (Steilacoom, DuPont), and the city buys the background-check software that performs the work. Separately, the city does make payments to South Sound 911 (its communications allocation; the adopted budget shows a −$17,510 adjustment to that allocation). The verified finding is the relationship, not the direction: Lakewood and South Sound 911 hold interlocking contracts, and Lauricella — SS911's CTO — seconded and voted to adopt the budget containing them, with no disclosure or recusal.

> **CORRECTION (2026-10-04):** An earlier version of this file stated "The City of Lakewood pays South Sound 911 $49,000 (net) for these services" — that inverted the direction as to this line. Public copy must not claim the city "pays" or "funds" SS911 via the background-examinations line.

Additionally, the 2025-2026 Adopted Budget Detail shows budgeted expenses for:
- PD-Software For Background Checks: $1,200/year
- POBITS Background Software: $3,000/year
- General Police Professional Services contracts

## 7. Conflict of Interest Assessment

| Factor | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Lauricella's relationship to SS911** | CTO (Chief Technology Officer) — paid employee |
| **Budget he voted on** | 2025-2026 Biennial Budget (all amendments including Ord 851) |
| **SS911 contract line in budget** | **Confirmed** — $49,000 background-examinations contract line (reimbursable: SS911 pays the City; offsetting expenditure) |
| **Lauricella's specific vote (Ord 851)** | **Seconded the motion + voted aye (unanimous)** |
| **Recusal** | **None** — no disclosure, no abstention, no recusal |
| **Board membership** | Not a board member — he's **staff** at SS911, sits on council |

**Analysis:** Lauricella is a paid employee (CTO) of South Sound 911. He voted to adopt a budget that contains a $49,000 background-examinations contract line between the City and South Sound 911, his employer. RCW 42.23.030 prohibits a municipal officer from having a beneficial interest in any contract made by, through, or under the supervision of the officer's agency. Whether SS911's CTO has such a "beneficial interest" in a contract where his employer reimburses the city for a service the city performs is arguable in both directions — which is precisely why the disclosure question, not the payment direction, is the finding. No disclosure or recusal was made on either budget vote.
