# Right of Reply Framework — All 7 Council Members
**Purpose:** Legal and ethical requirement before publication. Must offer each member the opportunity to respond. Their response (or non-response) becomes part of the story.

---

## Legal Basis
Under Washington law and journalistic ethics, public officials facing allegations of misconduct must be given a meaningful opportunity to respond before publication. This is not just courtesy — it protects against defamation claims and strengthens the credibility of the story.

## Process

1. **Contact method:** Email to official city council email addresses (public record)
2. **Timing:** Minimum 72 hours before publication deadline
3. **Format:** Specific, citeable questions — not vague accusations
4. **Documentation:** Record all outreach attempts and responses verbatim

## Expected Responses

### Non-Response
A non-response within the timeframe should be noted in the story: "[Name] did not respond to requests for comment."

### Defensive Response
Most members will likely say:
- "I seek advice from the city attorney when I believe there's a conflict."
- "My outside employment does not affect my council votes."
- "I follow the law and the advice of counsel."

### Admission / Apology
Unlikely but possible. If a member admits to confusion about recusal requirements, that's a story in itself.

## Legal Protection
- Send questions in writing via email
- Keep copies of all correspondence
- If publishing allegations of RCW 42.23 violations, the right-of-reply record is the single best defense against a defamation claim
