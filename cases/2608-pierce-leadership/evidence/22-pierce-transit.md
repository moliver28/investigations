## 1. Overview

Pierce Transit is governed by a **10-member Board of Commissioners**: **nine voting members** (elected officials representing Pierce County, Tacoma, Lakewood, Puyallup, University Place, and the smaller cities/towns) plus **one non-voting union representative**. The board meets the second Monday of each month at 4 p.m. Clerk of the Board: Deanne Jacobson.

The board is a **HIGH-priority unmapped power structure** (per supplementary gap analysis, INV-2026-002). It controls the region's largest transit agency, its ~$345M annual budget, and the Nov 2026 ballot measure that would reshape how Pierce County grows.

## 4. Budget

- **2026 Adopted Budget: ~$345 million** in total expenditures (all components), balanced by revenues and reserves. Adopted by the board Dec 8, 2025.
- **Sales tax is the primary revenue source:** projected **$117M in 2026** ($116,794,690), **73.1%** of operating budget revenue (excluding Sound Transit), up 2.4% over 2025.
- **Reserves:** ~$175M in 2026, but largely committed to capital projects; projected to fall below minimum requirements by **2032**.
- **Fiscal cliff:** expenses expected to outpace revenue as early as **2027** due to inflation; without new funding, service reductions could begin as early as **2031**.
- **2026 budgeted positions:** 967 total (2025: 984).
- **Service area:** Public Transportation Benefit Area (PTBA) covers ~300 sq mi of Pierce County's urban area, ~70% of the county's ~941,000 residents, across 13 jurisdictions.

## 5. Nov 3, 2026 Ballot Measure (Proposition 1)

**Adopted July 13, 2026 via Resolution 2026-006** — placed on the Nov 3, 2026 general election ballot. Moves to the Pierce County Auditor.

**The ask:** Raise Pierce Transit's local sales tax from **0.6% to 0.9%** (+0.3%, = 3 cents per $10). First local transit funding increase since **2002**.

**What it funds (if approved):**
- **47% increase in transit service** across the service area
- **4 new bus routes** (Northeast Tacoma, Port of Tacoma, East Tacoma, Puyallup)
- **More frequent service:** routes with 15–20 min service jump from 2 to 7; 30-min service routes from 4 to 22; only 2 routes left hourly
- **Later evening/weekend service:** 23 routes extended to ~10pm weekdays (4 to midnight); Sunday service to 8pm
- **Fare-free bus & paratransit for seniors** (transit already free for youth)
- **Expanded paratransit** to Puyallup and Northeast Tacoma
- **New light rail connections** to Sound Transit stations at Tacoma Dome, Portland Avenue, Fife
- **50 new buses, 3 new paratransit vehicles, 125 new operators**; service phased in over 5 years (2027–2032)

**Why:** Pierce Transit has the **lowest local sales tax rate among comparable WA transit agencies** (0.6% vs Community Transit 1.2%, Intercity Transit 1.2%, King County Metro 0.9%, Spokane 0.8%). Inflation-driven costs outpace revenue from 2027.

**History:** Similar measures **failed in 2011** (~46% vote) and **2012** (lost by ~600 votes), forcing ~35% service cuts in 2011.

**Supporters:** Pierce County Central Labor Council, SEIU 775, Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber, Transportation Choices Coalition, WA Sierra Club; elected officials incl. Rep. Jake Fey (House Transportation chair), House Speaker Laurie Jinkins, **Pierce County Executive Ryan Mello**, Councilmembers Robyn Denson & Jani Hitchen, Tacoma Mayor Anders Ibsen. Campaign: KeepPierceMoving.org.

**Board quotes:** Chair Walker — "Reliable transit creates opportunity." Vice Chair Ayala — "The last time Pierce Transit received a sales tax increase was in 2002... Since then, Pierce County has grown 30%."

## 6. March 2026 Governance Restructuring — Terminated Committees

**Resolution No. 2026-004, adopted March 9, 2026** — permanently cancelled two standing board committees:

1. **Executive Finance Committee (EFC)** — permanently cancelled effective March 9, 2026. Previously 4 board members + 1 alternate; oversaw board governance, fiscal/administrative policy; had delegated authority to approve contracts up to **$1M** and property-use agreements. Members included Chair Kristina Walker and Ryan Mello.
2. **Service Delivery & Capital Committee (SDCC)** — permanently cancelled effective March 9, 2026. Previously 4 voting members + 1 non-voting CTAG member; oversaw operational service needs, customer experience, significant capital projects. Members included Chair John Hines and Rosie Ayala.

**Significance:** The board **consolidated decision-making into the full board**, eliminating the two committees that had delegated contract-approval and capital-project oversight authority. This concentrates fiscal and capital decisions in the full 10-member body — a governance centralization that matters for how the ~$345M budget and the ballot-measure expansion are controlled. The Community Transportation Advisory Group (CTAG, 9-member advisory body) remains.

## 7. How Transit Decisions Shape Pierce County Development

- **Transit-oriented growth is the county's planning framework.** Pierce Transit's Destination 2045 Long Range Plan and the county's comprehensive plan center growth around **walkable, transit-oriented communities** and "Centers and Corridors." Most new housing and jobs are targeted within the Urban Growth Area near high-capacity transit.
- **Board controls the service network that makes TOD viable.** The Nov 2026 measure's new routes (Northeast Tacoma, Port of Tacoma, East Tacoma, Puyallup) and frequency upgrades directly enable denser development in those corridors. The Urbanist notes a new Tacoma–Ruston route targets an area "primed for significant development" that currently lacks fixed-route bus service.
- **Light rail integration (2035).** Pierce County Executive Ryan Mello has vowed light rail will reach Tacoma Dome by 2035 "come hell or high water." The ballot measure's new light-rail connections (Tacoma Dome, Portland Avenue, Fife) position the county to capture development around Sound Transit stations.
- **Land-use coordination.** Pierce Transit coordinates with all 13 jurisdictions and unincorporated Pierce County; during 2024 comprehensive-plan updates it consulted on transit service in high-growth/infill areas. The 2026-2031 Transit Development Plan requires periodic assessment of county land-use goals and demographics to set the PTBA boundary.
- **Development leverage.** Transit access is a key determinant of where housing, jobs, and commercial development locate. The board's funding and routing decisions — now centralized after the March 2026 committee terminations — are a primary lever shaping Pierce County's growth pattern for the next decade.
