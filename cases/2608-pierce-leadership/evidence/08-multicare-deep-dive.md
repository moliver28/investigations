## 3. Political Giving

### MultiCare's Federal PAC Activity
MultiCare does not have its own standalone PAC. Instead:
- **HHFPAC (Hospitals for a Healthy Future PAC):** The Washington State Hospital Association's political action committee. MultiCare executives and employees contribute to HHFPAC, which supports pro-hospital candidates. HHFPAC had $1.12M+ in total contributions as of recent filings (per PDC C4 report 2024–2025).
- **OpenSecrets Data (2024 Cycle):** MultiCare Health System individuals contributed **$99,406** total:
  - 85.64% to candidates
  - 11.26% to party committees
  - 100% from individuals (not corporate treasury)
- **Federal Lobbying:** $120,000 (2024), $130,000 (2025)

## 5. Notable Controversies, CON Battles, Union Disputes, and Expansion

### A. $3.7M False Claims Act Settlement (Feb 2026)
- **Settlement:** MultiCare paid $3,728,000 to resolve federal and state allegations
- **Allegations:** Knowingly endangered patient safety and fraudulently billed Medicare/Medicaid for medically unnecessary spinal surgeries at Deaconess Hospital (Spokane) performed by neurosurgeon Dr. Jason Dreyer (2019–2021)
- **Key Facts:**
  - Dreyer had resigned from Providence amid allegations of unnecessary surgeries and patient harm
  - Two physician assistants raised concerns to management within two months of Dreyer operating
  - MultiCare allegedly tied Dreyer's compensation to procedure volume
  - $1.6M of settlement was restitution due to "egregious nature of violations"
- **Parties:** U.S. Attorney's Office (EDWA) + Washington State Attorney General Nick Brown
- **Quotation from AG Brown:** "The alleged violations by MultiCare show a wanton disregard for ethics and the medical principle to do no harm."
- **Sources:** DOJ, HealthExec, Fierce Healthcare, KREM 2

### B. "No Confidence" Votes (2023)
1. **Indigo Urgent Care Providers (June 2023):** UAPD-represented doctors, NPs, and PAs voted unanimous "No Confidence" in CEO Bill Robertson. Cited $2B in Wall Street securities while shortchanging patient care, 15-min patient limits, no breaks on 12-hr shifts. Authorized strike.
2. **Good Samaritan Hospital Nurses (May 2023):** WSNA nurses voted "No Confidence" after Robertson refused town hall invite and rejected nurse-submitted staffing plan. 15 bargaining sessions with federal mediator.
3. **Tacoma General Nurses:** WSNA nurses at Tacoma General and Mary Bridge NICU fought for staffing ratios. Held picket Jan 23, 2026. Strike advisory vote scheduled then canceled after tentative agreement.

### C. Certificate of Need (CON) Battles
1. **MultiCare v. Department of Health (2003):** MultiCare argued its new facility did not require CON because it operated under an existing license. Court ruled against MultiCare — CON required.
2. **Capital Medical Center Acquisition (2021):** MultiCare acquired Olympia's Capital Medical Center for $44.6M. Sought exemption from CON review, arguing "little change" to the hospital. WA Department of Health granted exemption — controversial given competitor concerns.
3. **CON Rehabilitation Bed Battle (2016):** MultiCare proposed adding 23 Level I rehab beds at Good Samaritan (Puyallup) vs. Franciscan's 60-bed rehab hospital (Tacoma). DOH approved Franciscan's project.
4. **Tacoma General Expansion (2025):** CON application CN26-02 — MultiCare requested 36 additional acute care beds at Tacoma General ($3.1M capital). Part of broader $54M expansion adding 65 beds and 4 ORs.

### D. Union Disputes
- **Ongoing WSNA Negotiations:** Tacoma General, Mary Bridge NICU, Good Samaritan nurses in continuous bargaining
- **UFCW 3000 Organizing:** Patient relations officers at Capital Medical Center organized and entered bargaining (June 2026). MultiCare accused of union-busting tactics.
- **MultiCare Tacoma Women's Clinic & Urogynecology:** UFCW 3000 bargaining — workers rejected proposed wage cuts, held informational pickets (July–Aug 2026)
- **SEIU Healthcare 1199NW:** MultiCare workers at Navos Behavioral Health delivered picket notices (2024)
- **MultiCare Allenmore Hospital:** UFCW 3000 bargaining for "common vision" contract (Aug 2026)

### E. Behavioral Health Controversies
- **Navos Mental Health Incidents (Seattle Times, 2019):** After MultiCare's 2017 acquisition of Navos, Seattle Times investigation revealed unreported patient safety incidents including escaped patient found unresponsive, nurse assaulted. MultiCare promised to file reports.
- **Greater Lakes Mental Healthcare:** Affiliated July 2020. MultiCare Behavioral Health now largest BH provider in Pierce County, reaching 20,000+ individuals annually.

### F. Major Expansion Decisions Affecting Pierce County
1. **Tacoma General $54M Expansion (2025–2026):** 65 new adult beds, 4 new ORs, 36 acute care beds via CON. Addresses Pierce Central planning area capacity.
2. **Allenmore ED Expansion ($15M, 2019):** +14,600 sq ft, +10,000 patients/year capacity
3. **Good Samaritan Hospital (Puyallup) Expansion:** 160-bed addition, total reaches 535 beds. Completion 2026.
4. **Mary Bridge Children's Hospital:** New freestanding pediatric campus in Tacoma (opened 2025/2026) — first dedicated pediatric hospital in South Sound after sharing space with Tacoma General for 30+ years.
5. **Neighborhood Emergency Departments:** Parkland and Bonney Lake NEDs opened to improve South Sound access.
6. **Overlake Affiliation (Oct 2024):** 349-bed Bellevue hospital joined MultiCare's North Sound region.
7. **Samaritan Health Services Affiliation (pending 2026):** 5 hospitals in Oregon — MultiCare's first Oregon expansion.

## Summary of Influence in Pierce County

MultiCare Health System is arguably the single most powerful healthcare institution in Pierce County, with:
- **Largest private employer** in Tacoma (~30,000 system-wide, significant Pierce County concentration)
- **Dominant market share** in Pierce County acute care (Tacoma General, Allenmore, Good Samaritan, Mary Bridge)
- **Chairman** (Wiborg) deeply embedded in Tacoma-Pierce County business establishment (Chamber, GTCF, UW Tacoma)
- **Government relations** team active in Olympia (Mungia) and via WSHA's HHFPAC
- **Rapid expansion** — Overlake (Bellevue), Samaritan (Oregon), and ongoing Tacoma General and Good Samaritan capacity expansions
- **Political giving** channeled through WSHA's HHFPAC, which spent heavily in WA state elections
- **Behavioral health dominance** — largest BH provider in Pierce County after Navos, Greater Lakes, Community Psychiatric Clinic acquisitions/affiliations
