## 1. Registered Lobbyists Listing Pierce County as a Client

### Pierce County Government as an Employer (Self-Reported Public Agency Lobbying)

Pierce County itself files quarterly L-5 reports with the PDC as a public agency that conducts state-level lobbying. The County does **not** appear as a "lobbyist employer" in the traditional sense (i.e., it doesn't pay a private firm to lobby for it — it contracts them separately — see Section 3 below). The PDC database shows "Pierce County" as an employer with zero reported compensation/expenses (the actual expenditures are captured in the L-5 quarterly reports).

**Pierce County's in-house lobbying employees (identified from L-5 filings):**
- **Jason Gauthier** — SSHA3P Manager (salary ~$138,598/yr, ~0.52% time on lobbying)
- **Mary Connolly** — Social Services Program Specialist (salary ~$93,805/yr, ~0.77% time on lobbying)

These county employees lobbied on capital budget priorities, affordable housing funding (Frederickson property), and resident-owned manufactured home community (ROC) infrastructure funding.

### Direct Contract Lobbyist for Pierce County (State-Level)

- **Michael Shaw** (Seattle, WA — PDC ID: 17957) — Contract state legislative lobbyist for Pierce County. Paid $9,200/month ($27,600/quarter in Q4 2024) for state legislative representation.
  - Sources: PDC L-5 Report #9845 (Q4 2024), AccessHub PDC registration
  - Phone: (206) 595-6108

## 3. Pierce County's Own Contracted Lobbyists

### State-Level Lobbyist

Pierce County contracts with **Michael Shaw**, an individual lobbyist based in Seattle, for state legislative representation. Per PDC L-5 filings (Q4 2024):

- **Rate**: $9,200/month ($27,600/quarter)
- **Services**: "Contract State Legislative Lobbyist for Pierce County"
- **PDC Registration**: AccessHub node #17957
- Pierce County issues RFPs for state legislative representation services (most recently RFP #2053 in 2018 — "State Legislative Representation Services" coordinated by the Director of Policy).

### Federal-Level Lobbyist

Pierce County uses a separate procurement process for federal relations consulting (RFP #2052 — "Federal Government Relations Consulting Services"). The current contractor was not publicly identified in the sources reviewed, but the RFP states the consultant "will act in liaison with and report activities to the Director of Policy."

The federal lobbying contractor may be **GrayRobinson, P.A.** (a national firm with federal lobbying practice) or potentially **Gordon Thomas Honeywell Governmental Affairs** (GTH also has a federal practice arm), though this was not confirmed from publicly available records.

### City of Tacoma's Federal Lobbyist

The City of Tacoma contracts with **Gordon Thomas Honeywell Government Affairs** for federal lobbying. Per OpenSecrets federal LD-2 filings (2025), City of Tacoma paid GTH $28,000 in one quarter for federal representation. This suggests GTH is a plausible candidate for Pierce County's federal contract as well.

## 4. Notable Lobbying Expenditures (Last 2 Years)

### Top 10 Pierce County Lobbying Expenditures (2024-2025 Combined)

1. **Puyallup Tribe of Indians** — **$1,910,333** (2024: $1,434,500 + 2025: $475,833)
   - By far the largest lobbying spender in Pierce County
   - Likely related to tribal gaming compacts, land-into-trust, and legislative priorities

2. **LeadingAge WA** (Tacoma) — **$312,317** (2024-2025)
   - Senior care/aging services lobbying
   - Represents senior living facilities across the state, many in Pierce County

3. **Assn of WA Spirits & Wine Distributors** (Tacoma) — **$258,449**
   - Alcohol distribution and retail regulation

4. **Washington Independent Physicians Practice Assn** (Tacoma) — **$266,238**
   - Physician practice advocacy, healthcare policy

5. **MultiCare Health System** (Tacoma) — **$210,956**
   - Major Tacoma-based healthcare system; lobbying on hospital funding, Medicaid, regulation

6. **City of Tacoma** (Tacoma) — **$191,061**
   - Municipal government lobbying for state funding and legislation

7. **WA State Assn of Plumbers & Pipefitters** (Tacoma) — **$174,597**
   - Construction trades union lobbying

8. **WA State Chiropractic Assn** (Tacoma) — **$190,100**
   - Healthcare scope-of-practice and insurance lobbying

9. **Waste Connections Inc.** (Fife) — **$194,077**
   - Waste/hauling company based in Fife, lobbying on environmental/regulatory issues

10. **Child Care Aware of WA** (Tacoma) — **$191,000**
    - Child care advocacy and funding

### Key Spending Observations

- **Healthcare dominates**: Healthcare entities (MultiCare, Virginia Mason Franciscan, LeadingAge, WA Physicians, Chiropractic, Nurse Anesthetists, Home Care Assn) collectively spent **$800K–$1M+** annually lobbying in Olympia
- **Tribal lobbying is massive**: Puyallup Tribe alone spent nearly **$1.91M** over two years
- **Real estate/development interests**: Southport Real Estate ($116,800 in 2024), Tacoma-Pierce County Realtors (~$44K/yr), Manufactured Housing Communities (~$44K/yr), and NUCA WA Chapter ($48K/yr) all maintain active lobbying programs
- **Pierce County itself** paid Michael Shaw $110,400+/yr for state lobbying through their contract
- **Regional Water Cooperative of Pierce County** spends ~$75-80K/yr lobbying on water rights/utility issues

### Puyallup Tribe's Dominant Spending

The Puyallup Tribe of Indians is the single largest lobbying spender with a Pierce County nexus. Key data points:
- 2024: $1,434,500 total expenses (likely includes large political/IE contributions)
- 2025: $475,833 total expenses
- 2023: $501,800
- 2022: $985,000
- 2020: $662,527

This level of spending places the Puyallup Tribe among the top lobbying employers in the entire state of Washington.
