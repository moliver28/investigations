## Executive Summary

This file maps the major philanthropic foundations operating in Pierce County, their leadership structures, grantmaking priorities, and interlocking board relationships with corporate and government power structures. Foundation and nonprofit boards serve as a significant but often invisible dimension of regional power — they channel millions in funding, shape policy agendas, and serve as a revolving door for corporate, government, and civic elites.

### Key Overlap Themes

## 8. CONCLUSION & INVESTIGATIVE LEADS

### Key Findings

1. **Nonprofit/philanthropic power in Pierce County is heavily concentrated** across a small network of interlocking boards, with GTCF, United Way, and Tacoma Art Museum forming a governance triangle.

2. **Government officials sit on grantmaking boards** — Ryan Mello (now County Executive) and Rosie Ayala (Councilmember) have held GTCF board seats while holding elected office, creating potential conflicts of interest between funding decisions and government policy.

3. **Corporate influence flows through foundation boards** — major banks (Umpqua, Bank of America, Columbia, Northwest), health insurers (Coordinated Care, Regence), and wealth management firms (Morgan Stanley, Mission Wealth) all have representation on foundation boards.

4. **Labor has a formal seat at the table** through Nathe Lawver's dual board roles, but labor representation is concentrated in building trades, not service/healthcare unions.

5. **The Russell Family Foundation is the primary environmental/climate funder** in Pierce County, operating independently from the GTCF-United Way social-service nexus.

6. **Trust-based wealth ($207M GTCF, $77M TRFF, Cheney lumber fortune, Russell Investments fortune, PACCAR fortune)** is being deployed through increasingly racial-equity and climate-focused grantmaking, while the board composition remains heavily weighted toward professional and corporate elites.
