## Dimension 1: Fire District Commissioners — The Unmapped Elected Layer

**What We Have:** Nothing. Zero fire district mapping exists in the investigation.

**What Exists:** Pierce County has **20+ fire protection districts**, each with **3-5 elected commissioners** serving 6-year terms, each with **independent property taxing authority**.

**Key Power Facts:**

| District | Budget | Coverage | Notes |
|----------|--------|----------|-------|
| **West Pierce Fire & Rescue** (Districts 3, 19, 26 merged) | **$75.5M** (2026) | 31 sq mi, 100K pop | 5 elected commissioners. Alan Billingsley (Pos 1), John Clancy (Chair, Pos 2), Dave Durr (Vice Chair, Pos 4), Scott Dellinger (Pos 5). Fire Benefit Charge approved by voters. |
| **Central Pierce Fire & Rescue** (Districts 6, 18, 21 merged in 2025-26) | Large (consolidation) | Graham, Orting, South Prairie | Formed April 2025 via merger of 3 legacy districts. Board of Fire Commissioners from the 3 legacy districts. |
| **East Pierce Fire & Rescue** (District 22) | — | Lake Tapps area | — |
| **Key Peninsula Fire** (District 16) | — | Key Peninsula | 3 commissioners |
| **South Pierce Fire & Rescue** (District 17) | — | Eatonville, Roy | 5 commissioners |
| **Pierce 23 Fire & Rescue** (Pierce-Lewis County) | — | Ashford/Elbe area | Cross-county district |

**Why It Matters:** Fire commissioners are **elected officials** who set property tax rates (Fire Benefit Charge), approve labor contracts (IAFF union), and control capital spending on fire stations and apparatus. The 2025-2026 wave of fire district consolidations (Central Pierce merging Graham, Orting, South Prairie) is a **massive restructuring of public safety governance** that happened with zero reporting.

**Key Intersections:**
- Fire commissioners overlap with other elected bodies (some serve on planning commissions, school boards)
- Fire union (IAFF) is a major political donor and player
- Fire district mergers create new governance structures with new power centers

**Priority: HIGH** — This is an entirely unelected-mapped layer of government with taxing authority.

**What We Have:** Partial. We mentioned Sound Transit in passing but never analyzed the board as a power structure.

**What Exists:** The Sound Transit Board has **18 members** (10 King County, 4 Pierce County, 3 Snohomish, 1 WSDOT Secretary). **Ryan Mello is Vice Chair.** Board controls **$54 billion+** in light rail, bus rapid transit, and commuter rail investments.

**Pierce County's 4 Board Seats:**
- **Ryan Mello** (Pierce County Executive) — Vice Chair of the full board
- **Kristina Walker** (Tacoma City Council)
- **Jim Low** (departing; being replaced by a mayoral appointment from Pierce County cities)
- **John "the Mayor of Fircrest"** — position representing small cities

**Why It Matters:** Sound Transit is building light rail to Tacoma (Hilltop extension, Federal Way extension). The Pierce County delegation votes on multi-billion-dollar capital programs. Mello's vice chair position gives him outsized influence over regional transportation spending that shapes Pierce County development patterns for decades.

**Power Channel:** A small number of Pierce County elected officials control regional transit funding that determines land use, economic development, and commute patterns.

**Priority: MEDIUM** — Important but mostly duplicates actors already mapped (Mello, Walker).

## Dimension 3: Pierce Transit Board — Local Transit Power

**What We Have:** Nothing. We didn't map Pierce Transit governance at all.

**What Exists:** Pierce Transit is governed by a **10-member Board of Commissioners** made up of elected officials from Pierce County, Tacoma, Lakewood, Puyallup, University Place, and smaller cities. One non-voting union representative also sits on the board.

**Known Board Members (partial):**
- **Ryan Mello** (Pierce County Executive)
- **Kristina Walker** (Tacoma City Council) — Chaired Executive Finance Committee
- **John Hines** (Tacoma City Council) — Chaired Service Delivery & Capital Committee
- **Rosie Ayala** (Pierce County Council)
- **Paul Bocchi** (Lakewood Mayor)
- Representatives from Puyallup, University Place, and smaller cities

**Power Facts:**
- Covers 292 sq mi, ~70% of county population
- Operating budget approx. **$100M+** (estimated from agency size)
- Just placed a **ballot measure on November 3, 2026** to expand service ("More People. More Places. More Often.")
- Controls fixed route buses, paratransit (SHUTTLE), and ride-share programs

**Key Dynamic:** The board recently **terminated** its Executive Finance Committee and Service Delivery & Capital Committee (March 2026) — centralizing power in the full board. This restructuring is itself a power move worth investigating.

**Priority: HIGH** — Transit board controls service that shapes economic opportunity for 70% of county residents. Recent restructuring and upcoming ballot measure make this timely.

## Dimension 6: Special Purpose Districts — The Hidden Taxing Authorities

**What We Have:** We mapped county boards/commissions but not **independent special purpose districts** that have elected boards and independent taxing authority.

**What Exists:** Special purpose districts are the most overlooked form of local government. They have their own elected commissioners, their own property tax authority, and their own budgets — outside the county budget entirely.

**Types in Pierce County:**

| District Type | Count | Power |
|---------------|-------|-------|
| **Fire Districts** | 15+ active | Property tax + Fire Benefit Charge. Elected commissioners. |
| **Water Districts** | 10+ active | Water rates, infrastructure bonds. Some elected, some appointed. |
| **Sewer Districts** | Multiple | Independent rate-setting. |
| **Metro Parks Tacoma** | 1 | Elected commissioners. $50M+ budget. Controls Tacoma's parks, Point Defiance Zoo, etc. (NOT under city council control — independent). |
| **Library Districts** | 2-3 | Pierce County Library System Board of Trustees. Independent taxing district. |
| **Cemetery Districts** | Multiple | — |
| **Flood Control Zone District** | 1 | 15-member board. County-wide. Oversees flood management. |
| **Lake Management Districts** | Multiple | Advisory boards that recommend property tax assessments. |
| **Tacoma Narrows Airport Advisory Commission** | 1 | Advisory to county (already mapped in evidence/10). |
| **Drainage Districts** | 10+ | Mapped in the county elected officials database but not analyzed. |

**Key Example — Metro Parks Tacoma:**
- **Independent municipal corporation** with elected commissioners
- Controls **Point Defiance Park, Zoo & Aquarium** — major cultural institution
- Controls all Tacoma parks, recreation programs, community centers
- Approves **bond measures** for capital projects
- Predecessor of **Ryan Mello's first elected office** (he defeated an incumbent Metro Parks commissioner in 2005)

**Priority: HIGH** — These are entirely unmapped elected bodies with independent taxing power. Metro Parks alone is a significant cultural and economic institution.

**Sources:**

## Dimension 7: Environmental Justice & Community Organizing — Counter-Power

**What We Have:** Nothing. No community organizing, environmental justice, or "counter-power" mapped.

**What Exists:** Commencement Bay is a **Superfund site** (designated 1983), one of the largest and most significant toxic waste cleanups in US history. Over **$600 million** has been spent on cleanup.

**Key Organizations:**
- **Communities for a Healthy Bay (CHB)** — The leading environmental justice organization in Pierce County. Monitors the Superfund cleanup, holds polluters accountable, advocates for environmental justice. Pushes for tighter industrial regulations on the Tideflats.
- **Tacoma Water** (Tacoma Public Utilities) — Tacoma owns its own water utility (unusual — most cities buy from regional suppliers). Tacoma Water serves 100K+ customers.
- **Center for Urban Waters** (UW Tacoma research center) — Scientists studying ongoing PCB/PBDE contamination in Commencement Bay. Finding that despite 40+ years of cleanup, toxic chemicals still entering the waterway.

**Why It Matters:** CHB and environmental justice organizations represent **counter-power** — they shape corporate behavior through litigation, advocacy, and public pressure. The Port of Tacoma, Weyerhaeuser, and other industrial actors must respond to them. Any complete power map must include those who check power.

**Port of Tacoma Environmental Remediation:** The Port has spent decades cleaning up its Tideflats properties. They've remediated 4 of 7 Commencement Bay waterways and are working on cleanup action plans for 10 upland properties. This creates a **dependency** — the Port needs environmental approvals to develop land, which gives environmental groups leverage.

**Priority: MEDIUM** — Important for understanding checks on industrial power, but less about "who holds power" than "who constrains it."

**Sources:**

- https://www.portoftacoma.com/environment/cleanup-remediation
- https://apps.ecology.wa.gov/cleanupsearch/site/3032

## Dimension 8: Water Utility Power — The Emerging County Water District

**What We Have:** Nothing.

**What Exists:** Pierce County is in the process of creating a **county-wide water utility** — a major structural change in how water is governed.

**Key Developments:**
- **September 2024:** Pierce County expanded its Water Utility (existing small operation)
- **2026:** County exploring creation of a **Water Utility District** to ensure safe drinking water county-wide
- **Kapowsin Water District** — currently in **Superior Court receivership** (since 2024/2025). County and WA Department of Health managing the failed district.
- **Tacoma Water** — Tacoma's municipally-owned water utility (TPU). One of the largest in the state. Serves Tacoma, University Place, and portions of Pierce and South King counties.
- **Numerous small water districts** (Valley Water District, Clear Lake Water District, etc.) with elected commissioners

**Why It Matters:** The creation of a county water utility would centralize water governance — a power shift from dozens of small districts to county control. This is a **live issue** (2026-27 budget includes exploring it) that will reshape water infrastructure governance.

**The Kapowsin Receivership:** Shows what happens when small special purpose districts fail. The county and state step in. This is a template for future water district consolidation.

**Priority: MEDIUM** — Emerging issue. Important for understanding future governance consolidation trends.
