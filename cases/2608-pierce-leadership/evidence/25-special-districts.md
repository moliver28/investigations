## Why This Matters

Special purpose districts are **independent elected taxing bodies entirely outside the county budget**. Each has its own elected commissioners, its own property tax / rate authority, and its own budget. They are the most overlooked form of local government in Pierce County — dozens of elected officials who set tax rates and control capital spending with almost no public scrutiny. This file maps the major ones: Metro Parks Tacoma, the library system, water/sewer districts, the Flood Control Zone District, and drainage districts.

## 1. Metro Parks Tacoma (Metropolitan Park District of Tacoma) — 🔴 HIGHEST PROFILE

**What it is:** An independent municipal corporation (Metropolitan Park District) governed by a **5-member Board of Park Commissioners elected by the people to 6-year terms**. NOT under Tacoma City Council control. The policy-determining body for the park district.

**Assets controlled:** Point Defiance Park, Point Defiance Zoo & Aquarium, Northwest Trek Wildlife Park, all Tacoma parks, recreation programs, community centers, golf courses, marinas.

**Budget:** **$212.8M total revenue/expenditures for 2025-2026** (up from $180.8M in 2023-24). Parks & Recreation division budget $78.7M (2023-24), increasing 35% (+$27.6M) in 2025-26. ~500 employees.

**Current financial stress:** A **$17M deficit** in the 2025-2026 budget forced two rounds of budget cuts totaling a **21% reduction**. Park Board held a public hearing May 18, 2026 on budget cuts. Board selected **Tony Piasecki as interim executive director** (2026).

**Bond authority:** Board approves bond measures to voters. In 2026 the Board decided to take a **$155 million bond package** to voters for park improvements/renovations.

### Zoo Trek Authority (ZTA)
- Established 2001 to disburse sales tax revenue to Point Defiance Zoo & Aquarium and Northwest Trek.
- Funded by a **one-tenth-of-a-cent county sales tax** approved by voters in 2000. Half goes to the two zoos; half divided among park systems county-wide.
- Composed of **7 elected officials appointed by city and county councils**.
- **Approves the biennial operating budgets** for the two zoos before final adoption by the Park Board.

### Power Dynamics
- **Ryan Mello's first elected office** was Metro Parks commissioner (elected 2005, defeating an incumbent). This is the launchpad of his political career.
- The Park Board is a **stepping-stone to higher office** — a low-profile elected seat with real budget authority.
- The **$155M bond** and the **$17M deficit** are live, high-stakes issues in 2026.
- Finance Committee (advisory only) includes Commissioner Bronwyn Clarke (Chair), Andrea Smith (Vice Chair), plus community members Tony Piasecki, Tania Wink, Dixie Mooney, Robert Rovai, Darren Campbell, Sarah Sutton, Laura Castrilli, Clifton Larson Allen, Beth Boggs.

## 2. Pierce County Library System — Board of Trustees

**What it is:** An **independent library taxing district**. Governed by a **5-member Board of Trustees** appointed by the **Pierce County Executive** (NOT elected). Trustees serve **5-year terms** and are volunteer citizens representing different regions of the service area.

**Scale:** Serves **680,000 people** with **19 libraries** — the **4th largest library system in Washington state**. Property taxes are the main funding source.

**Budget:** **2026 operating budget $51.09M**; **total all funds $56.47M** (2025: $54.3M). 2026 property tax levy ~$450K + ~$300K new construction; mill rate 33.2–33.7 cents per $1,000 assessed value. Washington's 1% property tax cap limits growth; in 2025 General Fund expenses exceeded revenues.

## 3. Water Districts

Water districts are **independent municipal utilities** with **3 elected commissioners** serving **6-year terms**, each with independent rate-setting and bond authority. Pierce County has 10+ active water districts. All rosters below from the county elected officials database (Aug 7, 2026).

### Valley Water District (Puyallup) — largest rural district
- **District Manager:** Sean Vance (appointed)
- **Commissioners:** Gary T. Hardiman (Pos 1, term 2029), Robert Fulton (Pos 2, term 2027), Christine Johnson (Pos 3, term 2031)
- **Profile:** Created 1993 via voter certificate; operates 6 non-contiguous service areas in unincorporated rural east Pierce County; ~7 employees; "a local government that works without taxes since 1993" (rate-funded, not tax-funded). No sewer service.
- **Note:** Valley Water District is the entity conducting PFAS sampling referenced in the Kapowsin receivership page — it appears to be the county's operational partner for the Kapowsin system.
- *Sources:* https://www.valleywaterdistrict.com/sh_event.php?ev_id=24 ; county database p.72

### Kapowsin Water District — IN RECEIVERSHIP 🔴
- **Commissioners:** Chris Craig (Pos 1, 2027), Tricia Hodge (Pos 2, 2027), **VACANT** (Pos 3)
- **Receivership:** Pierce County was appointed **receiver by Pierce County Superior Court on Nov 29, 2017** after a failed water transmission line forced trucking water to the district's **40 residential customers**. The district had run a deficit 2017-2018; a special assessment was charged per connection from June 2019.
- **Status (2026):** Receivership continues. DOH + Pierce County jointly presented a Plan for Disposition to end the receivership and return the system to the KWD Board of Commissioners. Next review hearing **Nov 20, 2026**. County exploring options for the system's future.
- **Power dynamic:** This is the template for **county consolidation of failing water districts** — the county is actively acquiring/operating struggling systems. The county's Water Utility page states it will acquire struggling systems through public County Council meetings.
- *Sources:* https://www.piercecountywa.gov/5513/Kapowsin-Water-District-Receivership ; https://www.piercecountywa.gov/1675/Water-Utility ; county database p.69

## 7. Cemetery Districts

**Legal framework (RCW 68.52.220):** Cemetery districts are managed by a **board of 3 elected commissioners** serving **6-year terms**. They may levy property taxes and one-year excess levies for operations/maintenance (60% voter approval required). Washington has ~103 cemetery districts statewide.

**Pierce County status:** **No cemetery districts appear in the Pierce County Elected Officials database** (Aug 7, 2026). Pierce County's cemeteries are largely operated by cities, private entities, or the county. This is a **gap to verify** — if any cemetery districts exist in Pierce County, their commissioners are not in the county's official elected-officials list.

## Summary of Power Dynamics

1. **Metro Parks Tacoma is the crown jewel** — a $212.8M independent elected body controlling the region's signature cultural institutions (Point Defiance Zoo, Northwest Trek). Its 2026 $17M deficit and $155M bond measure are live power issues. It was **Ryan Mello's political launchpad** (2005).

2. **The library board is a patronage channel** — appointed by the County Executive, not elected. Mello controls who sits on a $56M taxing body. The **Ryan Wheaton (Pierce Transit) and Pam Duncan (housing) interlocks** connect the library to transit and housing power.

3. **Water district consolidation is the emerging story** — Kapowsin's receivership (since 2017) is the template for the county absorbing failing districts. The county is actively acquiring struggling water systems. This is a **power shift from dozens of small elected boards to county control**.

4. **Flood Control is county-controlled** — unlike the independent districts, its governing body IS the County Council, with Mello on the advisory committee. 5 of 15 advisory seats vacant.

5. **Drainage districts are decaying** — multiple districts with all-vacant boards (No 23, No 24) signal governance collapse in this elected layer. The **Amy Cruver (councilmember) / Drainage District 13 clerk** name match is a potential interlock to verify.

6. **Cemetery districts: none found** in Pierce County's official elected-officials list — a gap to confirm.
