## Overview

This document maps organized criminal influence in Pierce County across six vectors: (1) historical public corruption — the 1978 "Enterprise" RICO case, (2) current gang-landscape — Knoccout Crips and Black Gangster Disciples federal indictments, (3) the JBLM drug/weapons pipeline, (4) FBI investigations of county officials and agencies, (5) judicial misconduct, and (6) Tacoma Police Department scandals and patterns of abuse. These vectors do not operate in isolation; cross-connections exist between them — for example, the 1978 Enterprise case centered on the same nightlife economy around Fort Lewis/McChord that fuels today's drug pipeline, and federal RICO tools used against Janovich in 1978 are still the primary mechanism for dismantling the gang networks active today.

## 1. Historical Public Corruption: The "Enterprise" RICO Case (1978)

### Summary

The most significant corruption scandal in Pierce County history. On December 8, 1978, a federal grand jury indicted 15 men — including sitting Pierce County Sheriff George V. Janovich — for engaging in a racketeering conspiracy under the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organization (RICO) Act. The organization, called "The Enterprise," was led by Tacoma mobster John Joseph Carbone and his son Joseph Carbone.

### Key Figures

| Name | Role | Outcome |
|------|------|---------|
| **George V. Janovich** | Pierce County Sheriff | Convicted of racketeering; sentenced to 12 years, served ~6. Recorded on tape accepting a $1,300 bribe. |
| **John Joseph Carbone** | Enterprise leader / Tacoma mob boss | Convicted; sentenced to 25 years. Died 1998. |
| **Joseph M. Carbone** | Lieutenant / tavern operations | Convicted |
| **Ronald J. Williams** | Chief lieutenant | Convicted |
| **Frank J. Mazzuca** | Lieutenant / nightclub operations | Convicted |
| **Anthony J. Mladnich** | Gambling operations | Convicted |
| **Richard F. Caliguri** | Chief enforcer | Convicted |

### Criminal Activities (1971–1978)

The Enterprise was charged with 107 overt acts including:
- **Assault, arson, extortion, bribery, and attempted murder** — to control Pierce County's topless-dancing tavern business
- **Insurance fraud** — members burned their own businesses for insurance payouts, including Carbone's own Gig Harbor home
- **Protection rackets** — protection, prostitution, and illegal gambling
- **Attempted murder of a state agent** — Washington State Liquor Control Board Agent Melvin R. Journey was shot four times outside his home on November 15, 1977, and survived after four hours of surgery
- **Firebombing of competing taverns** — 10+ arsons over six years causing ~$2 million in losses
- **Sheriff's office complicity** — Janovich disclosed information to the Enterprise, discouraged investigations, used deputies to harass competitors, and permitted prostitution/illegal gambling

### Investigation

- 13-month joint FBI/BATF undercover operation
- Break came when two half-brothers (Jackie Bentley and Michael Johnson) were arrested in Kansas City with a shotgun used in the Journey shooting
- Robert Valentine became a government informant, introduced undercover agents into the Enterprise
- Key evidence: tape recording of Janovich accepting a $1,300 bribe
- Trial held in San Francisco due to extensive pretrial publicity
- Six defendants pleaded guilty before trial; after a three-month trial, all but one were convicted

### Aftermath

- Janovich's conviction led directly to a **structural overhaul of Pierce County government**: voters replaced the three-commissioner system with a county council and county executive, and made the sheriff an appointed (rather than elected) position
- The Sheriff's Office has been "trying to live that down for 25 years" per Detective Ed Troyer (2005)
- Janovich died in 2005 at age 77 from a ruptured appendix; he had been moved between 30 prisons on protective custody
- The county later paid a $3.7 million civil settlement related to the racketeering case

## 2. Current Gang Landscape: Knoccout Crips & Black Gangster Disciples

### 2A. Knoccout Crips — FBI "Summer Heat" Operation (August 2025)

A 36-month FBI-led investigation culminating in August 2025 with the arrest of 15 individuals, including members of the Knoccout Crips street gang, for a large-scale fentanyl trafficking conspiracy.

**Key Facts:**
- **Operation "Summer Heat"** — multi-agency takedown with 100+ officers
- **Drug mule network** — female couriers transported fentanyl through Sea-Tac Airport via checked luggage, doused in perfume to mask odors; transported ~80 lbs of fentanyl pills/powder in just two months from Phoenix to Tacoma
- **Seized:** 2,683.3g fentanyl, 6,850.7g methamphetamine, 227.9g cocaine, 40.7g heroin, 27,593.8g marijuana, $111,524 cash, 23 firearms
- **Trigger:** Investigation initiated in response to a string of homicides and drive-by shootings attributed to the gang
- **Agencies:** FBI South Sound Safe Streets Gang Task Force, HSI, TPD, Lakewood PD, Pierce County Sheriff's Office, WA DOC

### 2B. Black Gangster Disciples — Wiretap Indictment (February 2026)

On February 4, 2026, law enforcement arrested 14 defendants affiliated with the Black Gangster Disciples street gang following a wiretap investigation, executing 17 search warrants across Washington, Oregon, and California.

**Key Facts:**
- **Lead defendant:** Kevin Salgado, 27, of Puyallup — alleged ringleader
- **Criminal methods:**
  - Pressing counterfeit oxycodone and Xanax pills for distribution
  - Hacking into drug distribution companies to reroute shipments
  - Paying kickbacks to an Oregon pharmacist for filling fake prescriptions
  - Trafficking firearms with "Glock switches" (auto-conversion to machine guns)
  - Mailing narcotics across the country via USPS
- **Connected to at least 3 overdose deaths**
- **Seized on arrest day alone:** 39 firearms, 100+ Glock switches, 887.9g fentanyl pills, 924g fentanyl powder, ~7kg counterfeit oxycodone pills
- **Total seizure over investigation duration:** 38+ firearms, 4kg meth, 10.5kg counterfeit oxycodone pills
- **Agencies:** HSI Seattle, DEA, ATF, USMS, USPIS, IRS-CI, USSS, CBP, USCG Investigative Service

### 2C. Gang Injunctions

No evidence of active Tacoma gang injunction "safety zones" comparable to Los Angeles or other major cities was found during research. Tacoma appears to rely on federal prosecution (RICO, wiretap investigations) rather than civil gang injunctions. This is a potential research gap — check if the city or county has ever sought civil gang injunctions.

## 3. JBLM Drug & Weapons Pipeline

Joint Base Lewis-McChord, one of the largest military bases in the country, presents unique criminal risk vectors: active-duty personnel trafficking classified information, stealing military-grade weapons, and drug distribution flowing between the base and surrounding communities.

### 3A. Espionage / National Security (2024–2025)

Two active-duty JBLM soldiers (Jian Zhao, Li Tian) and one former soldier (Ruoyu Duan) were arrested following federal grand jury indictments for conspiring to sell military secrets to buyers in China.

- **Charges:** Conspiracy to commit bribery, theft of government property, transmitting national defense information
- **Targeted data:** Technical manuals for Bradley and Stryker fighting vehicles, HIMARS information, encryption-capable computers
- **Duration:** November 2021 to December 2024
- **Status:** One former soldier pleaded guilty; cases ongoing

### 3B. Military Equipment Theft / White Supremacist Cell (June 2025)

Two veterans (Charles Fields and Levi Frakes) were charged with robbing JBLM's U.S. Army Ranger operations facility over a two-year period.

- **Key findings at their Lacey home:** 35 weapons including machine guns, silencers, night vision devices, flashbangs, smoke grenades, body armor, Nazi flags, SS Bolts paraphernalia, white supremacist literature
- **Method:** Used their military IDs to enter the base, used bolt cutters to breach the Ranger compound, assaulted an employee with a hammer
- **Stolen equipment:** Helmets, body armor, communications equipment (~$14,000 in a single incident)
- **Status:** Federal charges pending; held on $500,000 bail each

### 3C. Drug Distribution Tied to JBLM

The 1978 Enterprise case itself centered on nightlife around Fort Lewis and McChord AFB. The pattern continues:

- The Knoccout Crips and BGD indictments above show drug distribution networks operating in the same geography as JBLM (Tacoma, Lakewood, Puyallup)
- Spanaway man Sean Michael Moinette sentenced to 12 years (2025) for fentanyl/meth distribution
- Pierce County-based drug trafficking organization indicted (December 2023) by DEA — the press release explicitly notes harm to "young people in our community"

## 4. FBI Investigations of County Officials and Agencies

### 4A. Pierce County Housing Authority — $7 Million Embezzlement (2019)

The largest fraud case on record against a Washington local government. The State Auditor's Office uncovered that former finance director **Cova Campbell** misappropriated **$6,948,277** from Pierce County Housing Authority funds since 2016.

- **Schemes:** Wire transfers to shell accounts, fabricated invoices, unauthorized payments
- **Referral:** Case forwarded to FBI and HUD OIG for criminal investigation
- **Outcome:** Campbell charged; the DOJ website lists a sentencing for "former low-income housing executive" for embezzlement of nearly $7 million
- **Significance:** Demonstrates vulnerability in county-level financial oversight — the embezzlement was detected during a routine financial audit, not through internal controls

### 4B. Sheriff Ed Troyer — Independent Investigation (2021–2022)

Pierce County Sheriff Ed Troyer was the subject of a confrontation with a Black newspaper carrier (Sedrick Altheimer) in January 2021 that led to state charges for false reporting.

- **Investigation:** Independent investigation commissioned by Pierce County found Troyer "seriously lacking" in professionalism
- **Criminal trial:** A Pierce County jury found Troyer **not guilty** of false reporting charges
- **Legal costs:** Taxpayers initially covered Troyer's legal costs; later determined they would not pay
- **Significance:** Created a crisis of confidence in the Sheriff's Office, particularly around racial equity

### 4C. 911 Tax Revenue Diversion — State Audit Finding

- Pierce County was found by the Washington State Auditor to have violated state law by diverting millions in tax revenue meant for 911 emergency services
- Noncompliance presented to County Council, Executive Office, and department leadership in May 2024
- InvestigateWest reported that the county's purchase of $4.5 million in law enforcement radio equipment jeopardized federal funding
- **FBI referral status:** Unclear from available sources — further investigation needed

### 4D. Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney's Office History

- Former Prosecuting Attorney Ron Hendry (1967–1974) charged county Licensing Director Jerry Aldridge with bribery, revealing a pattern of payoffs for protection going back decades
- The same office prosecuted the Carbone/Janovich racketeering enterprise
- No evidence of current FBI investigation specifically targeting the Prosecutor's Office was found

## 5. Judicial Misconduct

### 5A. Judge Grant L. Anderson — Pierce County Superior Court (1998–1999)

**Violation:** Received undisclosed payments from a purchaser of assets from an estate for which he was a fiduciary; failed to report payments on public disclosure filings; served as a corporate officer after becoming a judge.

- **Findings:** Violated Canons 1, 2(A), 5(C)(3), and 6(C) of the Code of Judicial Conduct
- **Commission recommendation:** Censure and 4-month suspension without pay
- **WA Supreme Court action:** **Removed from office** — unanimous decision overturning the Commission's more lenient recommendation
- **Discipline date:** April 3, 1998
- **Full removal:** July 29, 1999 (Seattle Times: "Pierce County Judge Ousted By High Court — Dishonesty Cited")

### 5B. Judge Mark S. Deming — Pierce County District Court No. 1 (1987)

The first public hearing in Washington Commission on Judicial Conduct history.

**Violations:**
- Improper romantic relationship with a probation department employee that adversely impacted court administration
- Frequent inappropriate and injudicious comments and sexual innuendos to women in public and in court
- Sexual harassment of personnel from District Court, Probation, Prosecuting Attorney's Office, and Department of Assigned Counsel (DAC)

- **Findings:** Violated Canons 1, 2(A), 2(B), and 3(A)(3)
- **Supreme Court action:** **Removed from office** (108 Wash. 2d 82, 736 P.2d 639)
- **Date:** May 7, 1987

### 5C. Judge A. Eugene Hammermaster — Sumner/Orting/Wilkeson/South Prairie Municipal Courts (Pierce County)

**Violations:**
- Exhibited patterns of failure to respect and comply with the law
- Disrespectful and undignified conduct
- Violations of fundamental procedural rights of criminal defendants

- **Commission action:** Found violations of Canons 2(A), 3(A)(1), and 3(A)(3)
- **Date:** Not precisely specified in available records

### 5D. Recent Court Administration Issues

In August 2026, The News Tribune reported that "Investigations preceded Pierce County court leader resignation" with the headline: **"Failed to act."** — suggesting ongoing issues in court leadership and oversight that led to a resignation.

## 6. Tacoma Police Department Scandals

### 6A. The Death of Manuel "Manny" Ellis (March 3, 2020)

The defining Tacoma PD scandal of the modern era.

- **Incident:** Ellis, a 33-year-old Black man, died of asphyxiation while being restrained by Tacoma police. Officers beat, Tasered, and hogtied him.
- **Medical Examiner:** Ruled a homicide — death caused by lack of oxygen during physical restraint
- **Initial investigation:** Pierce County Sheriff's Department conducted the initial probe; a Pierce County deputy was present during Ellis's arrest
- **State criminal trial:** Officers Christopher Burbank and Matthew Collins charged with second-degree murder; Timothy Rankine charged with first-degree manslaughter. **All three acquitted** by a jury in December 2023
- **Federal review:** DOJ opened an independent civil rights investigation in January 2024; **closed** in 2025 due to insufficient evidence for federal charges
- **City payments:**
  - Each officer paid $500,000 to resign "in good standing" (January 2024)
  - $4 million wrongful death settlement paid by Pierce County (2022)
  - $6 million settlement paid by City of Tacoma (July 2025)
- **Aftermath:** DOJ ended its federal civil rights review without charges; state officials are investigating whether to revoke the officers' certifications; one officer (Rankine) filed a $47 million defamation claim against officials

### 6B. Excessive Force / Racial Bias Lawsuit Settlement (April 2024)

- **Plaintiff:** Zimmeri Contreraz, a Black man singled out at Wright Park in July 2020
- **Allegation:** Officer Christopher Bain yanked him backward by his handcuffs, slamming his head onto a picnic table — while Contreraz was seated and compliant
- **Settlement:** $300,000 from City of Tacoma; additional $50,000 from Pierce County 911 for public records violations
- **Status:** Officer Bain remained on the force as of April 2024

### 6C. Lawsuit Alleging TPD Forced Out Black Leaders

- The News Tribune reported a lawsuit claiming Tacoma PD has a "pattern of harassing, forcing out Black cops"
- A former deputy police chief sued the city alleging misconduct

### 6D. No DOJ Consent Decree (Notable Finding)

Unlike Seattle (2012–2024 consent decree) or other Washington cities, **Tacoma has never been under a federal consent decree** for pattern-or-practice policing violations. The DOJ did open a civil rights review of the Manny Ellis case but closed it without a finding. This is notable because:

- Tacoma PD has faced multiple excessive force lawsuits but has not triggered a structural DOJ intervention
- The current DOJ under the Trump administration has been actively *ending* consent decrees (Louisville, Minneapolis, May 2025), making a new one unlikely
- **However:** A Disparate Policing & Charging analysis (waw.fd.org) references a "Department of Justice investigation that found 'a pattern or practice of constitutional violations. Tacoma Police officers'" — this finding needs verification against the primary DOJ source

## Cross-Dimension Analysis

### Connections Between Vectors

1. **1978 → 2026:** The Janovich Enterprise case and today's gang indictments both involve protection and drug economies centered on the JBLM nightlife corridor. The geographic and economic pattern is persistent across 50 years.

2. **JBLM ↔ Street Gangs:** The same base that generated the 1970s Enterprise tavern economy now generates stolen military-grade weapons flowing into Tacoma gangs. The white supremacist equipment-theft ring (Fields/Frakes, 2025) demonstrates an additional national security risk.

3. **Lack of Consent Decree:** Unlike Seattle, Tacoma PD has never been placed under a DOJ consent decree despite multiple high-profile excessive force cases. This may reflect either better policing, different political dynamics, or timing (the post-2024 DOJ has been terminating decrees, not initiating them).

4. **Judicial Accountability:** Two Pierce County judges have been removed from office by the WA Supreme Court (Deming 1987, Anderson 1999), suggesting systemic oversight issues in the county's judicial selection and monitoring. The 2026 court-leader resignation under cloud of "failed to act" may signal ongoing problems.

### Research Gaps (Further Investigation Needed)

- [ ] **Active FBI investigations:** No public confirmation of any ongoing FBI probe into Pierce County executive or council members. The 2019 Housing Authority case is the last confirmed FBI referral. Freedom of Information requests may reveal more.
- [ ] **Gang injunctions:** No evidence of Tacoma or Pierce County using civil gang injunctions. This contrasts with other WA cities. Confirm this gap through city legal department records.
- [ ] **JBLM drug statistics:** Specific data on active-duty personnel arrested for drug trafficking (as opposed to espionage/theft) is limited. The Army's ASAP (Substance Abuse Program) data and CID reports could provide more.
- [ ] **911 fund diversion — FBI referral status:** The InvestigateWest story confirms a state audit finding but does not confirm whether the FBI has opened a probe.
- [ ] **DOJ pattern-or-practice finding:** The waw.fd.org reference needs verification against the original DOJ document.
- [ ] **White prison gang ties:** The "Key Pierce County drug distributor tied to white prison gangs" case (DOJ press release) suggests connections between street gangs and prison-based organized crime that merit further investigation.
