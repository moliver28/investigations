## 0. Why this person or group is on the map

## 1. Who they are
- **Full name:** Robert Watson Ferguson (source 1, 2).

- **Born:** February 23, 1965, in Washington State. He is a fourth-generation Washingtonian (source 1, 2, 3).
- **Wife and kids:** Colleen Ferguson, a teacher. Twins Jack and Katie. They live in the Governor's Mansion in Olympia (source 1, 3).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **The office.** Ferguson is the 24th Governor of Washington. He took office on January 13, 2025. His term ends January 8, 2029. As governor, he runs state agencies, writes the budget plan, picks judges, signs laws, and sets the legislative agenda (source 1, 2, 3, 8).
- **The budget.** He proposed a 2026 supplemental budget with $4 billion in cuts and $1.3 billion in new programs. His priorities are housing, public safety, ferries, affordability, and education (source 10, 11). The 2025–27 two-year state budget added $9 billion or more in new or higher taxes to close a shortfall (source 11).
- **Judicial appointments.** He picks the judges for every state court seat. That includes the Washington Supreme Court, the Court of Appeals, and every Superior Court (source 8). He has chosen two WA Supreme Court justices (Colleen Melody and Theo Angelis) and several Superior Court judges, including Doris Walkins in Pierce County (source 6, 8, 9). He can also strike individual budget items with a line-item veto.
- **Executive orders.** He signs executive orders to direct state policy. Recent ones include EO 26-02 (the Governor's Economic Development Council, June 25, 2026), EO 26-01 (workplace rules for menopause, June 1, 2026), a license plate reader law (SB 6002, March 2026), wildfire emergency orders (August 2026), and a Medicaid executive order (September 2026) (source 13, 14, 15, 16).
- **Where his power reaches:** All 39 counties in Washington, including Pierce County.

## 5. How they touch government
- **Campaign money.** His 2024 governor's campaign raised a lot. He beat Reichert, who ran a $1.7 million+ operation (source 4). Full filings from the state campaign-finance agency (PDC) were not pulled in this pass.
- **Pierce County vote.** He won Pierce County 220,153 (50.79%) to Reichert's 212,218 (48.96%) in November 2024. About 443,447 ballots were cast, with 76.73% turnout (source 5).
- **Judicial picks.** He picked Doris Walkins for Pierce County Superior Court on February 25, 2026. She filled the seat left open by Judge Karena Kirkendoll's retirement in Department 17 (source 6, 7). He picked Colleen Melody for the Washington Supreme Court on November 24, 2025 (replacing Justice Mary Yu) and Theo Angelis on March 9, 2026 (replacing Justice Barbara Madsen) (source 8, 9).
- **Budget.** He proposed the 2025–27 state budget (signed May 2025) and the 2026 supplemental budget (proposed December 23, 2025). The plan includes $4 billion in cuts, $1.3 billion in new programs, and $9 billion+ in new taxes. It shapes Pierce County funding for housing, public safety, and schools (source 10, 11).
- **Orders that hit Pierce County.** His Economic Development Council order (EO 26-02) brought statewide planning. SB 6002 (the license plate reader law) led the Pierce County Sheriff to shut down Flock cameras. He also signed wildfire emergency orders (August 2026) and a Medicaid executive order (September 2026) (source 13, 14, 16).
- **PDC appointment.** He appointed Matt Segal of Pacifica Law Group to the Washington State Public Disclosure Commission (PDC) (source 17).

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** Bob Ferguson holds the most powerful single office in Washington State. As governor, he picks judges — including for Pierce County courts. He writes the state's budget plan. He signs executive orders. He sets the agenda for his party. He won the 2024 race statewide and narrowly won Pierce County too. That narrow win matters: it shows Pierce County is a swing area where his local actions carry political weight.
- **Conflicts to watch.** Picking Colleen Melody for the Supreme Court looks like cronyism to critics — she ran the civil rights division at his old AG office. His Economic Development Council puts Amazon, Microsoft, and Boeing at the table with labor and tribal reps, which raises policy-capture concerns. His $9 billion+ tax package while pushing "competitiveness" reforms sends mixed signals. No personal financial conflicts were found.
- **The counter-argument.** Ferguson's Pierce County power has limits. He won the county by only about 8,000 votes. His judicial picks face later elections and can be voted out. The Economic Development Council only advises — it does not decide. The legislature can change his budget plan. SB 6002 was a bill he signed, not an order he gave on his own.
