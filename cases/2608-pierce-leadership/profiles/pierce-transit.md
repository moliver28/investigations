## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory authority:** RCW 36.57A lets PTBAs levy sales tax (with voter approval), run transit service, contract with other agencies, and own and operate property. Pierce Transit runs fixed-route bus, dial-a-ride (SHUTTLE paratransit), vanpool, and rideshare services.
- **Taxing authority:** Pierce Transit currently levies a 0.6 percent local sales tax within the PTBA. The last increase, from 0.3 to 0.6 percent, was approved by voters in 2002. A November 3, 2026 ballot measure (Resolution 2026-006) would raise the rate to 0.9 percent. That is a 0.3 percentage-point increase (3 cents on a $10 purchase). If approved, it would fund a 47 percent boost in service and bring in about $64 million a year.
- **Market share:** Pierce Transit is the main (near-monopoly) public transit provider in Pierce County. It runs 29 routes and 174 buses. Weekday ridership averages about 29,700 (Q2 2026). Annual ridership was 7,833,600 in 2025.
- **Regulatory capture:** Limited. Pierce Transit must follow FTA rules and WSDOT oversight. It needs voter approval for tax increases.
- **Domain overlaps:** Government (transit authority and taxing power), economic (transportation infrastructure that supports commerce), and political (board members are elected officials who campaign on transit positions).
