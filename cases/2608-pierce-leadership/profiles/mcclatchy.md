## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** McClatchy controls Pierce County's only major daily paper (The News Tribune) under Chatham Asset Management ownership. Chatham is a hedge fund with a track record of aggressive cost cuts across its newspaper portfolio. McClatchy's power rests on two bases. First, it has a monopoly on the daily newspaper of record. Second, it is run by an absentee owner that extracts money through cuts. Decisions about Pierce County's news coverage are made at the corporate level by a hedge fund in Chatham Borough, NJ. Local accountability is limited. The 2024 layoff rounds and about a 41% revenue decline since Chatham's 2020 acquisition show that McClatchy's Pierce County newsroom has shrunk under hedge-fund ownership.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** Chatham's control of McClatchy is the central conflict. A hedge fund's duty to its investors is structurally different from a community newspaper's duty to its readers. Chatham's Postmedia Canada portfolio shows the pattern: 1,600 jobs cut, 30 papers shut down. At the Miami Herald, more than one-third of editorial staff were cut in 2024. Critics read this as a deliberate cost-extraction strategy. The defense counter: McClatchy emerged from Chapter 11 in 2020 under Chatham's ownership. Without Chatham, McClatchy might have liquidated and all 30 papers would have closed. Chatham's investment preserved jobs that would otherwise have been lost. McClatchy's press release frames Chatham as "stewards." Critics reject that claim.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** The opposing reading is that McClatchy's power is overstated. The News Tribune competes with digital-native outlets. Tacoma Weekly, Patch, South Sound Talk, social media, and direct government communications all compete for readers' attention. Under that view, McClatchy's gatekeeping power has eroded. However, for accountability journalism — investigations of Pierce County government, courts, and major institutions — The News Tribune remains the dominant local outlet. Its smaller newsroom directly weakens democratic accountability in the county. The structural reality is that McClatchy, under Chatham, holds significant power to set the agenda despite digital competition.
