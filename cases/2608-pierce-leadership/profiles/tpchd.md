## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why TPCHD is on the map:** TPCHD is the sole local public health agency for Pierce County (about 952,600 people as of 2024). State law (RCW 70.05) gives it power to protect health. It does this through disease prevention, food safety, septic permits, and family health services. Its $102.9M two-year budget and about 284 full-time staff (FTE) give it real muscle. The Board of Health — made up of Pierce County Council members, Tacoma City Council members, the County Executive, and others — sets policy for the whole county.
- **Why others near it are NOT on the map:** The Washington State Department of Health is a separate state-level agency. Franciscan Health System / Virginia Mason Franciscan Health (a hospital system) is mapped separately. The Perinatal Collaborative of Pierce County (a TPCHD-affiliated 501c3) is a sub-entity. The Pierce County Medical Society (which appoints one Board of Health member) is mapped separately.

## 1. Who they are
- **Legal name:** Tacoma-Pierce County Health Department (TPCHD)
- **Type:** Independent government agency (local health jurisdiction)
- **Founded:** 1972 (per Wikipedia). Created by an interlocal agreement between Pierce County and the City of Tacoma.
- **Governing law:** Revised Code of Washington (RCW) 70.05 (local health departments); RCW 70.05.070 (powers and duties of local health officers); RCW 70.28.031; interlocal agreement between Pierce County and the City of Tacoma.
- **Tax ID (EIN):** Government entity. Pierce County acts as the fiduciary agent.
- **Parent/subsidiaries:** No parent. Run by the Tacoma-Pierce County Board of Health, an independent policy body. Pierce County is the fiduciary agent.
- **Accreditation:** One of about 400 local health departments in the U.S. that meet Public Health Accreditation Board (PHAB) standards.
- **Headquarters:** 3629 South D Street, Tacoma, WA 98418-6813. Also has a Key Peninsula office at 9013 Key Peninsula Hwy N, Lakewood, WA 98349 (open Thursdays 11am-2pm).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory authority:** RCW 70.05 gives TPCHD power over food safety (restaurant inspections, food worker cards), drinking water safety, septic system permits, disease control (TB, HIV/AIDS, whooping cough), environmental health, and vital records (birth and death certificates). The Health Officer (Dr. James S. Miller) has powers under RCW 70.05.070 and RCW 70.28.031.
- **Budget:** 2026-2027 two-year budget: $102,899,820 (later amended to about $108.7M per the April 2026 Board agenda). That is 3.2% of the total Pierce County budget. Funding sources: Federal 9.1%, State 30.4%, Pierce County 12.8%, City of Tacoma 2.5%, Permits & fees 39%, Private/local/misc 6.2%. The budget fell 19% from the 2024-25 amended budget of $127.6M. The drop came from the end of federal COVID money.
- **Regulatory authority:** TPCHD enforces food safety codes, septic rules (including a new do-it-yourself septic inspection program), environmental health codes, and disease control rules. It inspects restaurants, issues food worker cards, and regulates on-site sewage systems.
- **Area covered:** All of Pierce County (about 952,600 people in 2024). This includes cities like Tacoma, Puyallup, Lakewood, Gig Harbor, and University Place, plus unincorporated areas.
- **Staffing:** 284 FTE in 2026. Down from 349 in 2025 and 355 in 2024. The drop reflects post-COVID budget cuts. Four divisions: Disease Prevention & Management, Environmental Health, Strengthening Families, Administrative Services.
- **Domain overlap:** Government (health rules, statutory authority), Social (public health services, family support, substance abuse prevention), Economic (food safety permits, septic permits that affect property values).

## 5. How they touch government
- **Board of Health:** TPCHD's governing body. Three members appointed by Pierce County Council. One by Tacoma City Council. Plus the Pierce County Executive and Tacoma Mayor (or their picks). One from Pierce County Cities and Towns Association. One from Pierce County Medical Society. Two-year terms.
- **Budget:** Pierce County Council appropriates the county share ($7,546,490 from the General Fund for 2026-27). The Board of Health recommended the 2026-27 budget via Resolution 2023-4795 (July 2, 2025).
- **Ryan Mello link:** Pierce County Executive Ryan Mello sits on the Board of Health. He has appeared with Director Reed on public health events (like a youth violence prevention funding event with Tacoma Police Chief Patti Jackson and the Billy Ray Shirley III Foundation).
- **Federal money:** 9.1% of the budget (about $9.4M) is federal. Reed told the Board that a $1M Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) grant (3-year, awarded 2023 for wildfire smoke health effects) was ended. That is an early sign of federal funding changes.
- **State money:** 30.4% of the budget (about $31.3M) is state. This includes Foundational Public Health Services revenue ($8.7M per year, down $374K in SFY 2026).
- **Regulatory work:** TPCHD regulates food businesses (permits, inspections), septic systems (permits, inspections, the new DIY program), drinking water, and enforces environmental health codes. It also runs the food worker card program.
- **Campaign donations:** None. As a government entity, TPCHD does not donate. The department may push for public health money through the Board of Health and professional groups.

## 1. Who they are
- **Director Reed probe details:** The Board of Health did not release the independent investigator's full report. News Tribune reporter Becca Most asked for the documents. The full findings, the scope of complaints, and any ongoing fixes remain partly UNVERIFIED. This is a real accountability gap.
- **Budget amendment path:** The 2026-27 budget started at $102.9M (a 19% cut). Then in August 2026 it was amended up to about $108.7M. What drove the $5.8M bump and whether more amendments are coming needs watching.
- **Federal money impacts:** Reed told the Board that a $1M EPA grant was ended. The full scope of federal losses (beyond COVID-era cuts and the EPA grant) and what they mean for specific programs needs more digging.
- **Staff reduction context:** FTE fell from 355 (2024) to 284 (2026) — a 20% drop. The link between this drop, the budget cuts, and the workplace culture probe is not clear. Whether services are still being delivered well needs checking.
- **PCPC governance:** The Perinatal Collaborative of Pierce County (501c3) has TPCHD staff on its board alongside hospital reps. The financial and working ties between TPCHD and PCPC need more clarity.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** TPCHD controls public health rules for all of Pierce County (about 952K residents). It does this through state law (RCW 70.05), a $102.9M+ two-year budget, and about 284 staff. Its power runs through the Board of Health (which includes the County Executive, three County Council members, and Tacoma City Council members) and through the Director of Public Health. The department's reach covers food safety, septic systems, drinking water, disease control, and environmental health. It touches daily life in ways most people do not notice. The 19% budget cut and 20% staff drop since 2024 come from the end of COVID money and have cut capacity.
- **Conflicts to watch (defense counter-argument expected):** The Board of Health's refusal to release the full probe report on Director Reed's workplace conduct raises an accountability concern. A defense attorney could say the Board protected staff privacy and that coaching was a fair fix. The counter: the lack of detail hurts public trust, especially while staff cuts continue.
- **The other side:** TPCHD is a low-power agency tied to its funders. Only 12.8% of its money comes from the county. Most comes from state and federal pass-throughs. Board members are part-time appointees with other day jobs. Its rule-making power is bounded by state law and its budget. Budget cuts may shrink TPCHD's real power, even though the law still gives it authority.
