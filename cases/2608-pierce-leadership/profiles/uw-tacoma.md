- **Official name:** University of Washington Tacoma (source #1). It is accredited by the Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities as a unit of the University of Washington (source #1).
- **Type:** Public university campus. One of three campuses of the University of Washington (source #1). It is not a separate 501(c)(3) nonprofit or LLC. It is a unit of the University of Washington, a state public institution. The UW Board of Regents governs it.
- **Tax ID (EIN):** NOT CONFIRMED. As a unit of the UW, UW Tacoma does not have its own EIN. The University of Washington's EIN applies. The UW Foundation, the fundraising arm, has its own 501(c)(3) status.
- **Parent and related units:** Parent: University of Washington (founded 1861, source #1). UW Tacoma is one of three campuses. They are Seattle (Robert J. Jones, President), Tacoma (Sheila Edwards Lange, Chancellor), and Bothell (Kristin G. Esterberg, Chancellor) (source #1). UW Tacoma has no subsidiaries.
- **Ownership chain:** State of Washington → University of Washington (public institution) → UW Tacoma (campus unit). The UW Board of Regents governs. The Chancellor reports to the UW President and Provost (source #4).
- **Governing law:** Washington State higher education law, found in state law (RCW) 28B. The UW Board of Regents governs. Executive Order 20 of the UW Policy Directory sets the rules for chancellor searches (source #4).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Power held:** UW Tacoma has **institutional and cultural power**. As a public university campus, it shapes who goes to college, who gets trained for jobs, and how the community sees itself in Pierce County. It does not have legal authority over county government. But as an anchor institution in downtown Tacoma, it has real power over land use, the economy, and community development. The campus has been called "a catalyst for the revival of downtown Tacoma" (source #4). Chancellor Lange also lobbies the state legislature in Olympia (source #5). UW Tacoma also has **economic power** as an employer and a buyer. The specific employee count and payroll were NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Power-domain overlap:**
 - **Tax status:** Public university, a state entity. Not a 501(c)(3). It is tax-exempt as a state institution. Gifts to the UW Foundation (a 501(c)(3)) are tax-deductible.
 - **Board roster and overlaps:** Chancellor Sheila Edwards Lange sits on the EDB Board (source #3). She also sits on these outside boards: Pugh Capital Management, the Seattle Branch of the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, Washington Campus Compact, AAA Washington, and the Seattle Art Museum (source #4). The UW Board of Regents governs the whole university. UW Tacoma has an Advisory Board (source #4).
 - **Government funding share:** Tuition is the "largest single source of revenue" (source #2). State "proviso" budget investments (special line items in the state budget) are large and growing (source #6: "UW Tacoma has seen significant growth in 'proviso' state budget investments, such as recent increases for engineering"). Specific state appropriation amounts are NOT CONFIRMED. Chancellor Lange pushes for permanent state funding for student services (source #5).
- **Key facts:**
 - **Enrollment:** 5,059 total headcount in 2025-26 (source #1). 4,301 undergraduates plus 758 graduate students (source #1). Enrollment rose 3.97% in autumn 2024 (source #6). That put the campus within 7.4% of its peak enrollment in 2020-21 (source #6).
 - **Student demographics:** 52% are first-generation college students (source #1). 46% are Pell-eligible, meaning they qualify for federal grants for low-income students (source #1). 19% are military-affiliated (source #1). 60% of undergraduates are from underrepresented minorities (source #1). The breakdown is 32% Caucasian, 19% Asian American, 16% Hispanic/Latinx, and 11% African American (source #1). 51% are male, 49% female. 86% are full-time. The student-to-faculty ratio is 15 to 1 (source #1).
 - **Academic programs:** Over 50 undergraduate degree programs and options. There are 15 graduate programs (source #1). The largest schools are the School of Engineering & Technology (1,148 students), the School of Interdisciplinary Arts & Sciences (925), the Milgard School of Business (795), and the School of Social Work & Criminal Justice (392) (source #1). The most popular majors are Computer Science, Biomedical Sciences, Information Technology, Psychology, Accounting, and Finance (source #1).
 - **Money:** UW Tacoma gave out $60 million in financial aid in 2023-24 (source #1). Undergraduate tuition is $13,577 per year for 2025-26 (source #1). Graduate tuition is $22,668 per year (source #1). 68% of students get financial aid, including loans. 57% get scholarships or grants (source #1).

## 5. How they touch government
- **State funding:** UW Tacoma gets state appropriations through the UW system. Chancellor Lange goes to Olympia to push for permanent state funding for student services (source #5: "visited Olympia last week to advocate for permanent state funding for student success services"). She pointed out that "nearly half of students are Pell eligible, more than half are first generation, and one-fifth are military-affiliated" to argue that "deep budget cuts would uniquely impact UW Tacoma" (source #5).
- **State proviso investments:** UW Tacoma has received growing state "proviso" budget investments. These include $854K in fiscal year 2024 and $2M ongoing starting fiscal year 2025 to grow engineering enrollments (source #7). A one-time state appropriation paid for student support positions (re-enrollment, financial aid, mental health, career readiness) that launched the HuskiesCare online hub (source #5). A House capital budget proposal also included $7.7M for land acquisition at UW Tacoma (source #5 context).
