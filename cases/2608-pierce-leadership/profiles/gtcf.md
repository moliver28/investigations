## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why we added GTCF:** The Greater Tacoma Community Foundation (GTCF) is the largest community foundation serving Pierce County. It manages about $185 million in assets and gives out millions in grants each year. It sits at the center of the region's giving, government, and nonprofit worlds. Its board overlaps reach into county government (Rosie Ayala, a Pierce County Council member), labor (Nathe Lawver, secretary-treasurer of the Pierce County Central Labor Council, or PCCLC), banking (Christopher Algeo, an executive at Umpqua Bank), and higher education (Sheila Edwards Lange, chancellor of UW Tacoma). ## 1. Who they are
- **Legal name:** Greater Tacoma Community Foundation (GTCF).
- **Entity type:** 501(c)(3) public charity. A 501(c)(3) is a tax-exempt nonprofit under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. GTCF is a community foundation, classified under NTEE code Philanthropy, Voluntarism and Grantmaking Foundations.
- **Tax ID (EIN):** 91-1007459.
- **Tax exemption issued:** March 1978.
- **Location:** Tacoma, WA (Pierce County).
- **Governing law:** Internal Revenue Code section 501(c)(3); Washington State nonprofit corporation law.
- **Parent or subsidiaries:** None. GTCF is an independent organization. It runs a donor-advised fund program. A donor-advised fund is a giving account where the donor recommends which charities receive grants. Schedule D is the IRS tax form for those funds.
- **Founded:** June 1, 1981.
- **Tax-deductible donations:** Yes.
- **Fact:** Per the IRS Form 990 for fiscal year 2024, GTCF reported revenue of $25,187,478 and expenses of $22,957,398. Total assets were $185,695,628. Net assets were $183,479,117 (source #1).
- **Fact:** GTCF has grown assets to more than $207 million and cumulative grants past $218 million as of mid-2026, per the CEO (source #3).
- **Fact:** GTCF runs a donor-advised fund per its December 2024 tax filing (source #1).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Philanthropic power:** GTCF controls about $185 million or more in assets. It gives millions each year in grants to Pierce County nonprofits. In 2024, it paid roughly $18.3 to $18.6 million in grants to 524 to 545 grantees (sources #1, #4). That gives GTCF the power to set the agenda over which nonprofits and causes get funded.
- **Convening power:** CEO Kathi Littmann said GTCF's "real superpower is not just the money, but the relationships. It's convening" (source #5). GTCF acts as a neutral convener that brings together government, nonprofits, and donors.
- **Intermediary role:** GTCF holds a "strategic framework" to help Pierce County groups access government money. It acts as a pass-through for federal and private dollars (source #6).
- **Domain overlap:** GTCF works across institutional (giving), political (board overlaps with elected officials), and financial (manages $185 million or more, earns $7 million a year in investment income) fields.
- **Tax status:** 501(c)(3). Donations are tax-deductible. Standard 501(c)(3) lobbying limits apply.
- **Board:** 18 or more directors, including elected officials, labor leaders, bankers, educators, and retired judges (see §6).
- **Revenue mix:** Contributions make up 59.8% of revenue ($15 million). Investment income makes up 27.9% ($7 million). Sales of assets make up 11.1% ($2.8 million). The dollar amount of government grants passed through is not separately disclosed in the IRS 990 summary (source #1).

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** GTCF controls major giving flows in Pierce County. It gives out about $18 million or more in grants each year and holds $185 million or more in assets. It acts as a key go-between for government money, big donors like the Gates Foundation, and local nonprofits. Its power comes from being the only community foundation for Pierce County and from how connected its board is. Board ties reach into county government, labor, banking, education, and the courts. GTCF shapes priorities through its grant choices (equity-focused) and through bringing people together. It does not write laws or set regulations. But it shapes how money flows across the nonprofit world.
- **Conflicts of interest:** Rosie Ayala sits on both the GTCF board and the Pierce County Council. That creates a possible conflict if county decisions touch GTCF's government funding deals, or if GTCF grants fund groups that want county support. Jacques Colon's dual role (City of Tacoma strategic manager and GTCF board member) creates a similar city-level overlap. Nathe Lawver's dual role (GTCF board and PCCLC) ties giving decisions to labor endorsements.
- **The other view:** GTCF may be a passive pipe rather than a power center. Donor-advised funds mean donors, not GTCF staff, direct most grant decisions. The $18 million in grants may reflect donor choices more than GTCF's own agenda. But GTCF's discretionary grants (for example, $450,000 in competitive grants to 36 groups), its government pass-through role, and its board make-up suggest active institutional agency, not just a passive conduit.
- **Confidence:** High — IRS 990 data, the official GTCF website, and several other sources back up the financials, board make-up, and role of the organization.
