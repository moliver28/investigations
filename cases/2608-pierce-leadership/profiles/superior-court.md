## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- Pierce County Superior Court is on this map for two reasons: it holds office, and it makes big decisions. It is the only trial court in Pierce County with full authority over serious cases. Its judges hear all felonies, big civil cases over about $100,000, real estate fights, tax disputes, divorce, and probate. It also hears appeals from lower courts.
- The judges are elected. They have wide power to decide sentences, civil awards, and family cases.
- Pierce County District Court is on the map as a separate node. State appeals courts are mapped separately. The county clerk's office is not on the map because it only keeps records.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory authority:** The state constitution gives the court full power over felonies, serious civil cases, real estate title fights, tax legality, divorce, probate, and naturalization. It can issue court orders to compel witnesses, review lower courts, and free wrongfully held people. Source: WA Constitution Art. IV § 6.
- **Size and reach:** Pierce County has about 920,000 people. The court is the second-busiest superior court in Washington State, behind only King County. Judges set sentences in felony cases, order civil remedies, and decide family and probate cases.
- **How judges are chosen:** All 23 judges are elected in nonpartisan contests. They serve four-year terms. If a seat opens between elections, the governor appoints a replacement from a list vetted by the county bar association. No judge can serve past age 75.
- **Cases and enforcement:** The court hears felony cases brought by the Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney (`pa-office`/Mary Robnett). It also hears major civil cases, family law, and probate. Judges enforce orders through contempt powers, court orders, and sentencing. Specific yearly case numbers are not published in an easy-to-find place.
