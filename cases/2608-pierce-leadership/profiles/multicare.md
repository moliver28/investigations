## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Inclusion rule — power and decision-making.** MultiCare Health System is on this map because it is the largest private employer in Pierce County. It has 22,789 employees, runs 9 hospitals across Washington, and takes in more than $5 billion a year.
- MultiCare runs Tacoma General, Good Samaritan, Allenmore, Auburn, Covington, Capital Medical Center, and Mary Bridge Children's Hospital. It also runs Deaconess Hospital and Valley Hospital in Spokane.
- Its board connects to Tacoma Community College (TCC), the Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce, the Washington State Hospital Association (WSHA), and Pacific Lutheran University (PLU). All four are on this map.
- MultiCare's choices about what services to offer, where to expand, and whom to hire shape Pierce County's healthcare and its job market.
- **Adjacent but excluded:** MultiCare Foundations (EIN 91-1514257) is the giving arm. Mary Bridge Children's Foundation (EIN 94-303-0039) raises money for one hospital. Rockwood Clinic in Spokane and Indigo Urgent Care are also MultiCare, but outside Pierce County or not power nodes.

## 1. Who they are

- **Hospitals (9):** MultiCare Tacoma General; MultiCare Good Samaritan (Puyallup); MultiCare Allenmore (Tacoma); MultiCare Auburn Medical Center; MultiCare Covington; MultiCare Capital Medical Center (Olympia); MultiCare Deaconess (Spokane); MultiCare Valley (Spokane); Mary Bridge Children's Hospital (Tacoma).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Market power:** MultiCare is the largest community-based, locally governed health system in Washington. In 2024 it had $5.01 billion in revenue and 22,789 employees. It operates 9 hospitals with 1,895 beds. In 2024 it logged 84,327 admissions, 431,429 patient days, 75,990 surgeries, 9,704 births, 480,752 outpatient visits, and 612,092 ER visits. That gives it the dominant share of the hospital market in Pierce County.
- **Employment power:** With 22,789 employees, MultiCare is the largest private employer in Pierce County. That comes from the IRS Form 990 and from MultiCare's own claims.
- **Tax-exempt power:** As a 501(c)(3), MultiCare does not pay property tax or sales tax. Donors can write off gifts. That is a big public subsidy.
- **Philanthropy and influence:** MultiCare's community fund gave 66 grants in 2024, totaling $10.8 million. This builds goodwill and softens public scrutiny.
- **Lobbying and policy:** MultiCare belongs to WSHA (Washington State Hospital Association). CEO Bill Robertson was WSHA Board Chair from 2020 to 2021. MultiCare also lobbies the Pierce County Council on health policy.
- **Key facts:**
  - Tax status: 501(c)(3) public charity, tax-exempt since May 1991.
  - Board size: 10 unpaid directors. Board seats overlap with TCC, the Chamber, WSHA, and PLU.
  - Government funding share: About 95.7% of revenue comes from Medicare and Medicaid (the federal health programs for seniors and low-income people). That is $4.795 billion of $5.01 billion.

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** MultiCare Health System controls the dominant share of hospital healthcare delivery in Pierce County through market position (9 hospitals, 1,895 beds, $5B revenue, 22,789 employees) and institutional influence (WSHA board chair, Chamber/TCC/PLU board overlaps, $10.8M/yr in community grants). Its power derives from three reinforcing sources: (1) statutory tax exemption (501c3) providing fiscal subsidy; (2) near-total dependency on government healthcare reimbursement (~95.7% of revenue from Medicare/Medicaid), making it effectively a government-funded enterprise operating with private governance; (3) board overlaps with educational, business, and political institutions that create a web of mutual obligation. MultiCare got to this position through decades of acquisition (Auburn, Covington, Capital Medical Center, Deaconess, Valley) and organic growth, expanding from a Tacoma-centric hospital system to a statewide empire under Robertson's leadership (2014-2026).
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags (defense counter-argument):** The defense would argue MultiCare's power is benign — it is a nonprofit providing essential healthcare services, its tax exemption is legally granted by IRS, and its community grants demonstrate genuine philanthropic intent. The ~95.7% government revenue share is a function of the U.S. healthcare financing system, not a power grab. board overlaps are standard nonprofit governance practice, not evidence of coordination. The CEO's WSHA chair role is industry advocacy, not regulatory capture.
- **Analysis of alternatives :** The opposing reading is that MultiCare's market dominance and tax-exempt status represent a form of privatized governance — public money (~$4.8B/yr in government healthcare payments) flows through private governance structures with no direct public accountability. The 10-member board is self-perpetuating (no public elections, no government appointees), yet the organization's financial viability depends on public funds. The community grants program could be interpreted as institutional capture — buying goodwill from organizations that might otherwise scrutinize MultiCare's pricing, billing practices, or expansion plans.
