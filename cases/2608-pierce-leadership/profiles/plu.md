## 1. Who they are
- Legal name: **Pacific Lutheran University, Inc.** (a Washington nonprofit corporation).
- Entity type: **501(c)(3) private university**. A 501(c)(3) is a tax-exempt nonprofit under U.S. tax law. It is affiliated with the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA). It is a tax-exempt nonprofit. It is governed under state law RCW 24.03 (Washington Nonprofit Corporation Act) and Title 28B RCW (Higher Education).
- Parent and subsidiaries: No parent company. PLU Foundation, Inc. is a separate fundraising arm. PLU Tacoma is the main campus. It is in Parkland, an unincorporated part of Pierce County. The mailing address is Tacoma, WA 98447.
- Founding: Started in 1890 by Rev. Bjug Harstad. The 14th president, **L. Allan Belton**, was picked by the Board of Regents. He started April 3, 2019. He had been acting president since 2017.
- Provost: Joanna Gregson. Faculty: 340. Students: 2,754 (2,366 undergraduate and 388 postgraduate). Campus: 156 acres in suburban Parkland, WA.
- Endowment: **$123.1 million** (Wikipedia infobox, FY24 figure).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Decision-making power (institutional):** PLU's Board of Regents sets tuition. It approves the academic-medical partnership. It governs the on-campus MultiCare Medical Center that opens in 2025–26. That gives the Board veto and approval power over any clinical site on its 156-acre campus.
- **Cultural influence (symbolic):** PLU is the largest ELCA university on the West Coast. It is the only private four-year school in central Pierce County. That makes it a key name for Lutheran employers, regional hospital systems, and Tacoma-area civic boards.
- **Workforce-pipeline power:** Through the Partnership for Health Innovation, PLU channels MultiCare hiring, WSU medical-school rotations, and NIH and HRSA grant applications. HRSA is the Health Resources and Services Administration, a federal agency. NIH is the National Institutes of Health. In short, PLU sits at a key point for the Pierce County healthcare workforce.
- How the score covers different domains: board overlap (Belton graph weight 5), partnership (MultiCare graph weight 5), and reputational role as the only private university in unincorporated Pierce County.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line:** PLU is a tier-3 institutional node whose power is indirect but structural. PLU does not control budgets the size of MultiCare's. But it controls who trains where in central Pierce County. And it controls whose medical school sits on its campus. The February 2024 Partnership for Health Innovation turned PLU from a passive reputational asset into an active decision-making node in the regional healthcare economy.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** (a) The MultiCare Medical Center at PLU is funded and run by MultiCare. But it sits on land controlled by an ELCA board. Questions of mission drift and the scope of reproductive and health services are foreseeable. (b) Allan Belton's role straddles academic governance and a quasi-development partnership. The PLU Board of Regents' conflict-of-interest policy should be reviewed.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** PLU could also be seen as a small liberal-arts school whose national rankings have slipped. US News has moved PLU out of top-tier regional categories in recent years. Its inclusion in this map rests almost entirely on the Belton board overlap and the MultiCare partnership. It is not based on raw institutional scale. **If the MultiCare partnership is renegotiated or unwound, PLU's graph weight drops sharply.**
- **Confidence:** **Medium** — three primary and secondary sources confirm identity and the partnership. The MultiCare ground-lease terms, board composition, and conflict-of-interest filings were not located.
