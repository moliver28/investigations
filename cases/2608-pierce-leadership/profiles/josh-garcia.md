## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** Garcia is the operational head of the third-largest K-12 system in Washington and a frequent national speaker on student-success frameworks. His power is institutional, not statutory — he drafts the policies the elected board approves, hires central-office and building administrators, and runs a general-fund budget of about $600 million. The 11-year rise in graduation rates and the "Zone of Innovation" designation are his signature wins. The Whole Child framework has become a brand he travels the country to promote. His pay is documented (base $432,781 in 2025-26; total pay $390,010–$403,217 in 2024-25), placing him among the highest-paid district superintendents in Washington.
- **Conflicts to watch:**
 - The "Innovation Zone" policy has allowed waiver of some state rules. Critics on both the left and right have flagged relaxed testing and accountability.
 - Whole Child curriculum buying decisions concentrate power in a small network of vendors and consultants (national AASA / ASCD affiliates).
 - Counter-argument: a school superintendent is not a "Pierce leader" in the political sense, and including him weakens the boundary.
- **The counter-argument.** A skeptical reading treats Garcia as a bureaucratic manager of state-mandated K-12 instruction, with most real decision-making power at the school board, the state Superintendent of Public Instruction (Chris Reykdal), and the Legislature. The opposing reading (favored here) treats him as the durable operational architect of TPS's brand, its innovation-zone status, and its national profile — all of which translate into well-connected capital that the Pierce institutional layer pulls on.
