## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Inclusion rule — from holding office + decision-making + well-connected:** Pierce County FCZD is the only countywide flood-control taxing district in Pierce County. It was formed under Chapter 86.15 of the state law (RCW). It can levy property taxes up to $0.10 per $1,000 of assessed value. It gives grants for flood-risk reduction. It has fiscal power over every parcel in unincorporated Pierce County and, through interlocal agreements, over incorporated cities too. That gives it a role in countywide land-use and infrastructure choices. Any city, tribe, or nonprofit with a creek or floodplain can apply for grants, so it is well-connected by design.
- **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** Pierce County Public Works Surface Water Management provides the executive director and staff under contract and appears in FCZD documents. Pierce County Council creates and oversees the District. City Public Works departments in Tacoma, Lakewood, Puyallup, Sumner, and Orting get FCZD grants but are separate. Tacoma Water and Puget Sound Energy are regulated separately. FEMA Region 10 and the National Flood Insurance Program are federal partners and are separate.

## 1. Who they are
- **Exact legal name:** Pierce County Flood Control Zone District (also called Pierce County FCZD or "the District").
- **Entity type:** Special-purpose quasi-municipal corporation and taxing district formed under Chapter 86.15 RCW ("Flood Control Zone Districts"). Pierce County is the "county with a population of 775,000 or more" named in RCW 84.52.816.
- **Authorizing statute:** Chapter 86.15 RCW. RCW 86.15.095 sets the rate cap of $0.10 per $1,000 of assessed value. RCW 84.52.816 protects part of that levy from prorationing. RCW 86.15.160 allows grants to cities and others. The Board must follow the Open Public Meetings Act (RCW 42.30).
- **Formation:** The Pierce County Council formed the District as allowed by Chapter 86.15 RCW (per the FIFE WA fact sheet). The District's "annual regular" levy is named in interlocal agreement exhibits.
- **Governing body:** Board of Supervisors, made up of Pierce County Council members and other officials appointed by member jurisdictions. The Board follows District Operating Rules like Resolution No. 2012-1, amended by 2017-2. Chair for 2024–2026 is Pierce County Council Chair Dave Morell.
- **Parent/subsidiaries:** No parent. The Executive Director and staff come from Pierce County Public Works / Surface Water Management under contract. The District office is at 1501 Market Street, Suite 140, Tacoma, WA 98402; phone 206-612-8138.
- **Governing law:** Chapter 86.15 RCW; RCW 84.52 (property tax); RCW 42.30 (Open Public Meetings Act); interlocal agreements with member jurisdictions.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory taxing authority (FACT):** RCW 86.15.095 caps the District's regular property tax levy at $0.10 per $1,000 of assessed value. RCW 84.52.816 protects part of the District's levy from prorationing by general government levies because Pierce County's population is over 775,000. Resolution No. 2016-8s and No. 2020-9s both name the District's "banked capacity" under RCW 84.55.092. "Banked capacity" means unused levy room the District can spend later.
- **Grant-making authority (FACT):** The District runs a recurring Flood Risk Reduction and Watershed Management Grants program. It is open to "local jurisdictions, Indian Tribes, and non-profit organizations." Per-program caps: up to $1,000,000 per construction project and up to $125,000 per study or plan (per a Pierce County announcement for the 2022 cycle; same pattern in 2024 resolutions). Chair Morell said the program funds "applicants … for construction projects or studies/plans that … protect lives and reduce the public risk caused by flooding."
- **Budget scale (FACT):** A countywide levy of about $0.09 per $1,000 AV in 2024 raises about $18.9 million in property-tax revenue for the District (per the District Funding page).
- **Comprehensive planning authority (FACT):** The District adopted its 2025 Comprehensive Plan of Development on April 23, 2025. The plan "incorporates key elements of Pierce County's Comprehensive Flood Hazard Management Plan." That makes the FCZD a layer in the county's flood-hazard-management system, not just a grant-maker.
- **Domain overlaps:**
  - **gov ↔ econ:** The property-tax levy plus grant disbursement (about $18.9M per year) makes the District a money bridge between county taxpayers and municipal, tribal, and nonprofit projects.
  - **gov ↔ environment:** Flood-risk reduction, salmon habitat restoration, stormwater cleanup.
  - **gov ↔ institutional:** Sets conditions on interlocal partners and absorbs Pierce County Surface Water Management capacity.
- **FACT vs INFERENCE:** The above are FACT (statute, district resolutions, district funding page). The "money bridge" framing is INFERENCE (a functional description, not contested).

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** Pierce County FCZD is a countywide, Council-created flood-control taxing district. Its statutory rate cap is $0.10 per $1,000 AV (RCW 86.15.095). At its current rate of about $0.09, it raises about $18.9M a year (2024). It is not a regulator or a service provider in the everyday sense. It is a fiscal and planning body. It adopts a Comprehensive Plan of Development (the latest was adopted April 23, 2025), sets an annual levy, and gives grants to cities, tribes, and nonprofits for flood-risk reduction and salmon-habitat restoration. Its practical power comes from (a) selective grant-making that shapes what cities and tribes build on floodplains (the White River Restoration in Sumner protects $1.14B in property and 16,000 jobs — a textbook funded project), and (b) convening power through its Board of Supervisors, which pulls Council members and member-jurisdiction officials around a shared flood-hazard plan.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** The Board of Supervisors is mostly Pierce County Council members and member-jurisdiction officials. In plain terms, the same people who control County and city budgets also sit as Supervisors of the District. RCW 86.15 allows this, but it creates an overlap risk: the cities that get grants are the same ones whose officials sit as Supervisors. The executive director and staff also come from Pierce County Public Works / Surface Water Management — the same county department that benefits from the District's work. These overlaps are allowed by the statute but should be flagged as the structural reason District grants tend to match County executive priorities.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** A defense reading would say the District's rate cap is set by law, the Board is publicly appointed, its meetings follow the Open Public Meetings Act, and grants are awarded through published application cycles with clear criteria. In short, it is procedurally constrained and transparent. The other reading is that the Board's overlap with Council members and the staff-sharing deal with Public Works mean the District acts less as an independent flood-control body and more as a fiscal arm of the County executive — especially when cities like Tacoma or Lakewood run their own stormwater utilities independently.
- **Confidence:** Medium — structural and statutory facts verified against the District's own website, Document Center, and Pierce County announcements. The FY2025 levy rate and budget figure come from the District Funding page.
