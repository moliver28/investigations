## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Inclusion rule — from holding office + decision-making + well-connected.** Joint Base Lewis-McChord (JBLM) is on this map as the largest employer in Pierce County and a federal military installation. Its economic, social, and political impacts run through the whole county. Its power from the office comes from being a federal installation. Federal land is not under local rules or taxes. Its power to make decisions covers base operations and land-use choices on the 90,283-acre base. ## 1. Who they are
- **Exact legal name:** Joint Base Lewis-McChord (JBLM)
- **Entity type:** Federal military installation. The U.S. Army installation Fort Lewis merged with the U.S. Air Force base McChord AFB under Base Realignment and Closure (BRAC) 2005. The joint base was established in 2010.
- **Parent organization:** U.S. Department of the Army (main). U.S. Department of the Air Force runs the McChord Field part.
- **Governing law:** Federal military law (10 USC). The Base Realignment and Closure Act. Federal land management rules.
- **Size:** 90,283 acres in Pierce County. Plus 323,431 acres at Yakima Training Center.
- **Location:** Mostly in Pierce County, near Lakewood, DuPont, and Roy, WA.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Federal military installation:** JBLM is a federal installation. It is not subject to local zoning, taxes, or rules. The Department of the Army runs its operations, not Pierce County government. This federal sovereignty (similar to but not the same as tribal sovereignty) means the county cannot regulate base activities. The county can coordinate through voluntary partnerships (SSMCP).
- **Economic power:** JBLM is the #1 employer in Pierce County and #2 employer in Washington State. Its economic impact includes:
 - 78,890 total jobs statewide (direct, indirect, and induced) per the 2024 Washington Statewide Defense Economic Impact Study.
 - About $9 billion to the state economy (GSP) per the 2024 study.
 - For Pierce County alone: $3.9B in direct output. $12.1B combined for Pierce and Thurston counties.
 - $622M in federal contracts and $11.3M in federal grants came into Pierce County in FY2023.
 - 41% of defense contract spending ($211M) went to commercial and institutional building construction.
- **Workforce pipeline:** About 7,000 service members per year go through JBLM's Transition Assistance Program (TAP). Of those, 2,000–2,500 stay in Washington State. Over 50% of transitioning members stay in the region. This creates a trained, disciplined labor pool for regional employers — a special economic-development asset.
- **Strategic port connection:** JBLM is linked to the Port of Tacoma. The port is classified as a strategic port by the Department of Defense. That gives the port access to federal funds to maintain operating capacity.
- **Key facts:**
 - *Tax status:* Federal land. JBLM is exempt from all state and local taxes. It is not a 501(c) entity.
 - *Board overlaps:* SSMCP (works with local governments). PMBA (community nonprofit). Adam Smith (House Armed Services Committee).
 - *Government funding:* 100% federal. No county or state appropriations. But JBLM creates demand for county services (roads, schools, housing) without adding to the county tax base — a fiscal asymmetry.

## 4. Their money
- **Economic impact (2024 Washington Statewide Defense Economic Impact Study):**
 - 78,890 total jobs statewide (direct, indirect, induced).
 - About $9 billion to the state GSP.
 - Over 6,000 workers owe their jobs to procurement. $681.4M GSP from procurement. About $62M in state and local tax revenue.
 - $622M in federal contracts to Pierce County (FY2023).
 - $11.3M in federal grants to Pierce County (FY2023).
 - 533 contractors. 229 industries supported.
 - 41% of defense contract spending ($211M) went to commercial and institutional building construction.
- **Pierce County-specific:** $3.9B direct output (Pierce-only). $12.1B combined for Pierce and Thurston counties. These are economic footprint figures, not a budget controlled by JBLM.
- **Defense spending statewide (FY2023):** $27.2 billion across all installations.
- **JBLE (Joint Base Langley-Eustis) comparison:** FY24 economic impact $4.51B, 38,166 jobs — a different base, not JBLM. Listed for scale comparison only.
- **Fiscal asymmetry:** JBLM is 100% federally funded. It does not pay property taxes to Pierce County. But it creates demand for county services: roads, schools (Clover Park SD), housing, healthcare, social services. This is a net fiscal cost to the county. It is partly offset by Impact Aid (federal payments to school districts serving military families) and the economic multiplier effect.
- **Port of Tacoma strategic port:** The Port's DoD strategic port designation gives it access to federal funds for keeping operating capacity going. That is a direct financial benefit of JBLM's presence.

SSMCP works on housing, transportation, education, land use (encroachment prevention), military spouse jobs, and veteran services. SSMCP members include Pierce County, Thurston County, Lakewood, Lacey, DuPont, Tacoma, University Place, Yelm, Nisqually Tribe, and many working group members (Bates Tech, Clover Park SD, Pierce Transit, Chamber, Port of Tacoma, etc.).
- **Pierce County Council (federal_appropriations, weight 6):** The Council connects to JBLM through the federal money pipeline. Rep. Adam Smith (House Armed Services Committee), Senators Cantwell and Murray, and Reps. Randall, Strickland, and Schrier all have federal_funding edges to the Council. JBLM-related federal money flows through this pipeline.
- **Clover Park School District (serves, weight 4):** CPSD serves JBLM. About 1/3 of CPSD students are military-connected. CPSD runs 6 elementary schools on base. CPSD is a Purple Star Award district. Impact Aid (federal money) partly makes up for the lost property tax revenue from federal land.
- **Rep. Adam Smith (armed_services_champion, weight 6):** Smith (WA-09) sits on the House Armed Services Committee. That is the main congressional body for defense authorization. He is JBLM's most important congressional advocate.
- **Boeing (aerospace_partner, weight 3):** Boeing's Frederickson plant builds 787 and 777X tail and wing structures. It is a defense aerospace supply chain partner.
- **PMBA (supports, weight 4):** Pierce Military & Business Alliance. It is a community nonprofit supporting JBLM-community relations.
- **Environmental / land use:** JBLM coordinates with local governments on encroachment prevention (clear zones, noise contours, land use compatibility). The Joint Land Use Study (SSMCP) works on ways to increase communication and apply land-use planning tools near the base.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** JBLM exercises power through three channels: (1) **economic gravity** — as the #1 employer in Pierce County (78,890 jobs statewide, about $9B state GSP, $622M in federal contracts to Pierce County), JBLM's presence shapes the county's economy, housing market, transportation system, and school districts; (2) **federal sovereignty** — as a federal installation, JBLM is immune from local regulation and taxation, creating a fiscal asymmetry where the county provides services (roads, schools, emergency response) without receiving property tax revenue from the 90,283-acre base; (3) **coordination infrastructure** — through SSMCP, JBLM keeps structured coordination with 15+ local governments on encroachment prevention, housing, transportation, and education. LTG McFarlane, as I Corps Commanding General, is the most powerful appointed (non-elected) official in the county. Rep. Adam Smith's position on the House Armed Services Committee provides key congressional protection.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags (defense-attorney counter-argument anticipated):**
 - *Fiscal asymmetry:* JBLM does not pay property taxes. Yet it creates demand for county services (roads, schools, emergency response, social services). This is a net fiscal cost to Pierce County. It is partly offset by Impact Aid and economic multipliers. A defense attorney would argue this is a federal mandate, not a local choice. The county cannot regulate or tax JBLM.
 - *Encroachment as veto:* JBLM's concerns about encroachment (clear zones, noise contours, land use compatibility) can effectively veto or constrain local development near the base. This is a federal power that goes beyond local zoning authority. The County Council's land-use power does not extend to the base or its surroundings in the same way it does elsewhere.
 - *Economic dependence:* The county's economic dependence on JBLM (57,000+ direct jobs, #1 employer) creates a structural risk. Any reduction in base operations (BRAC, deployment changes, budget cuts) would have cascading economic effects. This dependence limits the county's ability to push back against JBLM-related concerns.
 - *Influence without accountability:* JBLM's coordination through SSMCP gives it influence over local land-use and transportation decisions. But JBLM officials are not elected by Pierce County voters and are not accountable to the county electorate.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** The opposing reading is that JBLM's power is economic, not political. It does not lobby the County Council, donate to campaigns, or directly shape county policy. Its influence is passive — a result of its size and federal status, not an exercise of political agency. SSMCP is a voluntary coordination body, not a regulatory authority. The congressional delegation (Smith, Cantwell, Murray) protects JBLM's interests, but this is federal politics, not county politics. Under this reading, JBLM's rank of 5/163 reflects its economic footprint rather than active political power. It is a "force of nature" in the county economy, not a political actor. The real political power around JBLM is exercised by the congressional delegation and the SSMCP, not by JBLM itself.
