## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why included:** big member count plus well-connected. Teamsters Local 117 is the largest Teamsters local in Washington, with 19,500 members. Many work in Pierce County, especially as state corrections officers. Joint Council 28 covers 50,000+ members in Washington, Alaska, and Idaho. Both groups back Pierce County candidates. Both are part of the Pierce County Central Labor Council (PCCLC), the local AFL-CIO body. (Sources 1, 3, 5, 6)
- **Who was left out and why:** UFCW 3000, SEIU 925, SEIU 1199NW, and the Washington Education Association (WEA) are other unions. They sit in WSLC/PCCLC but are not Teamsters. Other Teamsters locals (174, 313, 38, 252, 763) are also part of Joint Council 28 but are smaller and less focused on Pierce County.

## 1. Who they are
- **Full legal name:** Teamsters Local Union No. 117. It is part of the International Brotherhood of Teamsters (IBT).
- **Type:** Labor union. Tax status: 501(c)(5), a tax-exempt labor group under the U.S. tax code.
- **Parent:** International Brotherhood of Teamsters (IBT), based in Washington, D.C.
- **Regional group:** Teamsters Joint Council No. 28 (JC 28), in Tukwila, WA. It links Teamsters locals in Washington, Alaska, and the Idaho Panhandle. It has 50,000+ members. (Sources 6, 7)
- **Local 117 HQ:** 14675 Interurban Ave S, Suite 301, Tukwila, WA 98168. (Source 7)
- **JC 28 HQ:** Same address. Phone: (206) 441-7470. (Source 7)
- **Local 313 (Tacoma):** 220 S. 27th St., Tacoma, WA 98402. Phone: (253) 627-0103. This is the direct Teamsters base in Pierce County. (Source 8)

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Members give them weight.** Local 117 speaks for 19,500 workers in Washington, both public and private jobs. That includes 6,000+ state corrections officers (DOC), plus nurses, maintenance, and office staff. (Sources 3, 4)
- **They bargain contracts.** The local holds contracts covering Washington State DOC workers (2023–2025 contract on file), freight drivers, and public safety staff. In April 2025 they won the top regional pay for fuel truck drivers under the APP/World Kinect deal. (Sources 4, 6)
- **They play politics.** They run a federal PAC (a political action committee that raises and spends money on elections) called DRIVE, funded by voluntary member donations. They also run a state PAC called Teamsters Local 117 Working Families PAC. They back candidates in Pierce County, King County, and across the state. A Political Advisory Committee screens candidates and picks who to back. (Sources 1, 5, 9)
- **They organize new workers.** In August 2026, 500 public safety workers from PSEU Local 519 voted to join Local 117 — a major organizing win. (Source 3)
- **Domains they span:** politics (endorsements, PAC), labor (contracts), government (DOC workforce), and corrections (6,000+ officers).

## 5. How they touch government
- **2025/2026 endorsements:** Pierce County Council District 5 — Bryan Yambe. Tacoma Metropolitan Park District — several candidates. LD 26 Sen. Deborah Krishnadasan. LD 27 — Laurie Jinkins, Jake Fey. LD 28 — Mari Leavitt, Dan Bronoske. LD 29 — Krista Perez, Sharlett Mena. (Sources 1, 10)
- **PCCLC link:** They sit in the Pierce County Central Labor Council (PCCLC), the AFL-CIO body for local unions. PCCLC 2026 endorsements include Marilyn Strickland (10th CD), Bryan Yambe (CC D5), Kelsey Barrans (CC D1), Brenda Lykins (CC D7). (Source 10)
- **PAC structure:** DRIVE is the federal PAC, funded by member donations. Teamsters Local 117 Working Families PAC is the state PAC, registered with the state's campaign-finance agency (PDC) and the IRS. DRIVE gave over $100,000 to the state PAC. A prior "Segregated Fund" shut down in 2020 after a Freedom Foundation lawsuit. (Source 9)
- **State contract:** The 2023–2025 DOC contract covers all non-supervisory DOC staff at prisons, correctional industries, the sex offender treatment program, and regional business offices. (Source 4)
- **Statewide endorsements:** Bob Ferguson for Governor (2024); Nick Brown for Attorney General; Emily Randall for WA-06. (Sources 2, 3)

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** Teamsters Local 117 holds strong labor power through 19,500 members (6,000+ are state corrections officers), contracts over major state workforce groups, and a PAC that shapes Pierce County elections. Its JC 28 link (50,000+ members) and PCCLC link boost its political reach. Its power comes from how many workers it speaks for in key sectors (corrections, public safety, freight) and from running endorsements as a group.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** They once ran a dues-funded political fund (Segregated Fund) until Freedom Foundation sued in 2020. DRIVE now uses voluntary donations only. But the line between dues-funded organizing and political activity is still debated. Backing politicians who regulate their own workplaces also raises conflict questions.
- **Counter-argument:** Local 117's 19,500 members are still only a small share of all Washington workers. Their endorsements often back Democrats who would win anyway. So direct impact is hard to measure. The PSEU win was modest (500 workers). Their PAC spends less than bigger statewide PACs.
- **Confidence:** High — strong primary sources from union websites, IBT press releases, the state OFM contract file, PCCLC endorsement records, and JC 28 official data.
