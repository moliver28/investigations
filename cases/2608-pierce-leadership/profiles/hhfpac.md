## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why HHFPAC is on the map.** HHFPAC is the political action committee run by the Washington State Hospital Association (WSHA). WSHA is the main hospital lobbying group in the state. HHFPAC gives campaign money to state legislative and judicial candidates who support hospital-friendly policies. Its state campaign-finance agency (PDC) filings and enforcement history show it moves a lot of money, so it matters in the campaign-finance network that shapes Pierce County and statewide health policy.
- **Who is NOT on the map.** WSHA itself (the trade group) is mapped separately as `wsha`. Other health-sector PACs (like the WHCA PAC and the Essential Hospitals PAC) are out of scope or federal. Individual HHFPAC donors are not institutional actors and are excluded.

## 1. Who they are
- **Legal name:** Hospitals for a Healthy Future Political Action Committee (HHFPAC).
- **Type:** A PAC registered with the Washington State Public Disclosure Commission (PDC). It is a state-level PAC, not federal.
- **Parent group:** The Washington State Hospital Association (WSHA). WSHA is a 501(c)(6) trade association. WSHA runs HHFPAC as part of its advocacy work. The WSHA Board and Public Policy Committee direct HHFPAC's political activity.
- **Governing law:** State law (RCW) 42.17A (Campaign Finance Disclosure). PACs must register with the PDC, file C3 (contribution) and C4 (spending) reports, and follow contribution limits and reporting deadlines.
- **PDC registration:** Registered as "Hospitals for a Healthy Future PAC" with the PDC. Also named in PDC enforcement case #169863.

## 5. How they touch government
- **PDC registration and reporting.** HHFPAC is a registered PAC with the WA PDC. It must file C3 (contribution) and C4 (spending) reports. It is subject to RCW 42.17A.235 and .240 (timely and accurate disclosure).
- **PDC enforcement case #169863.** Complaints filed by Conner Edwards (March 31, 2025 and May 20, 2025) said HHFPAC broke RCW 42.17A.235 and .240 by failing to timely and accurately disclose contributions and spending in 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025. The specific violations: (a) COH balance discrepancies of about $100K and about $50K between closing and opening C4 reports; (b) late C3/C4 reports (including a $61,700 deposit filed 8 days late during the primary voting period); (c) a missing $32,000 deposit and a $6,825 audit payment on a C4 report. Case status as of May 2026: "Investigation of Possible Violation." A second case (#194709) also appears in the May 2026 enforcement report.
- **Campaign contributions.** HHFPAC gives money to state legislative and judicial candidates. Known recipients: WA Supreme Court candidates Diaz, O'Donnell, and Melody in 2026. Other recipients: UNVERIFIED — full contribution history was not pulled from PDC in this pass.
- **Lobbying tie.** As a WSHA-run PAC, HHFPAC is closely linked to WSHA's lobbying work. WSHA lobbies on health policy bills (like HB 1123 on hospital cost containment, which WSHA opposed). The PAC makes sure legislative allies get financial support, which boosts WSHA's lobbying effectiveness.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** HHFPAC's power comes from its role as the political spending arm of WSHA, Washington's main hospital trade group. With hundreds of thousands of dollars on hand each year ($579K closing 2024), it moves hospital industry money to state legislative and judicial candidates who back hospital-friendly policies. Its recent move into judicial races (2026 WA Supreme Court) signals a strategic expansion past the legislature. But its credibility is hurt by PDC enforcement cases (#169863, #194709) that allege multi-year reporting violations — including about $150K in cash-balance gaps and repeated late filings. That suggests either operational sloppiness or deliberate hiding of the PAC's financial activity.
- **Conflicts to watch.** The $357K figure referenced in the assignment is UNVERIFIED — no primary source confirming it was found. The PDC enforcement complaints show reporting problems that could hide undisclosed contributions or spending. HHFPAC's move into judicial races creates a conflict-of-interest concern: a PAC funded by the hospital industry supports judicial candidates who may rule on hospital-related lawsuits. WSHA's opposition to cost-containment laws (HB 1123) while it backs the legislators who vote on those laws is standard PAC influence but warrants scrutiny.
- **The counter-argument.** HHFPAC's financial activity is large for a state PAC but small compared to major partisan PACs (like the Harry Truman Fund at $1.4 million total contributions). The reporting violations may be administrative errors, not deliberate misconduct — the PAC had "no prior enforcement matters" before these complaints. The complaints came from a single individual (Conner Edwards) who appears to file against many PACs and candidates (per the May 2026 enforcement report listing multiple Edwards complaints). That context may soften the weight of the enforcement cases.
