## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why included:** The Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney's Office is on this map because it holds a key legal office and makes major charging decisions. It is the only office that can bring felony charges anywhere in Pierce County. It also prosecutes misdemeanors outside city limits. And it represents every county office in civil lawsuits. State law (RCW 36.27.020) gives it this power. The office has about 263 full-time positions and an $88.6 million proposed budget for two years. The elected prosecutor has independent power under state law. She is not under the County Executive or Council. (sources #1, #2)
- **Adjacent but NOT included:** Pierce County Public Defender's Office (separate office, not on the map). Washington State Attorney General's Office (state-level, helps on some cases under RCW 43.10). Tacoma City Attorney's Office (handles city-only misdemeanors). These are nearby but not in the map at this level.

## 1. Who they are
- **Legal name:** Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney's Office
- **What kind of office:** County government office led by an elected prosecutor. Created under state law RCW Chapter 36.27.
- **Governing law:** RCW Chapter 36.27 covers the prosecutor. It sets who can serve (RCW 36.27.010: must be a lawyer in Washington and a U.S. citizen). It lists the duties (RCW 36.27.020). It lets the prosecutor hire deputies (RCW 36.27.040). It says the office must be at the county seat (RCW 36.27.070).
- **Location:** 930 Tacoma Ave S, Rm 946, Tacoma, WA 98402 (source #3)
- **Federal tax ID (EIN):** Not applicable — it is part of Pierce County, not a separate legal body.
- **Parent:** Pierce County government
- **Current head:** Mary Robnett, elected Prosecuting Attorney. Took office January 2019 (source #1).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **State law power:** RCW 36.27.020 sets the duties. The office must prosecute every criminal case in the county. It must represent the county in civil lawsuits. It must advise county officials. This gives the office a monopoly on felony prosecution in Pierce County.
- **Charging power:** The office chooses which cases to charge. This lets it control what enters the criminal justice system. In 2024, it received 7,155 case referrals. About 54% of those were violent crimes (source #5).
- **Three divisions:**
  1. **Criminal Division** — handles all felony cases in Pierce County and all misdemeanors outside city limits. It has 197 full-time positions (65% of the total 263). It also runs therapeutic court programs (sources #4, #5).
  2. **Civil Division** — represents county officials and departments in lawsuits. It supports civil commitment-forcing involuntary treatment under state law (source #4).
  3. **Family Support Division** — protects children, establishes who the parents are, and enforces child support (source #4).
- **Budget:** The proposed budget for 2024–2025 was $88.6 million (source #4). The office also faces $3.35 million in cuts. Twelve positions paid with federal COVID aid will end by 2026 (source #5).
- **Where the office reaches:** All of Pierce County (about 920,000 people). Felonies county-wide. Misdemeanors only outside city limits (cities have their own prosecutors).
- **How the head is chosen:** Prosecuting Attorney is an elected office. Terms are four years. Elections are non-partisan (no party listed on the ballot). Mary Robnett won in 2018 and took office in January 2019 (sources #1, #6).

## 5. How they touch government
- **County Council funding:** The Pierce County Council approves the prosecutor's budget. The Council approved the $88.6 million proposed budget for 2024–2025 (source #4).
- **Federal COVID aid:** Twelve positions paid with American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) money will end by 2026–2027. That saves about $3.35 million but cuts the Criminal Division by 6.5% (source #5).
- **Federal cases:** The office works with federal prosecutors at WDWA (the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington) when cases cross federal and state lines.
- **Superior Court:** The office prosecutes cases in Pierce County Superior Court.
- **Campaign finance filings:** Mary Robnett filed to run for Pierce County Prosecutor in 2018. Her campaign raised $325,656.40 and ended with $10,928.80 in debt, per the state Public Disclosure Commission (PDC) (source #6).
- **Conflict with Keith Swank:** The map shows a conflict tie with Keith Swank. The details are UNVERIFIED from primary sources.
- **County canvassing board:** Under RCW 29A.60.160, the prosecuting attorney sits on the county canvassing board. This board checks election results.

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** The Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney's Office has a monopoly on felony prosecution in the county. It also represents county government in all civil lawsuits. With 263 positions and an $88.6 million two-year budget, it is a major power center. Its charging choices shape criminal justice outcomes for about 920,000 residents. The office is independent of the County Executive and Council because the prosecutor is elected separately. But the Council still controls the budget. The end of ARPA funding (12 positions cut by 2026, 6.5% Criminal Division cut) is a current strain on the office.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** The prosecutor's seat on the county canvassing board (RCW 29A.60.160) could mix election oversight with partisan interests, even though the office is non-partisan. The conflict with Keith Swank (map weight 3) needs more checking — UNVERIFIED from primary sources. Mary Robnett's past work at the AG's Office is a coordination tie, not a conflict.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** The opposing reading is that the office is more limited than it looks. Charging decisions are bound by state law, evidence rules, and judges. The felony backlog (~1,800 cases) and flat staffing from 2000 to 2018 show the office is resource-limited, not powerful. Still, its monopoly on felony charging makes it structurally important.
- **Confidence:** High — official county website, RCW statutes, PDC filings, and county budget documents provide strong primary-source grounding.
