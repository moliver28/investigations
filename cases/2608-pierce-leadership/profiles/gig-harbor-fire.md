## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Inclusion rule:** From holding office and decision-making. Gig Harbor Fire & Medic One is a Title 52 state-law fire protection district. That is a Washington law (Revised Code of Washington, or RCW) that allows special districts to tax property owners. The district covers 54 square miles. It runs a multi-million-dollar annual budget. It serves about 53,000 residents with fire and emergency medical services. Those services include Advanced Life Support (ALS) ambulance transport through the Medic One program. It is on this map because it holds a public office (it is the sole fire and ALS provider in its service area) and it makes big decisions (its 5-member Board of Fire Commissioners adopts the budget, sets levies, and approves the Capital Facilities Plan).
- **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 16 is a peer district on the Key Peninsula. West Pierce Fire & Rescue and East Pierce Fire & Rescue are peer districts mapped separately. Tacoma Fire Department serves the City of Tacoma, which is separate. The City of Gig Harbor has a contract with GHFMO for service inside city limits. The District's boundaries extend beyond city limits.

## 1. Who they are
- **Exact legal name:** Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 5. It operates as "Gig Harbor Fire & Medic One" (GHFMO). Headquarters: 10222 Bujacich Rd NW, Gig Harbor, WA.
- **Entity type:** Government — a special-purpose fire protection district under Title 52 RCW. It is a municipal corporation with taxing authority.
- **Governing law:** Title 52 RCW (Fire Protection Districts). The Board of Fire Commissioners is the District's legislative body under RCW 52.14.
- **Formation history:** Formed by voter approval at a special election on January 6, 1945 (762 yes, 26 no). The Pierce County Commissioners established it on March 6, 1945 (Resolutions #1751 and #1827). It started as a volunteer department. The first paid staff were hired in 1973.
- **Board structure:** A 5-member Board of Fire Commissioners. Each member is elected at-large from the District. Each serves a 6-year staggered term, per Title 52 RCW. As of 2024–2026: Kevin Entze (Chair, Position 1), Matthew Fleming (Position 2), Joe Urvina (Vice Chair, Position 3), Phil Hayden (Position 4), Bryce Nelson (Position 5). Former Commissioner Tom Sutich served 17 years and retired before 2025.
- **Executive leadership:** Fire Chief Dennis Doan is the current chief as of 2024–2026.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory authority:** As a fire protection district under RCW 52, GHFMO can levy voter-approved property taxes. It can impose a Fire Benefit Charge (FBC) per RCW 52.18. It can issue general-obligation bonds with voter approval. It can provide fire protection and emergency medical services in its boundaries.
- **Taxing authority:** Property-tax levy and the Fire Benefit Charge are the two main revenue sources. A 2022 voter-approved bond funds the capital program. The Capital Facilities Plan was adopted unanimously by the Board in December 2021.
- **Service-area monopoly:** "Provides fire and life safety services to 53,000 people over 54 square miles" (per the District's own website). It runs 24/7 staffing at 5 stations: Station 51 (6711 Kimball Dr); Station 52 (2217 Pt. Fosdick Dr NW); Station 56 (5210 144th St NW); Station 58 (10302 Bujacich Rd NW); Station 59 (3828 62nd Ave Ct NW). Four more stations are not staffed. The District has 123 uniformed firefighters and paramedics.
- **Budget scale:** A 2022 voter-approved capital bond plus ongoing levy and FBC funding suggests an annual operating budget likely between $30 million and $60 million. This is UNVERIFIED. State Auditor (SAO) audit reports would have the figure.
- **Capital program:** Station 51 (6711 Kimball Drive) closed for demolition and rebuild starting the week of September 8, 2025. The project is expected to take 18 to 24 months. The new station will be a two-story, 20,740-square-foot facility. It is funded by the 2022 voter-approved capital bond.
- **Domain overlaps:**
  - **gov ↔ econ:** The District taxes a 54 square mile area. It issues large capital bonds. The chief has pushed the Washington Survey and Rating Bureau (WSRB) for fair Protection Class ratings, which affect insurance premiums.
  - **gov ↔ inst:** The District runs a Medic One ALS program with the regional Medic One foundation. It contracts with the City of Gig Harbor for service inside city limits.
  - **gov ↔ insurance/finance:** Fire-protection ratings drive commercial and residential insurance premiums. The WSRB Protection Class rating for the Station 57 (Crescent Valley) response area was upgraded on September 8, 2025.

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** Gig Harbor Fire & Medic One is a Title 52 RCW fire protection district (Pierce County FPD No. 5) with a 54 sq mi service area. It serves about 53,000 residents. It has 123 uniformed firefighters. It staffs 5 stations 24/7. A 2022 voter-approved capital bond funds Station 51's rebuild from 2025 to 2027. Its power has two parts. First, it is the sole fire and ALS provider for 54 square miles, including the City of Gig Harbor under contract. Second, it has fiscal power: voter-approved property-tax levy, FBC, and bond capacity. The District also works with the Washington Survey and Rating Bureau (WSRB). Protection Class ratings drive insurance premiums. The District's Chief has pushed WSRB for fair ratings. That makes insurance-rate-setting a quasi-political function the District plays a part in.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** (1) The Fire Benefit Charge is based on property use and square footage, not assessed value. This lines up District revenue with discouraging lower-intensity development. (2) Commissioner races under Title 52 RCW have low turnout, like other fire-district elections. That favors incumbents. (3) The District is the sole provider in its area. There is no competitive pressure on service quality. (4) The District's contract with the City of Gig Harbor creates a dependency. If the contract terms change, the District's city-based revenue is at risk.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** A defense-attorney reading would note that fire districts face many procedural checks. The Open Public Meetings Act requires open meetings. Levies and bonds need voter approval. Board members face public elections. The State Auditor's Office (SAO) audits the books. The WSRB relationship is a normal rating function. The opposing reading would say the District's structure gives it limited accountability on day-to-day decisions. Sole-provider status, FBC calculation, low-turnout elections, and the bond program together limit political checks. The District's September 2025 Station 57 WSRB upgrade and Station 51 rebuild show that the Board and Chief can move major capital and rating projects without much public pushback.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Structural facts (formation, Board, service-area size, capital program, WSRB upgrade) match the District's website, Pierce County ballot records, and the Chamber listing.
