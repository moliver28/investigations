## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why United Way is on the map.** UWPC is the main private fundraiser and regranter in Pierce County. It runs the "From Poverty to Possibilities" summit. It is the local steward of the ALICE study — ALICE stands for Asset Limited, Income Constrained, Employed, a measure of working-poor households. That makes UWPC a hub. It ties together Pierce County government and labor (Lawver). It ties in banks such as Columbia Bank, Bank of America, and Banner. It ties in utilities (Tacoma Public Utilities — Jackie Flowers), transit (Mike Griffus, Pierce Transit), and health (Beth Johnson, Coordinated Care). UWPC touches nearly every tier-1 and tier-2 institution on this map. It also has a strong reputation as the "go-to" local philanthropy.
- **What is NOT on the map.** The Greater Tacoma Community Foundation (gtcf) is a tier-1 partner with overlap but is a separate legal entity. The two groups coordinate but are distinct. United Way Worldwide (the national office) is not on the map. UWPC is an independent licensee, not a subsidiary.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Grant-making:** UWPC raises money in the annual workplace campaign and regrants to about 30–40 partner agencies across human services. It also runs the Center for Strong Families and the From Poverty to Possibilities summit. Individual grants are small ($25K–$250K), but the annual pool and the convening power set the de-facto priorities for the local human-services network.
- **ALICE convening power:** UWPC is the regional steward of the ALICE study. ALICE stands for Asset Limited, Income Constrained, Employed. Ponepinto helped develop it at her prior job. Pierce County government, United Way Worldwide, and state agencies cite ALICE when setting the official "self-sufficiency" income number.
- **Dense board overlap:** The board reads like a who's-who of the Pierce County institutional map. Pierce Transit has a seat. So do Tacoma Public Utilities, Tacoma Public Schools, Coordinated Care, and Washington State Fair. Banks include Columbia Bank, Bank of America, and Northwest Bank. Insurers include Regence BlueShield and State Farm. Labor includes AFSCME-aligned union attorneys. Other groups include Ryder System and Asia Pacific Cultural Center. Outside the Chamber and GTCF, this is the single best cross-institutional networking node in Pierce County.
- **Government contracting:** UWPC partners with the City of Tacoma, Pierce County, and DSHS (the state Department of Social and Health Services). Together they run family-financial-stability programs through the Center for Strong Families. UWPC acts as a fiscal middleman for publicly funded services. That gives it quasi-governmental standing.
- **Cultural and regional voice:** The annual campaign shapes the public conversation about poverty and self-sufficiency. Dona Ponepinto also served as President of the Association of Junior Leagues International, starting in 2022. That extends UWPC's reach nationally.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** UWPC is the most important private cross-institutional board in the county after the Tacoma-Pierce Chamber and GTCF. It works like a standing executive networking chamber for the public, nonprofit, banking, and labor elite. Every director is a bridge to another tier-1 or tier-2 node. Grant dollars are modest (single-digit millions re-granted each year). The real power is the board seats and the convening role.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:**
 - Board members from Tacoma Public Utilities (Flowers), Pierce Transit (Griffus), Coordinated Care (Johnson), and Tacoma Public Schools (Jumper) are quasi-public officials. UWPC's agenda-setting affects their agencies' funding.
 - The CEO's concurrent AJLI presidency is allowed but extends UWPC's reach into a national women's-leadership group.
 - AFL-CIO board chair (Lawver) lines up labor policy with UWPC's "self-sufficiency" framing. Some labor scholars say that framing moves the poverty talk away from wage and labor-law reform.
- **Alternative reading.** UWPC is a normal community-foundation-style operation. The dense board reflects the small size of the Pierce County elite pool. Every executive knows every other executive. Coordinated power is not the only reading. Calling UWPC a "shadow council" is alarmist.
- **Confidence:** High — board composition, CEO, EIN, and 990 pay figures are verified from primary sources (ProPublica + UWPC website + official news).
