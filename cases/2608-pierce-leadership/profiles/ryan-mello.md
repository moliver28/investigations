## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- Ryan Mello is the most powerful elected official in Pierce County, Washington. He is the County Executive, the top job in county government. He runs the county's departments, writes the budget, and can veto almost anything the County Council passes.
- He controls a $3.5 billion two-year budget. That money touches every part of the county: roads, police, courts, parks, and public health.
- The county's other top elected officials (the Sheriff, the Prosecuting Attorney, the Auditor) are mapped separately. The former Executive, Bruce Dammeier, is also mapped but no longer in office.

## 1. Who they are
- **Name:** Ryan N. Mello
- **Born:** about 1978 or 1979, in Kailua, Hawaii
- **Lives in:** Tacoma, Washington
- **Family:** His partner is named Sean. Mello is openly gay and was the first openly gay County Executive in Washington.
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree in politics and government from the University of Puget Sound (2001). He was student-body president there in 2000–2001.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **The office itself.** The County Executive is the chief executive of the county under its home-rule charter. He prepares the budget and runs all the departments.
- **The veto.** He can veto any law the County Council passes. To override him, the Council needs 5 of its 7 votes. With a 4-to-3 Council, a party-line veto is nearly impossible to overturn. This is his strongest power.
- **The budget.** He controls a $3.5 billion two-year budget. About 76% of it goes to law enforcement, courts, and the justice system.
- **Regional transit boards.** He is Vice Chair of Sound Transit (a regional transit agency) and sits on the Pierce Transit board. That gives him a say over billions in transportation spending.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** Ryan Mello holds the most concentrated power in Pierce County government. He writes the budget, runs the departments, and can veto the Council with a nearly unbreakable veto. He earned that power through a twenty-year climb up the county's elected ladder, and he won it narrowly (51–49%) in 2024.
- **The counter-argument.** A defender would say his narrow win means he has no strong mandate, that his veto power is checked by the Council's control of the purse, and that his transit-board seats are advisory.
