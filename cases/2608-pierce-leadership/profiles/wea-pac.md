## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why it is here:** The WEA-PAC is the political action committee (PAC) of the Washington Education Association (WEA), the state's largest teachers' union. It is the main political money arm for K-12 school employees in Olympia. The graph links it to two Pierce County nodes: Pierce County Democrats (weight 3) and Ryan Mello (weight 4). WEA-PAC moves union and Democratic money into Pierce County elections. The WEA claims 6 of its members sit in the state legislature and 55+ more sit on local school boards and in local offices. That is a real candidate pipeline.
- **Who is NOT on this page:** The NEA Fund for Children and Public Education (NEA-FCPE) is the federal PAC for the National Education Association. WEA-PAC works with it but stays a state and local PAC. PIE (Public School Employees of Washington) is a separate PAC for classified school employees. PIE is not on this page either.

## 1. Who they are
- **Full legal name:** Washington Education Association Political Action Committee (WEA-PAC). Registered with Washington's Public Disclosure Commission (PDC). Committee ID: co-2025-20621 (current 2025 registration); also listed as co-2023-20621 in the 2023 cycle.
- **Type:** State political action committee, registered under Washington law RCW 42.17A (the state's campaign-finance law). It is part of the Washington Education Association (WEA), which is the state arm of the National Education Association (NEA).
- **Parent and affiliates:** WEA-PAC is the political arm of WEA. WEA gives WEA-PAC a lot of in-kind support (staff time, office space, supplies). A PDC enforcement case (#139384) found that "WEA-PAC receives significant in-kind contributions from WEA that sustains its operation." NEA-FCPE is the federal PAC; WEA-PAC works with it on candidate recommendations.
- **Laws it follows:** Washington RCW 42.17A (Campaign Disclosure and Contribution). At the federal level, FECA (the Federal Election Campaign Act) and IRS rules for 527 political groups. Contacts: WEA-PAC coordinators Samantha Casne (253-765-7161) and Emily Hansen (206-412-4406); email WEAPAC@washingtonea.org.
- **Members and money in:** WEA members can sign up for monthly payroll-deduction donations: $2.25, $5, or $10.50 per month. They can also add $1 per month for NEA-FCPE.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Endorsing and recruiting candidates (decision-making):** WEA-PAC's stated goal is to "recommend and elect pro-public education, pro-labor candidates to office — regardless of party affiliation." Its track record claim: 6 WEA members in the state legislature and 55+ WEA members on local school boards and in local offices. That pipeline is the PAC's clearest claim to power — it turns endorsements into actual wins.
- **Independent spending and in-kind spending (decision-making + money):** 2023 PDC filings show WEA-PAC spent $968,027.15 in-kind, $250,040.25 in monetary contributions to other PACs, and $20,410.00 in independent expenditures. 2025 totals: $780,778.25 to other PACs and $754,947.72 in-kind. The Washington Observer Substack reports WEA-PAC spent $3.75 million in a recent year (the cycle is UNVERIFIED in this pass).
- **Partisan tilt (well-connected):** A Freedom Foundation analysis says WEA-PAC gave 63% of member contributions to Democratic candidates, totaling nearly $5 million. Another 35% went to support the union itself — likely for member-mobilization work. The "bipartisan" line in WEA's own mission statement is disputed by who actually got the money.
- **Compliance and enforcement history:** PDC enforcement case #139384 (Washington Education Association Political Action Committee) required WEA-PAC to fix its campaign-finance reports. Freedom Foundation says the filings "claimed WEA-PAC received under-the-limit contributions from nearly 27,000 unidentified contributors — six times as many as it reported."
- **Why it scores where it does:** The graph shows WEA-PAC tied to Pierce Dems (weight 3) and Ryan Mello (donated weight 4). WEA-PAC is a top-10 political spender in Washington and outpaces nearly every other PAC in the state.

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** WEA-PAC is a tier-3 political node with structural power. It is the top K-12 labor PAC in Washington. It runs the candidate pipeline for school-board races, state legislative seats, and pro-labor statewide offices. The graph weights understate its size: WEA-PAC's 2023 in-kind spending alone ($968K) is bigger than many entities' full contribution profiles in this dataset. Its 2025 PAC-to-PAC figure ($781K) puts it in the top tier of Washington PACs.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** (a) PDC enforcement case #139384 found that WEA-PAC's pre-2024 reporting broke the rules — "27,000 unidentified contributors" — and the fix came under PDC order. That is a real compliance problem for a group claiming $5M in pro-Democratic giving. (b) WEA's "regardless of party affiliation" line is misleading, since 63% of contributions went to Democrats per Freedom Foundation. The 35% in in-kind spending to WEA itself suggests the PAC also works as a member-mobilization slush fund, not just a candidate-support vehicle. (c) The Pierce Dems ↔ WEA-PAC ↔ Ryan Mello graph links suggest a clear Pierce County Democratic / WEA-PAC / candidate pipeline. It is worth mapping, not necessarily illegal.
- **Other view:** WEA-PAC could also be seen as a defensive union PAC fighting back against corporate school-reform dollars (charter-school advocacy, parental-choice PACs). The Freedom Foundation analysis is partisan. The PDC data behind it is not. The fair read is that WEA-PAC operates at the same scale as a big corporate trade-association PAC, with strong incumbent-protective effects on Washington K-12 policy.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** for spending figures (PDC is primary); **Medium** for partisan-share claim (single secondary source, Freedom Foundation, with admitted perspective bias); **High** for who they are legally and PDC enforcement-case fact pattern.
