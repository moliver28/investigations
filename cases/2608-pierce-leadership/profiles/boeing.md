## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why Boeing is on the map.** Boeing's Frederickson plant is Boeing's only major factory in Pierce County. It has about 1,500 direct jobs. It gives Boeing a seat at the local economic-development table. Boeing is on the map for four reasons. (1) It holds a place in the county: a 500-acre factory in the Frederickson industrial zone. (2) It makes decisions: Boeing runs subcontracting, hiring, and capital spending at the site. (3) It has a strong reputation: Boeing is a global Fortune 100 aerospace company, and its Frederickson plant puts Pierce County on national aerospace supply chains. (4) It is well-connected: Boeing has links to Joint Base Lewis-McChord (JBLM) as an aerospace partner, and seats on the Chamber of Commerce board and the Economic Development Board (EDB).
- **What is NOT on the map.** Boeing corporate (The Boeing Company, based in Arlington VA since 2022, formerly in Chicago) is the parent. It is the controlling entity here but is not a separate node. Other Boeing suppliers in the area (Toray Composites America in Frederickson; AIM Aerospace) are not separate entries. The Machinists Union (IAM District 751, SPEEA) is not on the map. Boeing's Federal Way office and Renton/Everett factories are out of Pierce County.

## 1. Who they are
- **Legal name:** The Boeing Company (operating unit: Boeing Fabrication — Frederickson).
- **Type:** Public industrial corporation (NYSE: BA). Incorporated in Delaware. Aerospace and defense maker.
- **Tax ID (EIN):** 91-0425694 (The Boeing Company). SEC CIK 0000012927. Files 10-K annual reports.
- **Parent and subsidiaries:** Parent is The Boeing Company. Frederickson is an operating site of Boeing Commercial Airplanes (BCA) and Boeing Defense, Space & Security (BDS). It builds skin and spar parts for BCA commercial wing programs. It also builds Apache-related parts for BDS.
- **Who owns it:** Public shareholders. Top holders include Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street, and Capital Group. CEO Kelly Ortberg (since Aug 2024).
- **Laws that apply:** Delaware General Corporation Law. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) Part 21 (aircraft production approval). ITAR (defense articles). Securities Exchange Act.
- **Founded:** Boeing was founded in 1916 in Seattle. The Frederickson plant opened in the 1990s (the plant marked its 20th anniversary in the mid-2010s).
- **Pierce County location:** Frederickson Industrial Park, south of Puyallup near the Joint Base Lewis-McChord (JBLM) military base. Bounded roughly by 176th St E and Frederickson Industrial Park Rd. In unincorporated Pierce County (the FRED 310 zoning case surrounds the plant).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Power held:** Boeing Frederickson holds economic power in Pierce County through four channels. (1) Jobs: about 1,500 direct jobs, making it one of the county's largest private employers (6th-largest in the 2017 EDB list). (2) Land: 500 acres of dedicated aerospace manufacturing. (3) Supply chain: the plant builds wing skins, stringers, and tail surfaces for the 747, 757, 767, and 777. That makes it a single point of failure inside Boeing's production system. (4) Capital spending: the FRED 310 zoning case proposes 310 acres of adjacent industrial development, expanding Boeing's landholdings.
- **Economic overlap:** Boeing's 66,800 employees in Washington state (Dec 2023, Seattle Metro Chamber data) make it the state's largest private employer. Its Pierce County site is a key part of the regional aerospace cluster.
- **Government contracts:** Boeing Frederickson builds Apache helicopter parts for BDS under federal contracts with the US Army and international customers (UK, India, Saudi Arabia, and others). Boeing's $25 billion in losses since 2019 (per AP) put cash-burn pressure on the company, including the Frederickson site.
- **Jobs controlled:** About 1,500 employees in Frederickson (2017 EDB count). It was 1,575 in 2017, down from 1,750 in 2016. The October 2024 layoff announcement hit 17,000 Boeing employees company-wide. By November 2024, 2,199 Washington state Boeing workers had received layoff notices (per ESD filings). The exact Frederickson layoff count is UNVERIFIED.
- **Land and real estate:** 500-acre factory in Frederickson Industrial Park. The Pierce County FRED 310 zoning case (2021) proposed two adjacent parcels of about 310 acres for industrial, warehouse, and office development for lease. The site is south of 176th St E and east of Frederickson Industrial Park Rd, around the current Boeing plant.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** Boeing's Frederickson plant is Pierce County's only major Boeing industrial site. It is a 500-acre aerospace factory with about 1,500 workers (down from 1,750 in 2016 to 1,575 in 2017, and likely lower after the 2024 layoffs). The plant's power base is its aerospace supply-chain role. Boeing Frederickson builds wing skins, stringers, and tail surfaces for the 737, 747, 757, 767, and 777. It also builds Apache helicopter parts for defense. That single-point-of-failure role gives Frederickson outsized weight inside Boeing's production system. The plant's economic weight in Pierce County far exceeds what the tier3 graph rank suggests. Boeing's Pierce County workforce is concentrated here, and the plant's fortunes after the 2024 layoffs will shape the county's economic path.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags.** Boeing Frederickson creates a structural conflict between private corporate interests and public Pierce County interests. Boeing's October 2024 layoff announcement (a 10% cut, 17,000 jobs company-wide) imposed real costs on Washington workers and on Pierce County's economic base. The 2024 IAM 751 strike and its 38% pay-raise deal shifted labor costs to Boeing. Boeing is now recouping those costs through layoffs. The 2024 FRED 310 zoning case proposed 310 acres of adjacent industrial development for lease. That suggests Boeing is planning for future growth, but it also creates land-use tension with neighbors. The defense reading: Boeing is a private company making market-driven choices. The 38% pay raise reflected workers' leverage. Layoffs reflect Boeing's $25B+ losses. The FRED 310 expansion would create jobs.
- **Two readings.** The opposing reading is that Boeing Frederickson's power is overstated. Boeing's $25B+ losses, FAA production caps on the 737 MAX, the 2024 IAM strike, and the deferred 777X program all threaten the plant's future. Under this view, Boeing Frederickson is on a downward arc and its about 1,500 jobs could shrink fast. The other reading: the 777X tail-fabrication work (announced 2014) and Apache helicopter production (runs through 2028) provide a stable floor. The FRED 310 zoning case shows Boeing is planning for future capacity. The 2025–2026 path is unclear, but the plant's role inside Boeing's production system gives it institutional protection.
