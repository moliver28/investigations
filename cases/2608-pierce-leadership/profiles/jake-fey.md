## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why he is here:** Jake Fey is a state House member for the 27th Legislative District. He took office on January 14, 2013. He is the Chair of the House Transportation Committee. That is one of the most powerful seats in the state for transportation money, and that money shapes Pierce County roads and buses. He led the $16.9 billion Move Ahead Washington package through the legislature.
- **Who is NOT on this page:** State Senator Emily Randall (D-26) chairs the Senate Transportation Committee. She is on a different page. Fey's former staff at the WSU Energy Program are not on this page. His wife Janine Fey is a private citizen, not a public figure.

## 1. Who they are
- **Full name:** Jacob Carl Fey
- **Goes by:** Jake Fey
- **Born:** 1949, in Port Angeles, Washington (per Wikipedia, citing official records)
- **Home:** Tacoma, WA. He has lived there for more than 30 years. Mailing address: P.O. Box 1372, Tacoma, WA 98401.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Elected office:** Washington State House of Representatives, 27th Legislative District, Position 2 (Democrat). He took office January 14, 2013, replacing Jeannie Darneille. He serves with Laurie Jinkins, the Speaker of the House. He has won re-election every time through 2024.
- **Committee chair:** He chairs the House Transportation Committee. He sets the agenda for state transportation money and major transportation bills in the House. He also sits on the House Environment and Energy Committee.
- **Legislative win:** He led the Move Ahead Washington package — $16.9 billion over 16 years, with big new spending on transit, bike and pedestrian projects, and road repairs. Governor Inslee signed it in March 2022. Some reports round the figure to "nearly $17 billion."
- **Where his work overlaps:**
  - **Government and economy:** His transportation choices shape roads, bridges, and jobs across the state, including in Pierce County.
  - **Government and institutions:** Former director of the WSU Energy Program (academic); former board member of Sound Transit and Pierce Transit (regional transit).
  - **Government and politics:** Democrat. He calls himself a lifelong progressive. He is endorsed by House Democrats.
