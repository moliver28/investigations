## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** Fortunato's power comes from his long tenure as State Senator for the 31st District and his wide committee portfolio (Law and Justice, Transportation, State Government/Elections, Business/Economic Development). He does well at the district level (consistently 55 to 60% in his district) but does not win statewide races. He lost the Insurance Commissioner race by 13 points and placed 6th in the gubernatorial primary. His business, Eco-3, sits at the meeting point of his private commercial work and his legislative role overseeing construction, environmental rules, and economic development.
- **Conflicts of interest:** Eco-3, Fortunato's erosion control company, works in a field directly regulated by the state he helps govern. His seats on the Business, Trade and Economic Development committee and the Transportation committee overlap with his business in construction-related environmental compliance. His CESCL training certification from the Washington State Department of Ecology is a state-recognized business relationship. His NRA and gun club memberships signal an ideological match that may shape his work on the Law and Justice committee.
- **The other view:** Fortunato is a back-bench minority party senator with limited real power. The Republican caucus holds only 19 of 49 Senate seats. His repeated statewide runs (Governor, Insurance Commissioner) have failed. His influence is confined to his conservative district. His wide committee list may reflect a lack of seniority-based specialization rather than strong influence. His company is a small enterprise, not a major contractor.
- **Confidence:** High — many primary sources (the WA Legislature, the PDC, the WA Department of Ecology, the Senate Republican Caucus, and certified WA Secretary of State results) confirm the key claims. The SRCC bio note that he was "elected to the House but appointed to the Senate before sworn in" is a small biographical wrinkle, but it stays consistent across sources.
