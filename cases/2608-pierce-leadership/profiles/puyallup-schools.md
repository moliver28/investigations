## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Why it is on the map — from holding office + decision-making:** Puyallup School District (PSD) is on the map for two reasons. (1) From holding office: it is the 6th-largest K-12 district in Washington by enrollment and the second-largest non-Tacoma district in Pierce County. It serves about 145,500 residents across central and east Pierce County (Puyallup, South Hill, Summit, Midland, Edgewood). (2) Decision-making: five elected board members set policy, levy taxes (with voter approval), control about $430 million in annual operating and capital budgets, negotiate the teachers' union contract (PEA — Puyallup Education Association), and decide on school closures and boundary changes that ripple through the South Sound housing market.

Sumner-Bonney Lake and Tacoma districts are excluded as separate jurisdictions. The Cascade/Bethel/Puyallup "inter-district transfer" market is referenced here but does not have its own entity.

## 1. Who they are
- **Exact legal name:** Puyallup School District No. 3, Pierce County, Washington (per Pierce County Assessor filings).
- **Type of entity:** A school district. It is a municipal corporation organized under state law (RCW) 28A.315 (school districts) and Title 28A (Common School Provisions). It is not a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. It is a quasi-municipal taxing body that can levy taxes with voter approval (RCW 84.52.053 EP&O levy + RCW 84.52.135 capital levy / bond authority).
- **Governance:** A 5-member Board of Directors. Members serve four-year staggered terms. They are elected at-large within the district. Current board (per PSD website): Gretchen Miles (President, Pos. #3, 2023-2027), Shannon Burch (Vice President, Pos. #5, 2023-2027), David Berg (Legislative Rep, Pos. #4, 2021-2029), Joseph Romero (Legislative Rep, Pos. #2, 2019-2027), Dr. Melissa Bedford (WIAA Rep, Pos. #1, 2025-2029).
- **Superintendent transition:** On March 4, 2026, the Board picked Dr. Kevin Alfano as the next superintendent. He starts July 1, 2026. He succeeds Richard Lasso. His prior roles are not confirmed beyond the PSD announcement.
- **Age:** One of Washington's oldest continuously operating school districts. Organized in the 19th century.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory authority:** The district sets K-12 curriculum, enrollment rules, transportation, capital construction, and labor contracts within its boundaries. It controls employee headcount (the largest employer in central/east Pierce County with about 4,100 staff).
- **Tax power:** The 2025 local property tax rate was $4.21 per $1,000 of assessed value (Bond $1.05 + Capital Levy $0.86 + EP&O Levy $2.30). Voters in the district approved these rates. A 2026 EP&O Replacement Levy is on the ballot. The state provides about 57% of revenue ($245.3M in apportionment + $78.5M in categorical funds in 2024-25). Local levies ($66.7M) and federal/state grants provide the marginal dollar.
- **Labor power:** The district is the largest employer in the Puyallup-area labor market. It sets wages for teachers in the PEA bargaining unit. Those decisions ripple into Bethel, Sumner-Bonney Lake, and Franklin Pierce districts during regional talks.
- **Land-use power:** School siting decisions drive residential development. PSD has capital-levy authority for new schools. The 2024 Capital Levy ($4.14 per $1,000 AV) funds the Rogers HS gym, Edgemont track, Carson playground, and other facility work.
- **Budget stress:** Per the PSD Budget Information page, the FY2024-25 ending fund balance was a negative $7.8M unassigned. The FY2025-26 forecast projects an ending balance of about –$1.7M. The district is operating below its minimum fund-balance policy without taking action. The 2026 EP&O Replacement Levy is the immediate lever.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line:** Puyallup School District operates like a $430M quasi-municipal corporation. Five elected board members govern about 23,110 students across 37 schools with about 4,100 staff. Its main powers are local property-tax levy placement (capital + EP&O), labor negotiation (PEA), capital construction, and school siting. Each one ripples into Pierce County housing prices, regional labor compensation, and inter-district boundary politics. The superintendent transition from Richard Lasso to Dr. Kevin Alfano (effective July 1, 2026) is the most visible current decision moment. It comes against a backdrop of a documented structural budget deficit (about –$7.8M unassigned ending balance for FY2024-25) that the 2026 EP&O Replacement Levy addresses but does not solve.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** Capital-construction contracts flow through to regional construction firms during a Capital Levy cycle (Cornerstone, Tucci & Sons, etc. — UNVERIFIED at firm level). The Board approves those contracts without a public referendum. The graph note of "boundary_tension" with Bethel School District is a known South Sound planning fault-line — overlapping residential growth (Edgewood/Midland) and inter-district transfer applications. Board members' employer ties are not in available primary sources (UNVERIFIED).
- **Analysis of alternatives:** The defense reading: PSD is a creature of state law (RCW 28A). It is accountable to voters through at-large board elections and levy ballot measures. Its power is limited by OSPI, state apportionment formulas, federal IDEA, and collective bargaining law. The skeptical reading: an at-large district-wide board election in a suburban district is unlikely to produce competitive or ideologically diverse boards. The structural deficit has been rolled forward without meaningful labor-cost concession, suggesting board-management alignment against taxpayer exposure (levy fatigue) rather than against entrenched labor interests. Either way, the next 24 months — Alfano's first year plus a 2026 levy — will be diagnostic.
