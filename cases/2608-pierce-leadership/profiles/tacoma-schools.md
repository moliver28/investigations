## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Inclusion rule:** Tacoma Public Schools (TPS) is on this map because it holds office and makes decisions. It is the largest school district in Pierce County. It has 28,272 students across 69 schools (2023-24). It runs a large budget (with a $30M shortfall for 2025-26) and hires hundreds of staff. Its elected board sets education policy for tens of thousands of families. The superintendent, Josh Garcia, is one of the highest-paid public officials in Tacoma at $342,038 (per OpenPayrolls). The district's school-bond link with Pierce County Council (graph edge weight 4) and its partnership with MultiCare Health (weight 3) spread its reach into county finance and healthcare.
- **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** Puyallup School District is the second-largest in the county and is a separate entity. Franklin Pierce Schools is a separate district (linked through Lance Goodpaster on the Greater Tacoma Community Foundation board). Bethel School District is separate. Clover Park School District is separate (linked through Alice Phillips). These districts are outside the boundary at the current tier.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory authority:** As a public school district, TPS can levy taxes (with voter-approved bonds and levies), run schools, hire and fire staff, set curriculum, and manage facilities. The board approves budgets and sets policy.
- **Budget and fiscal power:** TPS is facing a $30 million shortfall for the 2025-26 school year, which means "major position eliminations" (source #4). A $10 million shortfall is projected for 2026-27 (source #4). The district's budget process has public hearings and board votes, giving the board real fiscal power.
- **Enrollment and scale:** 28,272 students across 69 schools (2023-24). Minority enrollment 70%. About 43.1% of students are economically disadvantaged (source #6). That makes TPS the largest educational institution in Pierce County.
- **Superintendent power:** Superintendent Josh Garcia runs day-to-day operations. His salary is about $342,038 (source #7). That makes him one of the highest-paid public officials in the region. His three-year contract started at $292,750 when he was first hired in 2012 (source #8).
- **Domain overlaps:** Institutional (education), political (elected board, bond elections), economic (major employer, budget authority), health (MultiCare partnership).

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** Tacoma Public Schools is a major institutional power in Pierce County. It educates 28,272 students across 69 schools. It has a large budget and many employees. The elected 5-member board sets policy and approves budgets. Superintendent Josh Garcia (salary about $342K) runs operations. The $30M budget shortfall for 2025-26 creates real pressure: the board must decide which positions and programs to cut. Those choices affect thousands of staff and students. TPS's power comes mostly from holding office (legal authority over education) and being a major employer and budget holder.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** The MultiCare Health partnership (graph edge weight 3) could create conflicts if health-program choices favor one provider. Board members' other ties (UNVERIFIED for most) could create conflicts with budget choices. The shortfall context adds scrutiny to all financial decisions. Shawna Freeman Lane was appointed (not elected) to Position 2 in March 2026, which may raise questions about democratic input on budget-cut decisions before her election.

The $30M shortfall shows that the district's freedom is limited by enrollment drops and state funding levels. The board mainly chooses which cuts to make, not whether to grow. Still, the district sets the agenda for how cuts are spread and which programs are kept.
