## 3. Where their power comes from
- **What power does he hold?**
  1. **Tribal elected office.** On the Puyallup Tribal Council since June 11, 2026 (three-year term). He also served on the Council from 2014 to 2020. The Puyallup Tribe is the largest federally recognized tribe in Pierce County. It owns major land in Tacoma and runs the Emerald Queen Casino. It has strong say over Tacoma-Pierce County policy.
  2. **State executive-branch authority.** Director of GOIA, appointed by Governor-elect Bob Ferguson, effective January 2025. GOIA is the state's main link to Washington's 29 federally recognized tribes. The Director runs the Centennial Accord process (a state-tribal meeting agreement).
  3. **Municipal and civil-rights leadership.** Former Vice Chair of the Washington State Criminal Justice Training Commission. Former Vice Chair of the Washington Coalition for Police Accountability. Co-Chair of De-Escalate WA, which led the winning 2018 Initiative 940 campaign on police training. Served on the Governor's Task Force on Independent Investigations of Police Use of Force (2019) and the 2016 Joint Legislative Task Force on Use of Deadly Force in Community Policing.
  4. **Legal and HR expertise.** Over 22 years as a tribal attorney, Executive Director of HR / Training / Education, and People Operations Director / Compliance Officer at the Puyallup Tribal Health Authority.
- **Key facts:**
  - Tax status: he serves in tribal, state, and (formerly) municipal roles. None carry private tax implications for him personally.
  - Board overlaps: Vice Chair of the CJTC (Criminal Justice Training Commission). Secretary of the Purpose Dignity Action (PDA) Board. Former Vice Chair of the Washington Coalition for Police Accountability.

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** Reynon is a key figure who links tribal, state, and city government. He sits on the Puyallup Tribal Council (elected, June 2026) and runs the Governor's Office of Indian Affairs (appointed, January 2025). That gives him a rare "inside both rooms" view on tribal-state talks. His 22-year tribal career, plus Seattle city experience, plus his long work on police reform, make him one of the most well-connected Native leaders in the region.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** (a) **Dual role:** serving on the Tribal Council while running the state's tribal-liaison office is unusual. Counter — GOIA Directors are usually tribal members. The role is liaison, not representative of any one tribe. The State Ethics Act and tribal sovereignty together allow this, but it should be disclosed. (b) Vice Chair of CJTC while running GOIA — both touch criminal-justice policy. (c) His long tenure at many levels creates networks of mutual obligation that are hard to map from public sources.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** The opposing reading is that GOIA is a small office with a limited budget, so the Director's power is mostly symbolic. That is true by budget, but understates his convening and agenda-setting power: the GOIA Director runs the Centennial Accord and is the Governor's main tribal contact, which is real agenda power.
