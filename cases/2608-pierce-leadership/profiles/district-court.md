## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- The Pierce County District Court handles most criminal and civil cases in the county. It is the county's only "limited jurisdiction" court. That means it handles smaller cases, not big felonies.
- The Pierce County Council sets its budget. The graph shows this as an edge where the Council gives the court its funding.
- It is the front door for most state-court cases. Skipping parking tickets, about seven out of every eight cases filed in all state courts land at this level.

## 1. Who they are
- **Exact legal name:** Pierce County District Court.
- **Entity type:** A Washington state court of limited jurisdiction. It is a government body, not a private company. It is created and governed under state law, Title 3 of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW).
- **Governing law:** state law (RCW) 3.50 covers district courts in large counties. RCW 3.62 covers judges. RCW 3.66 covers civil cases. Pierce County is a "class A" county, meaning its population tops 500,000.
- **Parent or subsidiaries:** It runs inside Pierce County government. It has no separate corporate status. The court administrator is Misty Butler Robison. Location: County-City Building, 2nd floor, 930 Tacoma Ave S, Room 239, Tacoma, WA 98402. Phone: (253) 798-7487.
- **Officers:** Court Administrator Misty Butler Robison. The presiding judge rotates among the elected judges.
- **Departments and functions:** Criminal cases (misdemeanors, gross misdemeanors, DUIs, reckless driving, and some assaults). Civil cases up to $100,000. Small claims up to $5,000, or $10,000 for a person. Traffic tickets. Domestic-violence and no-contact orders. Name changes. Some lien foreclosures. First hearings in felony cases. It also runs a probation department, therapeutic courts, a court resource center, and court services.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Its caseload.** It handles the large majority of state-court cases in Pierce County. Every misdemeanor, traffic ticket, and most civil disputes up to $100,000 flow through it.
- **Its power to punish.** It can order up to 90 days in jail and a $1,000 fine for a misdemeanor. For a gross misdemeanor, up to one year and a $5,000 fine. It decides pretrial detention and supervises probation.
- **Protection orders.** It issues domestic-violence, antiharassment, and no-contact orders. These carry real limits on personal freedom.
- **Felony gatekeeping.** First hearings in felony cases happen here. Judges decide if there is enough evidence to send a case up to Superior Court.
- **Trials.** Six-person juries hear criminal cases. Small claims have no lawyers unless the court allows one. Traffic tickets have no jury.
- **How it is scored.** Its legal power overlaps with government (the County Council funds it) and law enforcement (prosecutors and defense lawyers work in it). Small claims is where everyday residents meet the court for consumer, landlord, and neighbor disputes.
- **Key questions answered:**
 - **Area of authority:** All of Pierce County.
 - **Judges' discretion:** Judges have wide sentencing discretion within the legal caps.
 - **How judges are picked:** Elected in nonpartisan races for 4-year terms. The top two from the primary advance to the general.
 - **Caseload:** Statewide, more than two million cases are filed each year. About seven of every eight state-court cases (not counting parking tickets) are district-court level.
 - **Judge pay:** Not confirmed. The County Council sets the budget.

## 1. Who they are
- **The bottom line.** This court's power is broad and routine, not concentrated. It is the front door for most state-court cases in Pierce County. Its eight elected judges hold real power to jail people, fine them, and issue protection orders. It depends on the County Council for its budget. That limits its independence on money and staff, but not on its rulings.
- **The defense view.** The court is an independent branch of government. Judges are elected by voters, not appointed by the Council or the Executive. The Council cannot cut a sitting judge's salary (state law, RCW 3.58). Eight judges and nonpartisan races limit any one seat from being captured. Small claims and protection orders are built to help people without lawyers and victims.
- **The other way to read it.** It is a routine service-delivery body. Its judges are professional, its docket predictable, and its budget is a small slice of county spending. Its power is capped by law. People can appeal to Superior Court. Its composite power score of 0.023 reflects this: it is on the map, but its influence on the big power structures is low.
- **Confidence:** Medium — jurisdiction, structure, judge roster, and how judges are picked are all primary-sourced. The caseload figures are statewide, and the Pierce-specific caseload is not confirmed.
