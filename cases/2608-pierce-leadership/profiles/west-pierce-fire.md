## 1. Who they are
- **Legal name:** West Pierce Fire & Rescue (WPFR). Legally, Pierce County Fire District No. 3, established in 1944.
- **Entity type:** Government — a junior taxing special-purpose fire protection district under Title 52 RCW, set up as a municipal corporation under RCW 41.24.010.
- **Formation history.** Lakewood Fire (District No. 2) and University Place Fire (District No. 3) merged on March 1, 2011 to form West Pierce Fire & Rescue. The original District No. 3 had been around since 1944. The merged district kept that legal identity.
- **Service area:** Lakewood and University Place (full service). Town of Steilacoom (contracted). 31 square miles. Just over 100,000 people.
- **Board of Fire Commissioners:** Five commissioners, elected at-large, six-year terms. As of 2026: John Clancy (Position 2, Board Chair, 2022–2027); Scott Casebolt (Position 3, 2024–2029); Dave Durr (Position 4, Vice Chair, 2022–2027); Marshal (Scott) Dellinger (Position 5, 2026–2031); Alan Billingsley (Position 1, 2026–2031).
- **Executive leadership:** Fire Chief Ryan McGrady. Deputy Chiefs Mike Boltz and Hallie McCurdy.
- **WSRB rating:** Class 3 from the Washington Survey and Rating Bureau (WSRB) as of the 2019 re-rate. (Class ratings go from 1 (best) to 10; lower numbers mean better fire protection and can lower insurance costs.) The Town of Steilacoom is also Class 3 because WPFR serves it.

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Statutory authority.** As a fire district under RCW Title 52, WPFR can levy voter-approved property taxes (Regular Fire Levy, EMS Levy), issue general obligation bonds with voter approval, and contract for fire-protection services. An elected Board of Fire Commissioners governs the district under RCW 52.14.
- **Taxing authority (FY2024 budget revenue):** Regular Levy $24.4 million. EMS Levy $8.1 million. M&O (Maintenance and Operations) Levy $17.0 million. GEMT Funds $4.3 million. Fire Protection Contracts $1.66 million. Plus intergovernmental revenue, transport fees ($2.5 million), and reserves. Total revenue: $72.8 million.
- **Budget control (FY2024):** Total appropriated expense: $63.8 million. Operations divisions: Suppression $29.0 million; EMS $12.0 million; Administration $4.5 million; Training/EMS Training $2.1 million; Community Risk Reduction $2.6 million; Logistics/Facilities $1.8 million; Fleet $1.75 million; IT $2.2 million; SS911 $0.65 million. Plus capital projects ($1.83 million), infrastructure ($1.75 million), grants ($0.76 million), debt service ($1.4 million), and reserve deposits ($0.2 million). Beginning fund balance $10.9 million. Ending balance $9.0 million.
- **Operational scope:** Six fire stations staffed 24 hours a day. About 17,721 incidents a year (2022 figure). Services: fire suppression, EMS and transport, technical rescue, hazardous materials response, marine operations, fire prevention, inspections and code enforcement, and fire and life-safety education.
- **Contracted service relationships:** WPFR has service contracts with DSHS (Western State Hospital), Clover Park Technical College, Pierce College, Pierce County (Chambers Creek Properties, Lakewood Community Center, Steilacoom Ferry Landing), Pierce Transit, Lakewood Water District, Clover Park School District, University Place School District, and the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (American Lake facility, outside the district).
- **Domain overlaps:**
  - **Government ↔ Economic:** Taxing power over property. Large purchasing power for trucks and supplies. Contracts with school districts, hospitals, and transit.
  - **Government ↔ Institutional:** Interlocal agreements (formal cooperation contracts) with peer fire districts through the Pierce County Fire Training Consortium.
  - **Government ↔ Social:** Emergency response and life-safety service to about 100,000 residents. Mutual-aid agreements with neighbors.

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** West Pierce Fire & Rescue controls a $72.8M revenue / $63.8M expense fire district that delivers critical life-safety services to ~100,000+ residents across Lakewood and University Place. Its power is fundamentally fiscal and operational: it levies property taxes, contracts for service, and dispatches fire/EMS resources. The Board of 5 elected commissioners holds the legislative authority; the Fire Chief (Ryan McGrady) executes operations. WPFR's market position is structurally protected by geographic monopoly (the district's boundaries are exclusive for fire-EMS service), but constrained by the 1% property-tax cap (Washington's I-747/Levy Lid), voter approval requirements for levy lid lifts, and WSRB ratings oversight.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** The Board's authority over the district's budget and contracts creates standard procurement-conflict potential. Some commissioners have business interests connected to construction (Dave Durr — painting contractor; Bart Dalton — financial advising at Edward Jones; John Sheeran — defense attorney; John Clancy — former Cardinal Health/manpower sales) — these are common in junior-taxing districts but should be disclosed. The 2011 merger consolidated two districts and increased market concentration, which reduces competitive pressure on service delivery costs. FY2024 includes a planned non-voter-approved bond issuance (~$16.5M, 20-year life) which would commit future tax revenue without explicit voter sign-off — UNVERIFIED whether voter approval was secured or whether it proceeds under existing statutory authority.
- **Analysis of alternatives :** An opposing reading: WPFR is one of several Pierce County fire districts, and its power is bounded by geographic scope and the political limits of property-tax-funded services. The Class 3 WSRB rating is not the best achievable (Class 1 is top) — improving it would require additional capital investment. Service contracts with peer entities (Western State Hospital, school districts) reduce direct tax pressure but create dependency. The merger with University Place eliminated a competing fire department and likely reduced service-delivery competition, but voter approval in both jurisdictions was required for the merger.
- **Confidence:** High — primary budget PDF (West Pierce official, FY2024) and the official Elected Officials page corroborate board composition, financial scale, and operational scope. Some biographical details (commissioner career paths from LinkedIn) are secondary.
