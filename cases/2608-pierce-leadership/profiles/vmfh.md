## 0. Why this person or group is on the map
- **Inclusion rule:** Virginia Mason Franciscan Health (VMFH) is on the map for both office-holding and economic reasons. It is one of the two major health systems in Pierce County (along with MultiCare). It runs St. Joseph Medical Center in Tacoma — the largest hospital on the Tacoma skyline. VMFH has 18,000+ employees, about 5,000 doctors, 10 hospitals, and about 1,500 beds across western Washington. It is a major employer and care provider. Its board overlaps with Weyerhaeuser, Wellfound (a joint venture with MultiCare), the Washington State Hospital Association (WSHA), and the Catholic Archdiocese. Its parent, CommonSpirit Health, is one of the largest nonprofit health systems in the U.S.
- **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** CommonSpirit Health (`commonspirit`) is a separate node in the graph. CHI Franciscan and Franciscan Health System are predecessor entities (excluded as historical). Virginia Mason Health System is also a predecessor. The Benaroya Research Institute is a VMFH subsidiary (excluded as the research arm). Bailey-Boushay House is a VMFH facility (excluded as a care site, not a governance node).

## 3. Where their power comes from
- **Market power:** VMFH is one of the two dominant health systems in Pierce County (alongside MultiCare). It runs 10 hospitals with about 1,500 beds, 18,000+ staff, about 5,000 doctors, and nearly 300 care sites across western Washington. In Pierce County it runs St. Joseph Medical Center (Tacoma), St. Clare Hospital (Lakewood), St. Anthony Hospital (Gig Harbor), and Wellfound Behavioral Health Hospital (Tacoma, JV). [Source #1] FACT.
- **Competitive position:** VMFH is MultiCare's main competitor in Pierce County. The two systems jointly run Wellfound Behavioral Health Hospital — a zone of cooperation in a competitive market. [Source #1, graph]
- **Religious and moral authority:** As a Catholic-affiliated system, VMFH follows the Ethical and Religious Directives for Catholic Health Care Services. That gives it power over moral and ethical health policy — including rules on reproductive health and end-of-life care. This is a power that secular MultiCare does not have. [Source #1]
- **Corporate scale:** Parent CommonSpirit Health is one of the largest nonprofit health systems in the U.S. (formed from the merger of Catholic Health Initiatives and Dignity Health). VMFH can tap national resources, buying power, and political advocacy through CommonSpirit. [Source #5, #6]

## 1. Who they are
- **Verdict:** Virginia Mason Franciscan Health runs the second-largest hospital network in Pierce County. It operates St. Joseph Medical Center (Tacoma's flagship), St. Clare (Lakewood), St. Anthony (Gig Harbor), and the Wellfound behavioral health JV. Its power comes from four sources: (1) market position — 10 hospitals, about 1,500 beds, 18,000+ employees, about 5,000 doctors, and about 300 care sites across western WA; (2) corporate parentage — CommonSpirit Health, one of the largest U.S. nonprofit health systems, gives VMFH national resources and buying power; (3) religious power — Catholic ethical directives give VMFH moral authority over health policy that secular MultiCare lacks, especially on reproductive health restrictions; (4) board overlaps with Weyerhaeuser (corporate), Catholic Archdiocese (religious), WSHA (industry), and through the Wellfound JV, MultiCare (competitor). VMFH reached its current size through the January 2021 merger of CHI Franciscan ($2.45B pre-merger revenue) and Virginia Mason ($1.15B), creating an about-$3.6B regional powerhouse under CommonSpirit. CEO Ketul Patel's departure for Wellstar Health System (Oct 2025) creates a leadership transition worth watching.
- **Conflicts-of-interest flags (defense counter-argument):** A defense would say: (1) VMFH is a nonprofit providing essential health care, including $304M in community benefits, so its tax exemption is justified; (2) the CommonSpirit tie gives resources that help patients, not corporate extraction; (3) the Catholic ethical directives are constitutionally protected religious exercise, not policy imposition; (4) the Wellfound JV with MultiCare is a cooperative arrangement that expands behavioral health access, not anti-competitive collusion; (5) the 3-person board is standard for a subsidiary of a larger system, with CommonSpirit providing oversight.
- **Analysis of alternatives:** The opposing reading is that VMFH is a form of unaccountable consolidated health care power — a $3.6B+ regional system run by a 3-person board, ultimately controlled by a national parent (CommonSpirit) with no direct public accountability, despite receiving hundreds of millions in tax exemptions. The 2021 merger reduced competition in the western WA hospital market. It may have raised prices and reduced patient choice. The Catholic ethical directives restrict access to legal reproductive health services in a state (WA) that protects those rights. That creates a gap between state policy and patient access at VMFH facilities. The Weyerhaeuser board tie connects VMFH to the corporate timber power structure. The Wellfound JV with MultiCare is a cooperative bridge between the two main competitors. It could enable coordination rather than competition. The CEO's departure for Wellstar raises questions about leadership stability and whether CommonSpirit's commitment to the PNW region is strategic or short-term.
