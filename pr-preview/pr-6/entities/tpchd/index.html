## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why TPCHD is on the map:** TPCHD is the sole local public health agency for Pierce County (about 952,600 people as of 2024). State law (RCW 70.05) gives it power to protect health. It does this through disease prevention, food safety, septic permits, and family health services. Its $102.9M two-year budget and about 284 full-time staff (FTE) give it real muscle. The Board of Health — made up of Pierce County Council members, Tacoma City Council members, the County Executive, and others — sets policy for the whole county. - **Why others near it are NOT on the map:** The Washington State Department of Health is a separate state-level agency. Franciscan Health System / Virginia Mason Franciscan Health (a hospital system) is mapped separately. The Perinatal Collaborative of Pierce County (a TPCHD-affiliated 501c3) is a sub-entity. The Pierce County Medical Society (which appoints one Board of Health member) is mapped separately. ## 1. Who they are - **Legal name:** Tacoma-Pierce County Health Department (TPCHD) - **Type:** Independent government agency (local health jurisdiction) - **Founded:** 1972 (per Wikipedia). Created by an interlocal agreement between Pierce County and the City of Tacoma. - **Governing law:** Revised Code of Washington (RCW) 70.05 (local health departments); RCW 70.05.070 (powers and duties of local health officers); RCW 70.28.031; interlocal agreement between Pierce County and the City of Tacoma. - **Tax ID (EIN):** Government entity. Pierce County acts as the fiduciary agent. - **Parent/subsidiaries:** No parent. Run by the Tacoma-Pierce County Board of Health, an independent policy body. Pierce County is the fiduciary agent. - **Accreditation:** One of about 400 local health departments in the U.S. that meet Public Health Accreditation Board (PHAB) standards. - **Headquarters:** 3629 South D Street, Tacoma, WA 98418-6813. Also has a Key Peninsula office at 9013 Key Peninsula Hwy N, Lakewood, WA 98349 (open Thursdays 11am-2pm). ## 3. Where their power comes from - **Statutory authority:** RCW 70.05 gives TPCHD power over food safety (restaurant inspections, food worker cards), drinking water safety, septic system permits, disease control (TB, HIV/AIDS, whooping cough), environmental health, and vital records (birth and death certificates). The Health Officer (Dr. James S. Miller) has powers under RCW 70.05.070 and RCW 70.28.031. - **Budget:** 2026-2027 two-year budget: $102,899,820 (later amended to about $108.7M per the April 2026 Board agenda). That is 3.2% of the total Pierce County budget. Funding sources: Federal 9.1%, State 30.4%, Pierce County 12.8%, City of Tacoma 2.5%, Permits & fees 39%, Private/local/misc 6.2%. The budget fell 19% from the 2024-25 amended budget of $127.6M. The drop came from the end of federal COVID money. - **Regulatory authority:** TPCHD enforces food safety codes, septic rules (including a new do-it-yourself septic inspection program), environmental health codes, and disease control rules. It inspects restaurants, issues food worker cards, and regulates on-site sewage systems. - **Area covered:** All of Pierce County (about 952,600 people in 2024). This includes cities like Tacoma, Puyallup, Lakewood, Gig Harbor, and University Place, plus unincorporated areas. - **Staffing:** 284 FTE in 2026. Down from 349 in 2025 and 355 in 2024. The drop reflects post-COVID budget cuts. Four divisions: Disease Prevention & Management, Environmental Health, Strengthening Families, Administrative Services. - **Domain overlap:** Government (health rules, statutory authority), Social (public health services, family support, substance abuse prevention), Economic (food safety permits, septic permits that affect property values). ## 3. The financial and working ties between TPCHD and PCPC need more clarity. ## 1. Who they are - **The bottom line.** TPCHD controls public health rules for all of Pierce County (about 952K residents). It does this through state law (RCW 70.05), a $102.9M+ two-year budget, and about 284 staff. Its power runs through the Board of Health (which includes the County Executive, three County Council members, and Tacoma City Council members) and through the Director of Public Health. The department's reach covers food safety, septic systems, drinking water, disease control, and environmental health. It touches daily life in ways most people do not notice. The 19% budget cut and 20% staff drop since 2024 come from the end of COVID money and have cut capacity. - **Conflicts to watch (defense counter-argument expected):** The Board of Health's refusal to release the full probe report on Director Reed's workplace conduct raises an accountability concern. A defense attorney could say the Board protected staff privacy and that coaching was a fair fix. The counter: the lack of detail hurts public trust, especially while staff cuts continue. - **The other side:** TPCHD is a low-power agency tied to its funders. Only 12.8% of its money comes from the county. Most comes from state and federal pass-throughs. Board members are part-time appointees with other day jobs. Its rule-making power is bounded by state law and its budget. Budget cuts may shrink TPCHD's real power, even though the law still gives it authority. - **Confidence:** High — the department's legal structure, budget, staffing, governance, and leadership are extensively documented in primary sources (TPCHD website, Board of Health packets, Pierce County budget documents). The workplace investigation is well-covered by secondary sources (KNKX, News Tribune) but the full report remains undisclosed.
