Known facts
Robert Watson Ferguson, born February 23, 1965; 24th Governor of Washington, assumed office January 13, 2025 (source 1, 2, 3). Served as 18th WA Attorney General (2013–2025); King County Council member (2004–2013) (source 1, 2). Elected Nov 5, 2024: defeated Dave Reichert (R) 55.5% to 44.3% statewide; won Pierce County 50.79% to 48.96% (source 4, 5). Appointed Doris Walkins to Pierce County Superior Court (Feb 25, 2026), filling vacancy from Judge Kirkendoll's retirement (source 6, 7). Appointed Colleen Melody and Theo Angelis to WA Supreme Court (source 8, 9). Budget priorities: public safety, housing, ferries, affordability; proposed $4B in cuts and $1.3B in new programs (source 10, 11).

## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why he's on the map:** Bob Ferguson is the Governor of Washington State. He runs the state's executive branch. He picks judges for every court in the state, including Pierce County Superior Court. He also writes the state budget plan. - **Direct Pierce County action:** In February 2026, he chose Doris Walkins to fill a Pierce County Superior Court seat. That was a direct use of his power over a Pierce County court. - **Pierce County vote:** He won Pierce County in the 2024 governor's race with 50.79% of the vote. It was close — only about 8,000 votes separated him from Republican Dave Reichert. - **Who is NOT on the map:** Lt. Gov. Where their power comes from - **The office.** Ferguson is the 24th Governor of Washington. He took office on January 13, 2025. His term ends January 8, 2029. As governor, he runs state agencies, writes the budget plan, picks judges, signs laws, and sets the legislative agenda (source 1, 2, 3, 8). - **The budget.** He proposed a 2026 supplemental budget with $4 billion in cuts and $1.3 billion in new programs. His priorities are housing, public safety, ferries, affordability, and education (source 10, 11). The 2025–27 two-year state budget added $9 billion or more in new or higher taxes to close a shortfall (source 11). - **Judicial appointments.** He picks the judges for every state court seat. That includes the Washington Supreme Court, the Court of Appeals, and every Superior Court (source 8). He has chosen two WA Supreme Court justices (Colleen Melody and Theo Angelis) and several Superior Court judges, including Doris Walkins in Pierce County (source 6, 8, 9). He can also strike individual budget items with a line-item veto. - **Executive orders.** He signs executive orders to direct state policy. Recent ones include EO 26-02 (the Governor's Economic Development Council, June 25, 2026), EO 26-01 (workplace rules for menopause, June 1, 2026), a license plate reader law (SB 6002, March 2026), wildfire emergency orders (August 2026), and a Medicaid executive order (September 2026) (source 13, 14, 15, 16). - **Where his power reaches:** All 39 counties in Washington, including Pierce County. ## 3. He writes the state's budget plan. He signs executive orders. He sets the agenda for his party. He won the 2024 race statewide and narrowly won Pierce County too.
