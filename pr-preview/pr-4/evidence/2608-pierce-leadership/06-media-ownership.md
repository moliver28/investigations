### McClatchy Ownership Chain

McClatchy is owned by **Chatham Asset Management**, a New Jersey-based hedge fund. Key facts:

- **2020:** McClatchy filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The U.S. Bankruptcy Court approved a sale to Chatham Asset Management for ~$312 million via a credit bid of first-lien debt plus $49 million in new cash. Chatham had already been McClatchy's largest creditor.
- **Chatham Asset Management** is a hedge fund that also owns a 66% stake in Canadian publisher **Postmedia** (National Post, Vancouver Sun, etc.).
- **December 2024:** Chatham merged its magazine publisher a360media (formerly American Media, Inc. — the *National Enquirer*, *Men's Journal*, etc.) into McClatchy, creating **McClatchy Media** with a new Lifestyle & Entertainment division.
- **Key people:** Tony Hunter (President & CEO), Kevin McClatchy (Chairman).
- **Corporate HQ:** 26 Main Street, Chatham Borough, NJ 07928.

### News Tribune History of Ownership

- Locally owned by the **Baker family** for 73 years (from 1912 to 1986)
- Purchased by McClatchy in 1986 for ~$112 million
- In 1986, Viacom bought the paper's TV/radio assets (KTNT stations) simultaneously
- The News Tribune is one of 30 daily newspapers McClatchy operates across 14 states
- McClatchy dropped Associated Press wire service in March 2024 (cost-cutting under hedge-fund ownership)

## 6. Notable Consolidation Patterns

### A. Hedge-Fund Ownership of Newspapers

The single most important consolidation pattern for Pierce County: **The News Tribune is owned by Chatham Asset Management, a hedge fund.**

- Chatham also owns **a360media** (the former *National Enquirer*/*Globe* tabloid publisher)
- Chatham owns 66% of **Postmedia**, Canada's largest newspaper chain
- Under Chatham, McClatchy dropped the AP wire (March 2024), consolidated newsrooms, and merged with a360media (Dec 2024)
- Stephanie Pedersen serves as editor of **four** McClatchy papers simultaneously (News Tribune, Olympian, Bellingham Herald, Tri-City Herald) — a clear sign of newsroom consolidation
- **May 2026:** News Tribune journalists went on a one-day strike over contract negotiations with McClatchy

### B. Radio Market Concentration

Three ownership groups control the vast majority of commercial radio in the market:

- **iHeartMedia** — dominant with 8+ stations (acquired KZOK/KJAQ/KPTR as divestitures from Entercom/CBS merger)
- **Audacy** (formerly Entercom) — controls 5 major FM stations after CBS Radio merger in 2017
- **Lotus Communications** — acquired Sinclair's radio cluster in 2021, bringing the last locally-relevant Sinclair radio holdings under a larger group

Each merger (Entercom/CBS, iHeart's station reacquisitions, Lotus buying Sinclair's Seattle cluster) reduced the number of independent radio voices.

### C. Television Market Concentration

**Zero locally owned TV stations** serve the Seattle-Tacoma market:

| Owner | Stations | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nexstar (via Tegna) | KING 5, KONG 16 | Nation's largest TV station owner (200+ stations) |
| Sinclair Broadcast Group | KOMO 4, KUNS 51 | 163 stations — attempted massive Tribune merger (blocked) |
| Cox Media Group (Apollo PE) | KIRO 7 | Private-equity backed (Apollo Global Management) |
| Fox Corporation | KCPQ 13, KZJO 22 | O&O (owned-and-operated by the network) |
| CBS/Paramount Global | KSTW 11 | O&O |

The Nexstar-Tegna merger (2025-2026) further consolidated the market: KING and KONG are now under Nexstar, the nation's largest station group.

### D. Cross-Ownership Historical Note

There was a time when a single Tacoma company — Tribune Publishing Company (the Baker family's News Tribune) — owned the daily newspaper, the KTNT-TV station (now KSTW), and KTNT radio. The 1986 sale split these assets: McClatchy got the paper, Viacom got the broadcast stations. This kind of cross-ownership is now virtually impossible under FCC rules and market conditions.

### E. Independent Media Resilience

Despite consolidation, some independent outlets remain:
- **Tacoma Weekly** remains independently owned and published (Matt Nagle)
- **KNKX** was saved from a UW takeover in 2016 and operates as a community-owned nonprofit
- **Substack newsletters** (Tacoma Stories, Washington Observer, VarsityWA) represent a growing independent journalism ecosystem, though none have the newsroom capacity of the legacy outlets
- **South Sound Business** and **South Sound Magazine** remain locally owned by Premier Media Group

## Summary: Pierce County Media Ownership at a Glance

| Medium | Outlet | Owner | Ownership Type |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Fox TV** | KCPQ 13 / KZJO 22 | Fox Corporation | Publicly traded (NY) |
| **CBS Ind.** | KSTW 11 | CBS / Paramount Global | Publicly traded (NY) |
| **Radio #1** | iHeart cluster (KJR, KZOK, etc.) | iHeartMedia | Publicly traded (TX) |
| **Radio #2** | Audacy cluster (KISW, KHTP, etc.) | Audacy, Inc. | Publicly traded (PA) |
| **Radio #3** | Lotus cluster (KOMO, KPLZ, KVI) | Lotus Communications | Private (LA, family-owned) |
| **Public radio** | KNKX 88.5 | Pacific Public Media | Community nonprofit |
| **Political news** | The Washington Observer | Paul Queary (Substack) | Independent |
| **Crime/community** | Tacoma Stories | Jack Cameron (Substack) | Independent |

**Bottom line: The News Tribune, Tacoma's primary source of local news, is owned by an out-of-state hedge fund that also controls Canada's largest newspaper chain and a tabloid publisher. All major broadcast stations are owned by national conglomerates or private equity. The only genuinely local ownership is in the independent weekly (Tacoma Weekly), the business magazine (Premier Media Group), and a handful of Substack newsletters.**
