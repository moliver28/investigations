## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - ILWU Local 23 is the Tacoma chapter of the International Longshore and Warehouse Union. It loads and unloads every commercial ship at the Port of Tacoma and the Northwest Seaport Alliance. - Local 23 is on the map because of three things. (1) It controls the supply of waterfront labor. Without ILWU workers, no ship moves at the Port of Tacoma. (2) It makes decisions about wages, work rules, and now AI and automation at port terminals. (3) It is well-connected: it has a graph edge to the Port of Tacoma as an employer, it is part of the PCCLC (the Pierce County Central Labor Council), and it has donated to state Senator Jani Hitchen. - Not on the map separately: ILWU International (the San Francisco parent union), the Inland Boatmen's Union, and other unions without direct port-shutdown power. ## 1. Who they are - **Full name:** International Longshore and Warehouse Union, Local 23 (ILWU Local 23). - **Type:** A local labor union. It is chartered by ILWU International. It operates under federal labor law (the National Labor Relations Act, or NLRA). - **Parent:** ILWU International in San Francisco. Local 23 is part of the Coast Longshore Division (CLD). - **Founded:** 1886 as the Stevedores, Longshoremen and Riggers Union of Puget Sound. Local 23 did not join ILWU until 1958. - **Headquarters:** 1306 Alexander Avenue East, Fife, WA 98424. The hiring hall is there. Phone: 253-272-6600. The street out front is named "Willie Adams Way" after a former ILWU International President who was once a Local 23 member. - **Governing law:** The NLRA. The Pacific Coast Longshore and Clerks' contract (called the PCL or PCL&C) covers all West Coast longshore workers. ## 3. Where their power comes from - **Labor monopoly:** Local 23 has the only crew to load and unload ships at the Port of Tacoma. Every container, bulk, and break-bulk vessel uses ILWU labor dispatched from the Fife hiring hall. - **Power to stop commerce:** Local 23 can shut down cargo operations at the Port of Tacoma. The Port is one of the largest container ports on the U.S. West Coast by TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit, the standard measure for shipping containers) volume. - **Power to block automation:** In 2024 and 2025, Local 23 fought Husky Terminal's plan to use AI for marine-clerk work. The union's resistance showed it can veto new technology at port terminals. - **Jobs controlled:** About 1,500 longshore and marine-clerk members (per the 2017 EDB Major Employers List). - **Political action:** ILWU endorses and donates to South Sound candidates. The PCCLC (Pierce County Central Labor Council) coordinates labor endorsements. Local 23 supports Democratic candidates. - **PAC and contracts:** None received. Revenue comes from member dues and dispatch fees. - **Property:** Local 23 owns the union hall at 1306 Alexander Ave E, Fife. The Tacoma Longshoremen Credit Union is also at the same address. ## 4. Bottom line - **The bottom line.** ILWU Local 23 controls the supply of longshore labor at the Port of Tacoma. With only about 1,500 members, it holds outsized power in Pierce County. Without Local 23, the Port cannot move cargo. - **Two main power effects.** (1) Labor relations: Local 23 sets the pace of automation at port terminals (the 2024 Husky Terminal fight showed the union is willing to veto AI deployment). (2) Political influence: ILWU households, donations, and endorsements feed into the PCCLC, which coordinates Pierce County Democratic politics. - **The counter-view.** The PCL contract expires on a coastwise cycle. Automation could erode longshore jobs over time. The 2024 Husky Terminal fight may be a defensive action. - **Why it still favors Local 23.** The 2024-25 PCL contract preserved the union's jurisdiction and added wage gains. Local 23's Ryan Whitman now sits on the ILWU International leadership as VP-Mainland. The current balance of power favors Local 23. - **Confidence:** Medium-High.
