## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - Puget Sound Energy (PSE) is the largest power and gas company in Washington. It is on this map because it serves Pierce County and most of the Puget Sound region. It holds power through three things: a state-granted monopoly, the rates it charges, and the lawmakers it talks to. - It has over 1.24 million electric customers and 881,000 gas customers across 8 or more counties. About 425,000 of those live in Pierce County. - PSE's profits go to five big foreign pension funds. The state Utilities and Transportation Commission (UTC) sets its rates.

## 1. Who they are - **Full legal name:** Puget Sound Energy, Inc. - **Type of company:** A private power and gas company. It is the main operating arm of Puget Energy, a Washington holding company. - **the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) filing:** CIK 81100; File No. 1-4393. PSE files 10-K reports with the SEC even though it is privately held. - **Parent company:** Puget Energy owns PSE. All operations run through PSE. - **Who owns Puget Energy:** Five foreign pension funds. They are: AIMCo (Alberta Investment Management Corporation, 13.6%), BCI (British Columbia Investment Management Corporation, 20.9%), OMERS (Ontario municipal employees pension, 23.9%), PGGM (Dutch pension fund, 10%), and CPPIB (Canada Pension Plan Investment Board, with a continuing stake). Macquarie Infrastructure Partners sold its 44% stake in 2018. - **Who regulates them:** The Washington Utilities and Transportation Commission (UTC) under state law RCW 80. PSE is a state-licensed monopoly. - **Founded:** 1997. A merger of Puget Sound Power & Light Company and Washington Energy Company. - **Headquarters:** Bellevue, Washington.

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Statutory monopoly:** The state lets PSE be the only gas and electric utility in most of its 6,000 square mile area. About 1.2 million electric customers and 900,000 gas customers must buy from PSE if they want gas or electric service in its territory. - **Big revenue and assets:** PSE made $4.86 billion in revenue in 2024. It had $754 million in operating income in 2025 and $602 million in 2024. Electric utility revenue was $3.89 billion in 2025 and $3.33 billion in 2024. - **Workforce:** PSE has 3,257 employees. It is a Fortune 1000 company, ranked #710. - **Power plants and lines:** PSE owns or controls 6,524 megawatts of power capacity. About 3,339 megawatts of that is PSE-owned. It has a 25% stake in the Colstrip Generating Station in Montana, which is about 700 megawatts. - **No government grants:** PSE does not get government grants. Its rates are set by the UTC. PSE must follow state laws like the Clean Energy Transformation Act (CETA).

## 5. How they touch government - **Rate cases:** PSE files rate cases with the UTC. On Feb 15, 2024, PSE filed a case asking for 6.7% electric and 19.0% gas hikes for 2025, and 9.3% electric and 2.1% gas hikes for 2026. A 2026 case (UTC Docket 260005) asks for 29% electric and 20% gas hikes over three years. The state Attorney General says the hikes are too high. - **Lobbyists:** PSE has five registered lobbyists in 2025. They lobby at the state capitol on energy bills. - **Campaign donations:** No data found in this research. Would need a search of state PDC records. - **No government contracts:** PSE does not get grants. It earns money by charging customers. The UTC approves the rates. - **Land-use cases:** PSE needs local permits for big projects. The Energize Eastside power line (started 2020) was controversial and needed local approval.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** PSE runs the largest power and gas utility in Washington. It is a state-licensed monopoly that serves 1.2 million electric and 900,000 gas customers in 8 or more counties, including Pierce County. Its power comes from its state-granted monopoly status, its control of 6,524 megawatts of power capacity, its 6,000 square mile service area, and its active role in state rate cases. The company is privately owned by five foreign pension funds: AIMCo, BCI, OMERS, PGGM, and CPPIB. That means profits leave the country. Critics point to this when PSE asks for rate hikes. PSE had $4.86 billion in revenue in 2024 and is a Fortune 1000 company. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** PSE is a for-profit monopoly regulated by the state. That sets up a built-in tension. The company wants higher returns for its foreign pension fund owners. The UTC is supposed to protect customers. The state Attorney General is actively fighting PSE's 29% electric and 20% gas hike request. Also, PSE lobbied for a coal-transition law while owning 25% of the Colstrip coal plant. That creates a gap between what PSE says about clean energy and where its money is. - **Analysis of alternatives :** The opposing view: PSE's power is smaller than it looks. A state agency (the UTC) sets its rates, not PSE. Rate hikes reflect real costs like wildfire prevention and clean energy. However, PSE has 5 registered lobbyists in 2025 and won a coal-transition law. That shows real political pull. - **Confidence:** High.
