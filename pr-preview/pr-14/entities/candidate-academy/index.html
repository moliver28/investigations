## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - The Candidate Academy is a candidate-training program run by the Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber. The Chamber is the region's main business advocacy group. Its tax ID number (EIN) is 91-0434830 (Source 3). The Academy meets the bar for being well-connected. It bridges the business world and the electoral world by training future candidates. It also meets the bar for from holding office. It is a formal program of the Chamber, a gatekeeper in Pierce County politics. The graph shows a weight-5 edge from the Academy to the Pierce County Council. That marks its pipeline function. - A few related Chamber programs sit outside this map. The Business Leadership Academy is a separate 10-week program, founded in 1978 (Source 5). It is not directly political, so it is excluded. Spaceworks Tacoma is a Chamber arts and business incubator. It is excluded. The Pierce County Business Accelerator is for BIPOC businesses. It is excluded.

## 1. Who they are - **Exact legal name:** The Candidate Academy is not a separate legal entity. It is a program of the Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce. The Chamber's legal name is "Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce." Its EIN is 91-0434830 (Source 3). - **Entity type:** The Chamber is a 501(c)(6) business league. It is a chamber of commerce (Sources 3 and 4, ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer: "Business leagues, chambers of commerce, real estate boards, etc., created for the improvement of business conditions"). Donations to the Chamber are not tax-deductible (Source 4). - **EIN and registration:** 91-0434830 (Sources 3 and 4). Year formed: 1884 (Source 3, CauseIQ). The tax exemption was issued before 2012. ProPublica has filings from 2012 onward (Source 4). The fiscal year ends in September (Source 3). - **Parent and subsidiaries:** The parent is the Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce. The Chamber also runs a separate foundation: "Tacoma Pierce County Chamber of Commerce Foundation" (EIN 20-4962532, 501(c)(3), tax exemption issued August 2006, Source 6). The Candidate Academy is a program, not a subsidiary. - **Ownership chain:** The Chamber is an independent nonprofit. It is run by a board of directors. The board has 31 voting members. All 31 are independent voting members per the most recent 990 (Source 3). - **Governing law:** Washington state nonprofit corporation law. State law (RCW) 24.03. The federal tax agency (IRS) 501(c)(6) status.

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Power held:** The Candidate Academy holds cultural power and power from connections. It is a candidate-recruitment and training pipeline. It shapes who runs for local office. It does not hold legal or regulatory power. It does not have veto power. Its power is indirect. By teaching campaign operations, fundraising, and media messaging, it shapes who enters local politics. The program helps people "determine whether to throw their hat in the ring or how to otherwise get engaged" (Source 1). - **Basis for power-domain overlap (political domain):** The program covers Civics 101, campaign strategy and operations, election filing, fundraising and event planning, media and messaging, and campaign management. Local political leaders teach these topics (Source 1). This puts the Chamber in the role of gatekeeper for business-aligned candidates. - **Key facts:** - **Who is funded and endorsed?** The Candidate Academy does not directly fund or endorse candidates. It trains them. The Chamber's separate Voters Guide handles endorsements (Source 2). It is unverified whether Academy graduates get Chamber endorsements at higher rates. - **PAC spending?** UNVERIFIED. No Chamber PAC was found in this research. The Chamber's 501(c)(6) status allows political advocacy, but not direct candidate donations (Source 4). - **Party machinery control?** The program is nonpartisan in framing. It is open to "civic-minded individuals." But the Chamber's business focus means the training likely leans toward pro-business candidates. Inference: the program acts as a soft recruiting channel for business-aligned candidates. This is not stated openly. - **Candidate output?** UNVERIFIED. The Chamber does not publish a list of Academy graduates. It does not track how many graduates run or win. The 47th Business Leadership Academy graduated "more than twenty participants" in 2025 (Source 5). No similar number is available for the Candidate Academy.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** The Candidate Academy is a candidate-training program run by the Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce. It does not directly govern, regulate, or donate to candidates. Its power comes from connections and culture. It shapes who enters local politics. It gives campaign skills and access to elected officials to future candidates. The program's 1-day format and lack of public graduate tracking limit its measurable impact. But its role as a gatekeeper for business-aligned candidates is structurally important. The Chamber also publishes a Voters Guide (Source 2) and runs the Academy. That creates a path from training to endorsement, though the Academy itself does not endorse. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** (1) The Chamber trains candidates through the Academy. It may then endorse them through its Voters Guide. That creates a path from training to endorsement that could favor Academy graduates. (2) The program is run by a 501(c)(6) business league. So training likely leans toward pro-business candidates, even if the program is nonpartisan. Defense: (1) No evidence shows that Academy graduates get special endorsement treatment. The Voters Guide and Academy are separate functions. (2) The program is open to all "civic-minded individuals" (Source 1). It covers neutral topics like civics and election filing. It does not cover ideology. No evidence of partisan filtering in participant selection was found. - **Analysis of alternatives:** The other reading is that the Candidate Academy is a small educational program with limited reach. It runs for one day. It does not publish a graduate list. It has no measurable impact on who gets elected in Pierce County. Under this view, the weight-5 `trains_candidates` edge overstates the program's real influence. But the program's structural position matters. It is run by the region's main business advocacy group. It features sitting elected officials as speakers. It covers the full campaign toolkit. That gives it more agenda-setting power than its length suggests. The lack of outcome tracking is also telling. If the Academy did not work, the Chamber would publish its success numbers. - **Confidence:** Medium. The program's existence, curriculum, and Chamber parentage are confirmed by primary sources (Chamber website, reliability 0.9). However, the program's actual impact — how many graduates ran for office, won, or received Chamber endorsements — is UNVERIFIED due to absence of published outcome data. The power assessment is structural/inferential rather than empirically demonstrated.
