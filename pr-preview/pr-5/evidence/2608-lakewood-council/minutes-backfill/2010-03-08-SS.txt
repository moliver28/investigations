Councilmember Moss asked if coordination has been made with the Joint Base Lewis-McChord for fire services. Councilmember Neary

Chair McCurley provided an overview of the University Place and Lakewood fire districts merger. He then called upon University Place
Fire Chief Sagers who reviewed the options the Regionalization Organization Committee studied.

City Council Study Session Minutes

-2-

March 8, 2010

Fire Chief Ken Sharp then reviewed the timelines and process for the merger calling for a special election in February or April 2011 for
Lakewood citizens to vote on the merger. He then reviewed the enhancements that would be realized from the merger which includes
a savings of $860,000 in taxpayer taxes.

Discussion ensued on how existing fire bonds would be impacted with the merger; what will be the status of service to Dupont as a
result of the merger; would the merger have any changes in the fire ratings for home insurance premiums; how would fire marshal
services be impacted with the City and what are the differences with another contract for services; what is the cost of the election and
will the Fire District be paying for the election; how does the election of Commissioners work with the merger; will there be a Lakewood
citizens campaign committee for the merger; what is the average savings per household (approximately 14 cents per $1,000 assessed
value); how many calls for service does each District respond to (10,000 for Lakewood, 3,500 for University Place); is Arrowhead and
Partridge Glen area included in the merger (Yes); and what was the sentiment of the University Place public meetings.

Chair McCurley indicated that the overall sentiment was positive.

Assistant Chief Jim Sharp reviewed a proposal to consolidate Public Safety Answering Points in Lakewood and Tacoma to handle all 9-1-1
calls in Pierce County by co-locating their communications facilities by January 2013 with a long term goal to be fully consolidated by
January 2015.

Discussion ensued on what Lakewood can do to co-locate the communication facilities in Lakewood; describing the sales tax increase of
1/10 of one percent sales tax to fund 9-1-1 communications; and is there a proportionate structure between police and fire 9-1-1 calls.

**********

Speaking before the Council was:

Dennis Haugen, Lakewood resident, spoke about the lowering of the quality of service and attitude when an organization gets too large.

**********

Mayor Richardson asked what the Fire District provided in the maintenance of fire hydrants. Chief Sharp indicated that the Fire District
does minimal fire hydrant maintenance. Caps on the fire hydrants are checked and any needed repairs are reported to the Lakewood
Water District.

BRIEFING BY T HE CIT Y MANAGER

Review of proposed increase of membership on t he Landmarks
and Herit age Advisory Board.

City Manager Neiditz provided a summary on the proposed increase of membership on the Landmarks and Heritage Advisory Board
(LHAB).

Councilmember Neary commented on the LHAB’s desire to increase the number of members from seven to nine. He then spoke about
removing the requirement for term limits.

Discussion ensued on meeting the requirement for the mix of professionals serving on the LHAB; and if there could be concerns about
meeting the requirements for a quorum by increasing the membership. Staff was requested to research if the emeritus position was a
voting or non-voting member; possibly making it non-voting or increasing the Board by one instead of two positions.

Council requested that this item be brought back for further consideration in two weeks.
