## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why EDB is on the map:** The Economic Development Board for Tacoma-Pierce County (EDB) is the main regional economic development group for Pierce County. It meets two tests. First, it holds office-like power: it is the named economic development body and staffs the Economic Development Corporation of Pierce County (EDCPC) by contract (source #2). Second, it is well-connected: its board includes major regional employers — MultiCare, Port of Tacoma, Columbia Bank, Virginia Mason Franciscan Health (VMFH), TOTE Maritime, Puget Sound Energy — plus elected officials and other leaders. That mix creates board overlaps across sectors. Graph edges to the County Council, Boeing, Amazon, and the Chamber show it bridges economic, government, and advocacy domains. - **Why others near it are NOT on the map:** WorkForce Central (Katie Condit sits on the EDB board, source #3 — a workforce development partner, excluded). Pierce County Business Accelerator (a Chamber program for BIPOC businesses — excluded). The City of Tacoma Economic Development Department (Hyun Kim sits on the EDB board, source #3 — a government economic development function, excluded).

## 1. Who they are - **The bottom line.** The Economic Development Board for Tacoma-Pierce County runs a convening platform. It brings together 40+ institutional leaders from Pierce County's economic, government, health, education, and labor sectors. EDB does not hold statutory authority, taxing power, or regulatory power. Its power is well-connected and convening — it brokers ties between businesses, government, and institutions to create jobs and capital ($1.6B and 25,000 jobs claimed over 20+ years, source #2). The board roster is EDB's strongest asset. It includes the two biggest health systems (MultiCare, VMFH), the Port of Tacoma, TPU, PSE, two banks, the Chamber, the labor council, UW Tacoma, and elected officials. That makes EDB a structural hub of Pierce County's power network, even though its formal authority is small. The 2024 leadership change from Bruce Kendall (25-year CEO) to Michael Catsi (source #8) is a generational shift. - **Conflicts to watch (defense counter-argument expected):** (1) The EDB board includes direct competitors (MultiCare and VMFH are the two largest health systems; Key Bank and Columbia Bank compete). Their shared board seat could lead to quiet coordination on markets. (2) Elected officials (Dave Morell, Ryan Mello, Anders Ibsen) sit on the EDB board while EDB pushes for economic development policies — a revolving door between government and advocacy. (3) The FY2023 audit found "material weakness in internal controls" and "significant deficiency in internal controls" (source #4), which raises governance questions. **Defense counter:** (1) Rival firms on a business league board is normal for chambers and EDBs; antitrust rules are usually handled through board policies. (2) Elected officials on EDB boards is a common model, giving policy alignment rather than conflict. (3) The audit findings are about internal controls, not fraud or theft — they are technical issues common in small nonprofits. - **The other side:** EDB is a dues-funded trade group with limited real power. Its $2.9M revenue (source #4) is modest. It employs only 7 people (source #7). Its claimed impact ($1.6B, 25,000 jobs) is self-reported and unproven. Under this reading, EDB is a networking group whose power is reputational, not structural. But the density of its board overlaps (40+ members across every major sector) gives it convening power beyond its budget. EDB's role in big wins (U.S. Open at Chambers Bay, state highway money) shows real policy influence, even if the group itself is small. - **Confidence:** Medium. However, the self-reported impact metrics ($1.6B, 25,000 jobs) are not independently verified, the audit findings (source #4) are not fully explored, and the specific government contracts/grants are UNVERIFIED. The power assessment relies on board composition analysis (structural inference) rather than documented policy outcomes.
