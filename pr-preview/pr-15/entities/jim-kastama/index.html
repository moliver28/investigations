He is also the current Mayor of Puyallup. He held a state Senate seat for 12 years before that. ## 1. Who they are - **Full legal name:** James Matthew Kastama. - **Born:** October 5, 1959, in Bellingham, Washington (Wikipedia, Ballotpedia, and the City of Puyallup bio all agree). - **Lives in:** Puyallup, WA. He owns the family home in downtown Puyallup (Source #1). - **Family:** His wife is Barbara. They have five children: Isaac, Anna Laura, Michael, Rachel, and Sarah (Source #5). They also have one grandchild. - **Schooling:** Graduated from Puyallup High School in 1978. Then Claremont McKenna College in California. His courses include Organizational Assessment, Strategic Planning, and "Managing in a Political and Legislative Environment." He is also an Examiner for the Washington State Quality Awards program.

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Power from his current job:** He is the Puyallup City Councilmember for District 1, serving from 2026 to 2029. The Puyallup City Council writes the city budget, votes on land-use and zoning, awards contracts, and confirms mayoral picks. He has served since January 1, 2018. He won a third term in November 2025. He is also the current Mayor of Puyallup (Source #2; Source #3). Under Puyallup's weak-mayor charter, the mayor seat rotates each year among council members. So the mayoral gavel is procedural, not extra power. - **Power from his old job:** Washington State Senator for the 25th LD, January 8, 2001 to January 14, 2013. He chaired the Senate Economic Development, Trade & Innovation Committee. He also sat on Higher Education & Workforce Development, Transportation, and Ways & Means (Source #5). He served as Assistant Democratic Whip and as House Assistant Democratic Leader from 1999 to 2000. - **Where his power overlaps:** His old Senate role on Economic Development touches the same auto-dealer and real-estate donors who back his local races. His current council vote affects those same dealers' land-use and sign permits on River Road. - **How much power he has now:** As of 2026, he is one of seven Puyallup council votes plus the rotating mayoral gavel. Real power, but not unilateral.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** Jim Kastama is a long-time, low-drama local officeholder. His power is procedural, not transformational. He holds one of seven Puyallup council votes. He also holds the annually rotating mayoral gavel. Before that, he held a 12-year Senate seat on Economic Development and Ways & Means. That gave him real power over state economic-development policy and city land-use. - **Why he matters to this investigation:** He is a node inside the 25th LD donor and endorsement network. That network links Puyallup auto dealers (Toyota of Puyallup gave $1,000 to Kelly Chambers in 2020) to Pierce County Republican power structures (Dammeier then Chambers held the same seat). - **How he got here:** Won state House in 1996. Won state Senate in 2000. Retired in 2012. Came back to City Council in 2017. Re-elected in 2021 and 2025. - **Conflicts of interest:** As a sitting Councilmember and Mayor who "owns and operates his own business," he could face a Puyallup Municipal Code chapter 2.60 conflict if that business contracts with the city. The type of business is not public. Defense: he has filed PDC reports every cycle. No complaints show up on his PDC candidate page. - **The other reading:** The opposing view is that Kastama is a benign insider. He is a moderate Democrat (the Seattle Times called him a "political moderate" in its 2012 endorsement page). His cross-aisle work and decade of bipartisan Senate experience make him a coalition-builder, not a power-broker. That fits his self-description as a Washington State Quality Awards examiner and a community-college teacher. - **Confidence:** Medium. Direct identity facts are fully confirmed across the City of Puyallup, Wikipedia, Ballotpedia, and the WA Legislature Pictorial Guide (3+ independent primary sources). But the "well-connected" claims tying him to the auto-dealer donor pool come from graph adjacency, not direct receipts of those contributions.
