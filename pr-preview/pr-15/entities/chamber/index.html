## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - The Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce runs the county's top business group. It shapes local politics in three main ways. - First, it runs the Candidate Academy. This program trains people who want to run for office. The Chamber does not say who the graduates are. - Second, it issues voter guides. These tell members which candidates and ballot measures to back. - Third, its board seats are filled by leaders of the county's biggest employers. They include MultiCare, the Port of Tacoma, Boeing, Puget Sound Energy (PSE), and Banner Bank. These board ties give the Chamber a direct line into policy fights. - The Chamber is a 501(c)(6) business league. The IRS gives this tax status to groups that work to improve business conditions. Its address is 950 Pacific Ave, Suite 300, Tacoma, WA 98402. Its phone number is (253) 627-2175. Its EIN (Employer Identification Number, the IRS tax ID) is 91-0434830. - **Related but separate groups (not part of the Chamber):** The Economic Development Board for Tacoma-Pierce County (EDB) is a separate partner. The Manufacturing Industrial Council for the South Sound is a Chamber program, not its own group. The Chamber Foundation (EIN 20-4962532, revenue $76K) is a separate nonprofit that supports the Chamber.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** The Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce holds power in three ways. First, the Candidate Academy trains and may pick future candidates who back business. Second, board ties link the county's biggest employers (MultiCare, Port, PSE, Boeing) and growth-machine developers (Rush, Miles, Southport, Tucci, Lincoln Park, Nall) into one advocacy network. Third, the Chamber lobbies directly and issues voter guides. Its $3.08 million in revenue is small next to the billions run by its board members' groups. The Chamber's value is as a coordinator, not as a money mover. Falling revenue (down 22% over two years) and four straight years of audit findings (FY2022–FY2025, including two "material noncompliance" findings) point to stress that could weaken it. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags (the defense would make these points):** - *Candidate Academy secrecy:* The Chamber will not say who the graduates are. That looks like a shadow political machine. A defense would say it is normal nonprofit privacy. But pairing candidate training with voter guides and board overlaps creates a clear conflict between the Chamber's "civic education" story and its political work. - *Developer board ties:* Developers sit on the board (Rush, Miles, Southport, Tucci, Lincoln Park, Nall). They have land-use cases before the County Council. That creates a direct conflict between the Chamber's advocacy role and its members' money interests. - *Audit findings:* Four straight fiscal years (FY2022–FY2025) carry audit findings — "material noncompliance" in FY2022 and FY2024; internal-control deficiencies in FY2023 and FY2025. The audits' presence suggests the Chamber handled $750K+ in federal grant money in those years (inference from the single-audit requirement; confirm from the audit reports). This raises money-oversight questions if the Chamber handles any public funds. - *501(c)(6) versus political activity:* As a business league, the Chamber can do some political work but cannot give money to candidates directly. The line between "civic education" (voter guides, candidate forums) and election work is thin. - **Analysis of alternatives:** The other reading is that the Chamber is in decline and its power is overstated. Revenue is falling. Reserves are dropping. The CEO has changed twice in three years. Audit findings point to governance problems. The "Chamber candidate" label may be a relic of past power, not a sign of current power. Under this view, real economic power sits with the corporations on the board (MultiCare, the Port, PSE, Boeing), not with the Chamber as a coordinator. The Candidate Academy may produce few real elected officials. The voter guide may barely move votes. The Chamber's rank of 2 out of 163 in the graph may come from its dense connections, not from real power to make decisions. - **Confidence:** Medium — IRS 990 data is primary-source and highly reliable, but the Chamber's political influence channels (Candidate Academy, lobbying) are opaque. The audit findings are concerning but not yet fully investigated.
