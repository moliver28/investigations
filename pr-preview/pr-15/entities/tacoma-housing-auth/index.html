## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - THA is the main public housing agency for the City of Tacoma. It runs thousands of housing units. It also hands out federal housing vouchers. It is a key partner in big city projects like Salishan. Its choices shape land use, affordable housing, and community growth across Pierce County's largest city. - THA is on the map because it has power from running a big organization. It controls federal housing money and a large redevelopment budget. - A few related groups sit outside this map. Pierce County runs its own housing programs in a different area. Lakewood and Bremerton have their own housing agencies. They do not work inside Pierce County's main power structure, so they are left out.

## 1. Who they are - **Exact legal name:** Housing Authority of the City of Tacoma. It does business as Tacoma Housing Authority, also called THA. - **Entity type:** THA is a public housing agency. It is a part of government. It was set up under Washington state law. It is not a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. It is not an LLC. It is a quasi-governmental agency. - **Governing law:** A Washington state law says THA must have a five-person Board of Commissioners. The Mayor of Tacoma picks each member. They serve five-year terms. Since 1998, one of the five members must be a current THA housing aid recipient (Source 1). - **tax ID number (EIN):** UNVERIFIED — could not find the EIN in public records. - **Parent/subsidiaries:** THA has no parent group. It runs as an independent agency. It manages tax-credit partnerships, like Hillside Terrace and Bay Terrace, through its property arm. - **MTW designation:** THA takes part in the Moving to Work (MTW) program. This is run by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). Only a few agencies nationwide get this status. It lets THA design its own housing plans (Source 6).

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Power held — statutory authority:** THA has the legal power to run housing aid programs in Tacoma. It manages federal HUD money. It runs public housing units. It gives out Housing Choice Vouchers (HCVs). It controls about 3,552 Section 8 vouchers. It also gives out special vouchers: VASH, FUP, FYI, NHT, and EHV (Source 4). - **Market share / scale:** THA is the biggest housing authority in Pierce County. Its Board adopted the FY2026 budget on January 28, 2026. The FY2022 budget had about $55.3 million in revenue and $50.8 million in spending. It added 57 new Project-Based Voucher units (Source 5). The FY2026 budget covers departments: Asset Management & Real Estate Development ($4,081,892), Client Support & Empowerment ($2,954,327), Executive ($904,800), Finance ($2,733,284) (Source 2). - **Redevelopment authority:** THA ran the largest housing redevelopment in Tacoma's history. It was the $200 million Salishan project. THA tore down 855 public housing units from the World War II era. It then rebuilt a mixed-income neighborhood on 188 acres. HUD gave THA a $35 million HOPE VI grant in 2001. THA picked Lorig Associates as its development partner (Source 7). - **Budget controlled:** THA controls an annual operating budget in the tens of millions. Most of the money comes from HUD. Monthly cash payments were $11,792,969 in November 2025 (Source 2). - **Appointment/veto powers:** The Board of Commissioners approves the yearly budget. It signs off on contracts and payments. It hires and fires the Executive Director. It sets policy. The Mayor of Tacoma picks the Board members. So the Mayor has indirect control over THA through these picks.

## 5. How they touch government - **Federal (HUD):** HUD is THA's main funder. THA gets HUD money for Section 8, HCV, public housing, VASH, FUP, FYI, NHT, and EHV vouchers. THA sends its MTW plan to HUD each year for approval. Congress's FY2026 budget gave THA more voucher money (Source 6). - **City of Tacoma:** The Mayor picks THA Board members. THA works with the city on land use, redevelopment, and community growth. - **Pierce County Council:** THA works with Pierce County on regional housing, human services, and law and justice issues (connection weight 3). - **Tacoma City Council:** THA works with the Tacoma City Council on redevelopment projects (connection weight 3). - **Contracts and grants:** THA partners with private developers on housing redevelopment. Its partners include Lorig Associates, 1DROP, LMC Construction, and Brawner. THA has also gotten state money for supportive housing. - **Lobbying:** THA lobbies at the federal level through CLPHA, the Council of Large Public Housing Authorities. Executive Director April Black joined the CLPHA board in September 2023 (Source 3).

## 6. Other seats they hold - **Board overlaps:** - Stanley Rumbaugh (Chair) is a founding partner at the law firm Rumbaugh, Rideout, Adkins and Wallace. He is a former Pierce County Superior Court judge. He served 10 years on the Bates Technical College Board of Trustees. He is a World Trade Council director. He sits on the WA State Association for Justice Board of Governors. - Dr. Minh-Anh Hodge (Vice Chair) directs Second Language Acquisition, Elementary Education and Leadership at Tacoma Public Schools. - Nathe Lawver (Commissioner) is Secretary-Treasurer of the Pierce County Central Labor Council AFL-CIO. He is a member of LiUNA Laborers' Local 252. He chairs United Way of Pierce County. He sits on the Pierce County Workforce Board, the Greater Tacoma Community Foundation Executive Board, and the Pierce County Economic Development Board. - Rev. She had been THA Deputy Executive Director for six years under longtime Executive Director Michael Mirra. - **Development partnerships:** Lorig Associates worked on Salishan. 1DROP, LMC Construction, and Brawner worked on the Salishan-Hillside renovation in 2026.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** THA controls a large set of affordable housing and federal funding in Tacoma. Its power comes from its legal role as Tacoma's housing agency. It also comes from its MTW status, which gives it more freedom. And it comes from its ability to run big projects like Salishan. The volunteer Board has deep ties across Pierce County: Rumbaugh in law and the courts, Lawver in labor, Hodge in schools. This gives THA reach across many areas. Executive Director April Black runs day-to-day work. She also represents THA at the national level through CLPHA. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** The Mayor picks Board members who then decide on redevelopment deals. This could lead to conflicts. Board members may back the Mayor's political goals. Developers who get THA vouchers may have reasons to shape who sits on the Board. Nathe Lawver serves on the THA Board and leads the AFL-CIO. This creates a possible tie between THA contracts and labor interests. Stanley Rumbaugh has served since 1998. That is 28 years. It shows deep, lasting influence rather than rotating civic service. - **Analysis of alternatives:** A different reading would say THA is just a federal-policy tool. Its budget comes mostly from HUD. The Mayor picks its Board. Under this view, THA's "power" is borrowed from federal money and mayoral priorities. The Salishan project, while big, came from a federal grant. So it could be seen as a federal project, not an independent THA choice. - **Confidence:** Medium — THA's who they are legally, governance structure, budget, and major projects are well-documented in primary sources. However, the full extent of its informal political influence (lobbying, developer relationships, Board appointment dynamics) is under-documented in available sources.
