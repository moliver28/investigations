## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why they are on the map:** The Foss family is on the map for two reasons. First, the Thea Foss Waterway is the heart of Tacoma's maritime story. Every big Port of Tacoma narrative mentions the Foss name. Second, Foss Maritime is now a Saltchuk company. It is a major player in the Port of Tacoma economy. The map treats the "Foss family" as a legacy brand and an old network of relatives. It does not treat them as the active operating company. - **Who is NOT on the map:** Foss Maritime itself is mapped under Saltchuk. Individual family members (like Thea's three sons) are not on the map. Only the family as a whole is mapped.

## 1. Who they are - **Old company:** Foss Launch and Tug Company (1904). Later renamed Foss Maritime Company. It was founded in **1889 in Tacoma, Washington**, by **Thea Foss** (a Norwegian immigrant) and her husband **Andrew Foss** (a carpenter). - **Where they stand now:** The Foss family no longer runs Foss Maritime. **In 1987, Foss Maritime was sold to Saltchuk Resources, Inc.** Saltchuk calls itself "a privately owned family investment company that mainly focuses on maritime transportation." Foss Maritime still operates on its own. But it is part of a worldwide Saltchuk network. - **Saltchuk's other companies:** Foss Maritime is one of seven business units under Saltchuk Resources. They include TOTE (TOTE Maritime Alaska runs the Tacoma to Anchorage RO/RO ships), Carlile Transportation, Alaska Air Cargo, Young Brothers in Hawaii, Hawaiian Tug and Barge, and Saltchuk Logistics. - **EIN (Employer Identification Number) / structure:** The Foss family is not a registered company. The active companies are Saltchuk Resources and its subsidiaries. **Family-trust details: not found in public records.** - **Legacy assets that still matter:** - **Thea Foss Waterway** — Tacoma's downtown waterfront. It used to be called City Waterway. The City of Tacoma renamed it after Thea Foss. (Date of renaming not found.) - **"Tugboat Annie"** — The 1937 RKO film and later stories were reportedly based on Thea Foss.

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Cultural power:** The Foss family still has a lot of symbolic power in Tacoma. The Thea Foss Waterway hosts the Museum of Glass, the Hotel Murano, and the Dock Street waterfront. Every big story about downtown Tacoma mentions the Foss name. The Port of Tacoma's own history starts with the Foss boat business. - **Old economic power:** The Foss family started with one used rowboat. By 1904, they had 200 boats. Later they moved into ocean towing, shipyards, and shipping along the coast. At their peak in the mid-1900s, Foss Maritime was a big Pacific Northwest employer. Today the family is an investor in Saltchuk. They are not an active operating business. - **Current power through Saltchuk:** Saltchuk's combined yearly revenue is about **$2.75 billion**. They have about **6,000 employees**. The company calls itself "one of the largest privately held businesses in Washington." The Foss family still benefits from any ownership stake they keep. The exact ownership split is **not known**. Saltchuk is private and family-controlled. - **Power over the port and rules:** Foss Maritime works as a tugboat contractor at the Port of Tacoma and the Port of Seattle. Saltchuk also owns TOTE Maritime Alaska. TOTE runs ships from Tacoma to Anchorage. That makes it a major U.S. Maritime Administration / Jones Act carrier. So the family, through Saltchuk, takes part in Jones Act policy fights. - **Civic and giving power:** Foss Maritime has a corporate giving program. The family's Tacoma ties are still active in local maritime history work. That includes the Museum of Glass and the Foss Waterway Development Authority.

## 1. Who they are - **The bottom line:** The Foss family built Tacoma's maritime economy. Today they are legacy shareholders through Saltchuk, not active operators. Their lasting power is their name. The Thea Foss Waterway is the heart of Tacoma's waterfront story. For map purposes, the family marks where the Port of Tacoma and Saltchuk story began. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** - Foss Maritime competes for tug-assignment contracts at the Port of Tacoma against other operators. Saltchuk leaders and Port of Tacoma commissioners move in the same civic circles. **Specific director overlap: not found.** - The family name is used by the City of Tacoma in waterfront marketing. New buildings along Thea Foss Waterway (like the Murray Morgan Bridge and Thea Foss Apartments) help the brand owners. - **Alternative reading:** Since the 1987 sale to Saltchuk, the family is mostly a brand name. They are not an active power. Saltchuk is the real player. The Foss family is mostly a historical marker on the map. - **Confidence:** Medium — founding history, sale date, and brand are well documented.
