# Concord Counsel Business Search & Lakewood Ethics Code — Findings

## Concord Counsel
The WA Secretary of State CCFS portal (ccfs.sos.wa.gov) is a client-rendered SPA that cannot be searched programmatically. The SOS website does not expose a searchable API. To verify Concord Counsel's business registration, UBI number, and filing status, someone must manually search at:
https://ccfs.sos.wa.gov/

Known data:
- Address: 705 S 9th St, Tacoma, WA (per concordcounsel.com)
- Lindholm LinkedIn: lists "Chief Mediator" at Concord Counsel, "CEO" previously at Terry Wise & Associates
- The business exists and operates — but whether it's an LLC, sole proprietorship, or corporation determines F-1 filing requirements
- Under WA law, sole proprietorships don't need to register with the SOS (only file a DBA/trade name with the county or DOR)

**Verdict:** Cannot determine Concord Counsel's legal structure programmatically. Manual search of CCFS portal required.

## Lakewood Code of Ethics
**CRITICAL FINDING:** Lakewood DOES have a local Code of Ethics. The 2019-07-01 Council Agenda PDF references:
> "Chapter 1.32 Lakewood Municipal Code, Lakewood Code of Ethics"

The Council Rules of Procedure (Section 7.3) state:
> "Councilmembers are subject to the provisions of the City of Lakewood's Code of Ethics and should refer to that document in questions of Conflict of Interest."

The voting rules (Section 10.1) state:
> "Each member present must vote on all questions before the Council and may abstain only for reasons acceptable to a majority of the Council such as stated conflict of interest of an issue of appearance of fairness."

This means:
1. Lakewood has had a codified Code of Ethics (LMC Chapter 1.32) since at least 2019
2. Council rules explicitly reference it for conflict-of-interest questions
3. Abstention is permitted for "stated conflict of interest or appearance of fairness" — but requires majority approval
4. Despite having BOTH state law (RCW 42.23) AND a local code of ethics, the 16-year recusal record is still zero

**This actually strengthens the systemic failure argument.** It's not that the rules don't exist — they do. It's that they've never been enforced.
