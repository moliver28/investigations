Why this person or group is on the map - **Why we added Kelly Chambers:** She served three terms as a Washington State Representative for the 25th Legislative District, Position 1, from January 14, 2019 to January 13, 2025. ## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** Kelly Chambers' power has shifted. She moved from a state legislative role (state representative with caucus leadership) to a federal executive role (SBA Regional Administrator for 4 states). As a state legislator for three terms, she held office but had limited individual power (one of 98 representatives). Her Republican caucus leadership roles (Vice Chair, Assistant Whip, Ranking Member of Appropriations) gave her institutional influence within the minority party. Her loss in the 2024 Pierce County Executive race (by 2.7%) showed she could compete countywide but could not win the top executive office. Her SBA appointment is a jump to federal administrative power. She now controls SBA program delivery across a 4-state region. That may be more powerful in practice than her old legislative role. Her donor base is heavy on business (PACs, developers, auto dealers, construction). That matches her pro-business record and her small-business-owner identity. - **Conflicts of interest:** As SBA Regional Administrator, she oversees federal small business lending and programs. Her own businesses (Visiting Angels franchise and Lomcevak Cellars winery) are small businesses. They could in theory use SBA programs. That is a possible conflict. Federal ethics rules usually require her to step aside from matters involving her own businesses. Her wide network of business donors from her legislative career (10 or more business donors in the graph) could also raise appearance-of-conflict issues in her SBA role. This is especially true if those donors later seek SBA help. No actual conflict has been identified. - **The other view:** Chambers' SBA appointment may just be a standard post-election patronage job for a defeated candidate. It may not be a real power gain. The Regional Administrator role is administrative, not policy-making. She carries out federal SBA policy. She does not set it. Her legislative record (three terms, no major signature legislation found) shows limited policy impact. Her county executive loss shows she could not win a countywide race even as the Republican nominee in a competitive county. Her business donors may reflect ideology rather than transactional ties. - **Confidence:** Medium — primary sources (SBA.gov, the PDC, and WA election results) confirm her positions, election results, and campaign finance. Her federal appointment is confirmed by SBA.gov and several news outlets. Personal details are well documented through Wikipedia and the News Tribune. How much real influence she has inside the SBA, and her future political path, are still uncertain.
