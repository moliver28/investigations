## 0. Why this person or group is on the map <!-- id:boundary --> - **Inclusion rule — well-connected** : The Master Builders Association of Pierce County (MBA or MBAPC) is on this map because it gives money to Pierce County Council campaigns. In 2024, its Affordable Housing Council political action committee (PAC) gave $2,400 to Paul Herrera (District 2) and $2,400 to Amy Cruver (District 3). The News Tribune reported those donations on September 4, 2024. That makes MBA a player in land-use and housing-supply politics that shape Pierce County growth. - **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** (a) Master Builders Association of King & Snohomish Counties is a separate legal group. (b) The Building Industry Association of Washington (BIAW) is the state-level umbrella. Pierce MBA is affiliated with BIAW, but BIAW is not profiled in this case file at this tier. (c) Absher Construction and other MBA builder-members are firms, not the association itself. They are routed to open questions.

## 1. Who they are <!-- id:identity tier:1 --> - **Legal name:** Master Builders Association of Pierce County (ProPublica lists "Master Builders Of Pierce County"). The brand name is "Master Builders Pierce County" or "MBAPC". - **Entity type:** 501(c)(6) trade association. The 501(c)(6) is a federal tax category for business leagues. This is FACT plus INFERENCE based on CauseIQ and ProPublica records. - **EIN:** 91-0550621. - **Tax-exempt since:** October 1960. - **Founded:** 1945 (per the Chamber listing). - **HQ:** 1211 S Pearl St, Tacoma WA 98465 (member-zone login page); phone (253) 272-2112. - **Parent / affiliations:** State-affiliate of the Building Industry Association of Washington (BIAW). Sister association to Master Builders Association of King & Snohomish Counties in Bellevue, WA. MBA runs a separate political action committee called "Master Builders Association of Pierce County Affordable Housing Council" (per TNT 2024 reporting). - **Governing law:** Washington state nonprofit law and the federal 501(c)(6) tax rule. The state Public Disclosure Commission (PDC) oversees the PAC.

## 3. Where their power comes from <!-- id:power-basis --> - **What power does this group hold?** MBA speaks for about 800+ residential-construction firms in Pierce County. Its "Government Affairs team works alongside local and state leaders to advocate for: Predictable permitting timelines · Efficient review processes · Balanced regulations · Housing policies that increase supply—not costs." That quote is from an MBAPC Facebook post cited in the LinkedIn overview. - **Power-domain overlaps:** - **Economic / market-share:** About 10,000+ employees work for member firms. MBA has "over 850 Builder & Associate Members" and "50+ Professions & Specializations" (MBAPC sponsorship page). It gives the home-building industry one voice in county politics. - **decision-making / political:** The Affordable Housing Council PAC gave $2,400 each to two Pierce County Council incumbents in 2024 (Herrera District 2, Cruver District 3). That PAC money was 8.5% of Herrera's raised total and 4% of Cruver's (per The News Tribune). It gives MBA a direct lever on the body that votes on zoning, code changes, and the County's Comprehensive Plan. - **Reputational:** Annual REX (Remodeling Excellence) Awards and "Best of the South Sound" recognitions give MBA visibility and standard-setting power in the industry. - **Basis for each overlap (scoring framework):** trade-association size × campaign-finance lever × state-association umbrella = small absolute power score (0.013), but a real, repeatable channel into housing-supply politics. - **key facts:** Revenue = program-services revenue $1,316,095 on the most-recent 990 (CauseIQ; year-stamp UNVERIFIED, so treat as a fiscal-year reference, not a fact for a specific year). Ownership = member-owned 501(c)(6), not investor-owned. Market position = leading trade voice for residential building in Pierce County (self-described). Government contracts/grants = none directly identified; revenue mix is dues, sponsorships, and program fees. Jobs controlled = indirect via member firms (~10,000). Land/real-estate holdings = none identified (association, not a developer).

## 1. Who they are <!-- id:assessment --> - **Verdict:** MBAPC is a mid-tier, repeatable input-channel actor in Pierce County housing politics. It does not hold formal power (no public-board seats, no contract authority). But its PAC turns about $5,000 of biennial donations into access to two sitting Council members who vote on every land-use item in front of the County Council. The method is small-dollar, high-frequency, and durable: it works no matter who wins. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** (a) The PAC name "Affordable Housing Council" suggests an affordability mission, but the broader group's stated stance is "supply-not-costs" deregulation. In plain terms, the public brand is softer than the policy stance. A defense reading would say that adding supply IS an affordability strategy. That is a fair counter, not a debunking. (b) Donations to incumbents in both parties (Herrera R, Cruver R) signal bipartisan access, not partisan capture. - **Analysis of alternatives:** The other reading is that $2,400 donations are tiny in a modern Pierce County race and that MBA's real influence is through comment letters at planning commissions, not campaign cash. The evidence supports both: the TNT story documents the donations as one slice of a much broader pattern of housing-industry PAC activity. Confidence in this profile is Medium. The group's *existence* and PAC activity are well-documented. The *weight* of its donations on Council votes is INFERENCE. - **Confidence:** Medium — trade-association identity, EIN, and 2024 PAC donations are primary-sourced. Influence on outcomes is inferred from PDC filings and the association's own lobbying stance.
