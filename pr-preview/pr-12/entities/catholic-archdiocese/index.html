## 0. Why this person or group is on the map <!-- id:boundary --> - The Archdiocese of Seattle is on this map for two reasons. First, it directly runs Catholic parishes and schools in Pierce County. These include Holy Rosary, St. Ann, St. Rita, and Visitation in Tacoma, plus Bellarmine Preparatory and several grade schools. Second, it sponsors Virginia Mason Franciscan Health (a major hospital chain in Pierce County). That gives it a voice in Pierce County's health-care market. Its 2020 to 2022 "Pierce Deanery" plan merged four Tacoma parishes into one. That was a big land-use and institutional change. - **Adjacent groups not profiled.** The Vatican and the global Catholic Church are too remote. Catholic Community Services of Western Washington is a separate nonprofit. CommonSpirit Health (the parent of VMFH) has its own profile. Independent Catholic schools that are not archdiocesan (like Annie Wright, which is Episcopal) are not included.

## 1. Who they are <!-- id:identity tier:1 --> - **Legal name:** Archdiocese of Seattle (in Latin: Archidiœcesis Seattlensis). - **Entity type:** A Roman Catholic archdiocese (Latin Rite means the standard Western form of the Catholic Church). The civil corporate side is the **Corporation of the Catholic Archbishop of Seattle**, a nonprofit under Washington state law (RCW 24.03). This corporation holds title to parish property, schools, and investments. *Inferred — UNVERIFIED in this round of research, but standard for US Latin-rite dioceses.* - **History:** 1850 as the Diocese of Nesqually. Renamed in 1907. Became a metropolitan archdiocese (the head of a church region) in 1951. - **Territory:** 24,814 square miles — all of Western Washington, including all of Pierce County. - **Stats (2022):** 143 parishes, 72 schools, 6.1 million people in territory, about 899,000 Catholics (14.7%). - **Mother church:** St. James Cathedral, Seattle (804 9th Avenue). - **Current archbishop:** Paul D. Etienne (DD, installed 2019). - **Governing law:** Canon law (the Catholic Church's own legal system) and Washington state law (RCW 24.03 for nonprofits, RCW 84.36 for religious-property tax breaks).

## 3. Where their power comes from <!-- id:power-basis --> - The Archdiocese holds three types of power. It owns and runs Catholic parish, school, and cemetery real estate in Pierce County. It sponsors CHI Franciscan / Virginia Mason Franciscan Health (a hospital chain). That gives it three power levers: 1. **Land-use decisions.** It can consolidate, sell, or redevelop property. In 2022 it sold four First Hill Seattle properties to Westbank. In 2020 to 2022 its Pierce Deanery plan merged Holy Rosary, St. Ann, Visitation, and St. Rita into one parish. 2. **Cultural and community reach.** About 900,000 Catholics live in Western Washington. That makes the Archdiocese the largest single organized religious group in Pierce County. Its parishes can mobilize people on school-board, charter, and immigrant-services issues. 3. **Health-care influence.** The Archdiocese sponsors CommonSpirit, the parent of VMFH. The archbishop's office has a say in mission and ethics at Pierce County's largest hospital chain. That includes St. Joseph Medical Center in Tacoma; St. Anthony in Gig Harbor; St. Clare in Lakewood; St. Elizabeth in Enumclaw; and St. Francis in Federal Way. - **Key facts:** - **Tax status:** Houses of worship and their integrated groups are tax-exempt under IRC §501(c)(3) (a federal tax code for charities). Parishes and schools pay no federal income tax. They also get a property-tax break under RCW 84.36.020. - **Board overlaps:** The archbishop sits on Catholic Health Ministries and the Archdiocesan Finance Council. Pastors sit on parish councils. Lay trustees sit on parish finance councils. - **Government funding:** Schools get state K–12 funding (under RCW 28A.150) from the Common Schools fund. Parishes rely on Sunday collections and the Annual Catholic Appeal. Downstream, VMFH brings in large Medicaid and Medicare payments.

## 1. Who they are <!-- id:assessment --> - **Verdict:** The Archdiocese is a stable, long-lasting institution in Pierce County. Its power comes from parish real-estate decisions (the 2020 to 2022 Pierce Deanery merger is a clear example), sponsorship of the dominant Catholic hospital chain (CHI Franciscan → VMFH), and a parish-school network that reaches thousands of families each week. Its composite power score (0.012) is too low, because the framework under-weights canonical and religious power. - **Conflict flags:** Sponsorship of VMFH gives the Archdiocese a voice in a major Pierce County employer with a large land footprint. Canonical authority over a hospital chain creates a parallel accountability channel outside public meetings. The property-tax break for parish and school land is a recurring public-finance question. The defense: religious property-tax breaks are constitutional (Walz v. Taxpayers, 1970) and predate the modern hospital chain. The Archdiocese's Pierce County footprint is small compared to public-school or secular nonprofit landholdings. - **Counter-reading:** The Archdiocese is in steep national demographic decline. Western Washington's Catholic population is still growing (Hispanic and immigrant inflows). VMFH is one of the largest private employers in Pierce County. The Archdiocese is not vestigial, but it is constrained by sexual-abuse bankruptcy filings that have drained reserves in other dioceses.
