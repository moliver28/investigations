## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Inclusion rule — from holding office + decision-making:** The Pierce County Council is the county's legislative branch. It is the "policy-determining body" under the Home Rule Charter (Charter Section 2.20). It has "all powers of the County not otherwise reserved to the people, the Executive, or general law." Its powers include: approving the budget, controlling zoning and land use, approving contracts, and setting taxes. The Council's 70+ graph edges make it the most connected node. Anyone who wants county money, zoning changes, or policy changes has to come to the Council. - **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** The Pierce County Charter Review Commission (21 members, elected 2025) is a separate temporary body that reviews the charter. It is excluded because it only meets from time to time. The Board of Health (which includes Council members) is mapped separately as `tpchd`. The Performance Audits Committee is a Council committee, not a separate entity. ## 1. Where their power comes from - **Statutory authority (Charter Section 2.20):** "The Council has all powers of the County not otherwise reserved to the people, the Executive, or general law." This is the broadest possible grant of legislative authority. Specific powers include: - **Budget appropriation (Charter Section 2.20(1)(a)):** "To levy taxes, appropriate revenue, and adopt budgets for the County." The Council adopts the biennial budget by Dec. 1 of each odd-numbered year. - **Zoning and land use:** Controls county zoning, the comprehensive plan, and land-use rules. This is the most direct power over the growth-machine developers in the graph. - **Contract approval:** Approves major county contracts. - **Ordinance enactment:** Adopts all county ordinances and resolutions. - **Appointment confirmation:** Confirms certain Executive appointments. - **Budget authority:** The 2026–2027 biennial budget is $3.5 billion (adopted Nov 25, 2025, ordinance O2025-562s3). The General Fund is $967.4M (up 2.2%). The Council can amend the Executive's proposed budget. The budget passed 4–3 on a party-line vote. - **Veto override (Charter Section 2.50):** Override requires 5 affirmative votes. That is a supermajority of 7. With a 4D–3R split, party-line vetoes by a Democratic Executive cannot be overridden. This limits the Council's power compared to the Executive. - **key facts:** - *Budget controlled:* $3.5B biennial ($1.75B each year) - *Appointment/veto powers:* Confirms certain Executive appointments; 5-vote supermajority to override Executive veto - *Jurisdictions governed:* All of unincorporated Pierce County (about 920K people); some county-wide functions affect incorporated cities too ## 3. It approves the $3.5B biennial budget. It controls zoning and land use. It approves contracts and sets taxes. Its 70+ graph edges make it the most connected node. The 4D–3R makeup, combined with the 5-vote veto override threshold, means the Council is subordinate to a same-party Executive on vetoed items.
