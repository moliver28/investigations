## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Inclusion rule:** Pierce Transit is on this map on the grounds of "from holding office," "decision-making," and "well-connected." It is the main public transit operator in Pierce County. It controls a $367 million annual budget. It runs the county's bus and paratransit service. Its Board of Commissioners is made up of elected officials from Tacoma, Pierce County, Lakewood, Puyallup, and other cities. That creates direct board overlaps with municipal governments. Pierce Transit has its own sales tax. The current rate is 0.6 percent. A proposal would raise it to 0.9 percent. That is direct taxing power over all purchases in the Public Transportation Benefit Area (PTBA). - **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** Sound Transit is a separate regional transit agency (mapped as `sound-transit`). Community Transit is in Snohomish County, outside the boundary. King County Metro is also outside the boundary. The Pierce Transit Workers' Union, ATU Local 758, has a non-voting seat on the board. It is not on the map as a separate node. ## 1. Where their power comes from - **Statutory authority:** RCW 36.57A lets PTBAs levy sales tax (with voter approval), run transit service, contract with other agencies, and own and operate property. Pierce Transit runs fixed-route bus, dial-a-ride (SHUTTLE paratransit), vanpool, and rideshare services. - **Taxing authority:** Pierce Transit currently levies a 0.6 percent local sales tax within the PTBA. The last increase, from 0.3 to 0.6 percent, was approved by voters in 2002. A November 3, 2026 ballot measure (Resolution 2026-006) would raise the rate to 0.9 percent. That is a 0.3 percentage-point increase (3 cents on a $10 purchase). If approved, it would fund a 47 percent boost in service and bring in about $64 million a year. - **Market share:** Pierce Transit is the main (near-monopoly) public transit provider in Pierce County. It runs 29 routes and 174 buses. Weekday ridership averages about 29,700 (Q2 2026). Annual ridership was 7,833,600 in 2025. - **Regulatory capture:** Limited. Pierce Transit must follow FTA rules and WSDOT oversight. It needs voter approval for tax increases. - **Domain overlaps:** Government (transit authority and taxing power), economic (transportation infrastructure that supports commerce), and political (board members are elected officials who campaign on transit positions). ## 3.
