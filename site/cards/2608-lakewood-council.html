2026-08-16: Lakewood City Council Investigation

Summary

Investigation into all seven Lakewood, Washington City Council members revealing systemic ethics failures: 56 council meetings reviewed across 16 years (2010-2026) produced exactly ONE recusal (Brandstetter, 2010). The Lakewood City Council has an institutional culture of zero recusal enforcement despite multiple members having direct employment and financial conflicts with matters they vote on. Five of seven members hold paid positions with another government or public agency (Bocchi and Pearson/Pierce County, Belle/Kent, Talbo/Renton, Lauricella/South Sound 911) — a rate of 71%, versus a peer range of 0–29% (median 14%) among comparable WA code cities (recount 2026-10-04: evidence/dual-government-recount.md). One owns a real estate brokerage (Lindholm), one works as a county engineer while voting on city infrastructure contracts (Pearson), and one is CTO of South Sound 911, a regional public agency funded by a dedicated sales tax in member cities (Lauricella). The investigation identified a podcast-donor overlap (Lindholm), a sworn-in-by-boss oath ceremony (Belle), and a PDC enforcement case (Bocchi).

Key questions

  1. Why has no council member formally recused themselves in 16 years of meetings?
  2. Do dual-employment council members (Bocchi, Belle, Talbo) raise conflict-of-interest concerns under Washington law?
  3. Does Lindholm's F-1 omission of Concord Counsel income raise questions about disclosure compliance (RCW 42.17 reporting threshold is $2,400)?
  4. Is Lindholm's podcast (The Informed Citizen) a platform where campaign donors have also appeared as guests?
  5. Does Lauricella's CTO role at South Sound 911, which receives Lakewood city funding, create a divided loyalty conflict?
  6. Does Brandstetter, as South Sound 911 board representative, have a governance overlap with Lauricella as his council colleague?
