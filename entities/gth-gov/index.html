## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Inclusion rule — from holding office + well-connected + decision-making.** GTH is short for Gordon Thomas Honeywell Governmental Affairs. It is included as Washington State's self-described "leading lobbying & government relations firm." Its home office is in Tacoma (Pierce County). It meets all three Laumann/Marsden/Prensky criteria: it holds power through its office (the largest WA lobbying firm by client count, with offices in Olympia, Tacoma, and DC). It is well-connected (it has lobbying ties to Pierce County Council and the Port of Tacoma). It makes decisions (it represents 60+ clients — cities, ports, utilities, and corporations — before the WA legislature and executive agencies).

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** GTH controls access to Washington State's legislative and executive decision-making process for 60+ clients. That makes it the state's most active lobbying firm by client count. Its power is access-based. It uses a bipartisan network with legislators, agency officials, and local elected officials. The firm's Tacoma home and Pierce County client list embed it in the local power structure. Local governments hire GTH with taxpayer funds to lobby the state. That is a key feature of Washington's political system. Cities lack their own legislative lobbying capacity. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** GTH represents many clients with possibly competing interests. For example, utilities (Cowlitz PUD, Lewis County PUD, Inland Power, WA Independent Energy Distributors) sit alongside energy consumers and cities. The firm also represents both government entities (cities, ports) and private corporations (FedEx, T-Mobile, Total Wine) that may have conflicting legislative priorities. Defense counter: Lobbying firms often represent many clients. Conflicts are managed through internal walls and separate lobbyist assignments. All client ties are publicly disclosed via PDC filings. - **Analysis of alternatives :** The opposing reading is that GTH's "leading firm" claim is self-promotion. Its real power is limited to access, not control. Legislators ultimately make their own choices regardless of lobbyist pressure. Under this reading, GTH is a service provider, not a power center. Its 60+ clients show business success, not political power. But the firm's 30+ year history (since 1993), its Olympia, Tacoma, and DC offices, and the fact that government entities pay GTH to influence other government entities suggest it gives real value in the legislative process. - **Confidence:** High. GTH's legal form (UBI, incorporation date, registered agent), client list, lobbying registrations, and setup are confirmed by many primary sources. These include WA Secretary of State business registries (reliability 0.9), PDC lobbyist filings (reliability 0.85), OpenSecrets federal lobbying data (reliability 0.9), and the firm's own site (reliability 0.8). The main gaps are specific legislative outcomes tied to GTH lobbying and individual lobbyist campaign contribution records.
