## 3. Where their power comes from - **Endorsement power:** The PCRP endorsement is a formal process. Candidates fill out questionnaires. The Endorsements Committee interviews them. The central committee votes. An endorsement signals party support to Republican primary voters and can pull in volunteers and donors. In Pierce County's nonpartisan local races, a Republican endorsement helps conservative voters find aligned candidates. - **Donor channeling:** PCRP has two committees (exempt and non-exempt). Together, they took in about $633,290 in the 2024 cycle ($437,656 non-exempt plus $195,634 exempt). This money goes to endorsed candidates, independent expenditures, and party operations. - **Candidate recruitment and training:** PCRP takes endorsement applications each year. It publishes candidate guides. Filing week for candidates is usually in May. - **Party machinery:** Chair McMullan controls strategy, fundraising, and the endorsement calendar. He also sits on the WAGOP Executive Board (Region 7), linking the county party to the state party. - **Key facts answered:** - **Who is funded and endorsed?** Republican candidates for Pierce County offices (Executive, Council, Assessor-Treasurer), state legislative seats in Pierce County districts, and some nonpartisan races. - **political action committee (PAC) spending?** The non-exempt committee spent $439,502 in the 2024 cycle. Specific IE targets were not confirmed in this pass. - **Party machinery control?** Chair McMullan and officers run the group. State committeeman Prutzman represents Pierce County at WAGOP. - **Candidate recruitment and training?** Endorsement forms come out each year. Candidate guides are planned for spring 2025. Endorsement interviews happen with the Endorsements Committee.
