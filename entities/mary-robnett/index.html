## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why she is here:** Mary Robnett is the elected Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney. She decides which felony cases to charge, plea, or drop in a county of about 900,000 people. She runs an office with a $20 million budget and more than 100 lawyers and staff. Her power is real because of the office itself. - **Who is left out:** Mark Lindquist lost to her in 2018 and is no longer in office. Coreen Schnepf and Bryan Chushcoff want her job in 2026, but they do not hold office yet. Bob Ferguson is Washington State Attorney General, not Pierce County. Robnett once worked for him.

The office budget is around $20 million and pays more than 100 lawyers and staff. The exact current budget is not verified. - **Prosecutorial discretion:** She has near-absolute power over charging decisions. The Sheriff, Council, and courts cannot override her. Only voters can recall her or defeat her. This power is unique to the prosecutor's role. (Per Washington state law.) - **Jurisdiction:** All of Pierce County (about 900,000 people) for felony cases. She also handles civil matters for Pierce County government (legal advice, representation). - **Key facts:** - Office: Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney (elected, nonpartisan). - Budget: about $20 million (exact figure not verified); set by the County Council. - Hiring power: She hires and fires deputy prosecutors and staff. She has no veto over legislation. - Jurisdiction: All felony cases in Pierce County. Civil counsel to county government.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** Mary Robnett runs the Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney's Office. That office is the only one that files felony charges in Pierce County. She won the job in 2018 with 63% of the vote and won again in 2022. Her power is rooted in state law (Revised Code of Washington (RCW) 36.27) and her own discretion. She alone decides which cases to charge, what plea deals to offer, and which cases to drop. This is one of the most concentrated forms of individual power in the criminal justice system. She won by beating an incumbent (Lindquist) who had politicized the office. She ran on a de-politicization platform. She had strong legal community support (Minority Bar Association "Exceptionally Well Qualified" rating). Her power is fading now. She is not running again in 2026, so she is a lame duck. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags (defense counter-argument):** A defense lawyer would say: (1) Robnett's 18 years in the PA Office and her deep community roots (Special Assault Unit, Sexual Assault Center) show commitment to public service, not power-seeking; (2) the Weyerhaeuser donation is a standard corporate contribution to a nonpartisan office — there is no proof it changed her charging decisions; (3) her husband's law enforcement background is common among prosecutors and does not bias her; (4) her nonpartisan filing and de-politicization platform show she is not a political operator. - **Other reading:** The opposing view is that prosecutorial discretion is power with almost no accountability.
