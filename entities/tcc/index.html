## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why it is on the map — from holding office / institutional:** Tacoma Community College is on the map as a public college in Pierce County. It has a large budget (about $84M a year). Its Board of Trustees is appointed by the Governor and makes policy. Board members connect the school to major Pierce County power players (MultiCare, United Way, Pierce County Executive's office, Puyallup Tribe). Its inclusion is from holding office (it runs a public institution with a budget and land) and well-connected (board members link it to MultiCare, United Way, Pierce County government, and tribal government) — Laumann, Marsden & Prensky 1983. - **Adjacent but EXCLUDED candidates:** The TCC Foundation (a separate 501(c)(3) supporting organization) is treated as part of TCC, not profiled separately. The Washington State Board for Community and Technical Colleges (SBCTC) is the statewide coordinating body and is excluded as a state-level entity outside Pierce County.

## 1. Who they are - **Exact legal name:** Tacoma Community College (also called TCC). - **Type of entity:** A public community college district. One of 34 community and technical college districts in Washington State. It is a government entity, not a nonprofit or private company. - **tax ID number (EIN)/registration:** Not publicly available in sources checked. As a public entity, it is part of the Washington State public higher education system. - **Parent/subsidiaries:** TCC is a standalone college district. It is coordinated by (not a subsidiary of) the Washington State Board for Community and Technical Colleges (SBCTC), which sends state money to individual colleges. The TCC Foundation is a separate 501(c)(3) supporting organization. - **Ownership:** Public entity of the State of Washington. A 5-member Board of Trustees governs it. The Governor appoints trustees, and the State Senate confirms them. - **Governing law:** state law (RCW) 28B.50.100. The Board sets and reviews college policies, oversees management, sets direction, approves budgets, and protects the school's finances. - **Established:** 1965.

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Statutory authority:** The Board of Trustees holds authority under RCW 28B.50.100. It sets college policy, approves budgets, sets direction, and oversees management. The Governor appoints trustees to 5-year terms. The State Senate confirms them. This is a Governor appointment — political patronage at the state level. - **Budget size:** FY2023 total expenses: $83,971,405 (per the TCC FY23 Financial Report). Major categories: instruction ($31,7M), student services ($12.5M), institutional support ($10.1M), operations/maintenance ($9.7M), scholarships/fellowships/aid ($9.3M), academic support ($6.0M), auxiliary enterprises ($4.5M). Net position at end of FY2023: $105.9M. - **Students:** About 9,912 students (per the FY2023 report). Other sources report different numbers depending on the date and method. TCC serves about 9,000 to 10,000 students a year. - **Revenue sources:** Per the FY2023 report: 42% state money (through SBCTC), 24% tuition and fees, 19% local funds and grants, 8% auxiliary, 6% other, 1% federal. The state is the largest single source. TCC's budget depends on state legislative choices. - **Tax status:** Public entity. It does not pay income tax. It is funded through state appropriations (tax dollars), tuition, grants, and auxiliary revenue. - **Board roster and overlaps:** See Section 6 for board overlaps. - **Government share of revenue:** About 42% state (the largest source), plus about 1% federal grants and about 19% local/grant funding. Government funding is most of TCC's revenue.

## 5. How they touch government - **State funding:** TCC's main government link is with the Washington State Legislature (through SBCTC) for biennial budget appropriations. The 2025 Legislature kept $28.6M for the college system. TCC's government relations page tracks legislative requests. - **Board appointments:** The Governor appoints trustees, and the Senate confirms them. This is a direct political link. Current board members were appointed by Governors Inslee (Bernstein 2017, Ponepinto 2019, Santiago 2022, Wortham 2020) and Ferguson (Strobel 2026). - **Lobbying:** TCC advocates through its Government Relations office and the Trustees Association of Community and Technical Colleges. Specific lobbying costs are not confirmed. - **Grants:** TCC receives federal and state grants. The TCC Foundation has a Grants Research Manager. Specific grant amounts are not confirmed. - **Land use:** TCC's campus development (new buildings, parking, facilities) is subject to local land-use rules. Recent capital projects include the Center for Innovative Learning & Engagement and an EMS lab (per the 2023 annual report). - **Rules that apply to TCC:** Washington State audit (State Auditor's Office), SBCTC oversight, federal education rules (Title IV financial aid, accreditation), and local building and zoning codes.

## 1. Who they are - **The bottom line:** Tacoma Community College is a public community college with moderate institutional power. It has an $84M annual budget, about 9,912 students, and a Governor-appointed Board of Trustees. Its power is mostly institutional. It runs a college, a budget, a campus, and a workforce pipeline. It influences policy through its Board. Board members hold overlapping seats with MultiCare (2 members), United Way (CEO as Board Chair), the Pierce County Executive's office (Andrew Strobel), the Puyallup Tribe (Strobel's former role), Tacoma Public Utilities (Wortham), and the WA State LGBTQ Commission (Santiago). The TCC Foundation Board adds more overlaps with KeyBank, BECU, TAPCO Credit Union, and Virginia Mason Franciscan Health. That makes TCC a key institutional link between health care, banking, government, tribal, and nonprofit sectors in Pierce County. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** Trustee Andrew Strobel's dual role — Strategic Advisor to Pierce County Executive Ryan Mello AND former Puyallup Tribe Planning Director — could conflict when TCC matters touch county policy or tribal relations. Trustee Lois Bernstein's dual service on the TCC Board of Trustees AND the TCC Foundation Board could blur the line between public governance and private fundraising. Two MultiCare executives on the Foundation Board (Michael McDonald, Dr. Scott Meehan) plus Bernstein (retired MultiCare) create a strong MultiCare-to-TCC overlap — relevant if TCC's health training programs or campus health services touch MultiCare's interests. - **Analysis of alternatives:** The opposing reading: TCC is a standard community college with standard board appointments. The overlaps reflect the natural makeup of Pierce County civic leadership, not deliberate power-building. Board members are Governor-appointed (not self-selected), and their multi-board service is typical of civic leaders in mid-sized cities. TCC's $84M budget is modest compared to MultiCare ($7.2B) or Joint Base Lewis-McChord ($12.1B impact). TCC is a mid-tier institution, not a power center. Its 42% state funding dependency means its fiscal health is controlled by the Legislature, not by its board. - **Confidence:** Medium — primary sources (TCC website, WA Governor's office, TCC financial report) confirm the board composition, trustee terms, budget, enrollment, and presidential leadership. The board overlap analysis is based on documented board memberships. The significance of these board overlaps for actual policy influence is inferential — no specific instance of board overlap-driven policy change has been documented. The financial data is from FY2023 and may not reflect current-year figures.

- **open questions & leads:**. - Current-year (FY2025 or FY2026) budget and enrollment figures would update the FY2023 data. - TCC's specific capital project funding sources (state capital budget vs. local bonds vs. donations) would clarify the funding overlaps. - Andrew Strobel's appointment (January 2026) is recent — his impact on board decisions and county-tribal-TCC relations warrants monitoring. - The MultiCare-to-TCC board overlap (3 members across Trustee + Foundation boards) and whether TCC's nursing/allied health programs create a training pipeline that benefits MultiCare's workforce needs.
