## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - Kristin Ang is on the map because she holds real power in Pierce County. She is a Port of Tacoma Commissioner. The Port is one of the county's biggest landowners, employers, and tax collectors. The graph records her as a commissioner of the Port (weight 4). - She is also a Managing Member of the Northwest Seaport Alliance (NWSA). The NWSA is a joint group run by the Port of Tacoma and the Port of Seattle. It runs marine cargo terminals. It is the fourth-largest container port in North America. - She holds many outside board seats. She is president of the City Club of Tacoma board. She is in Tacoma Rotary 8. She is an ambassador for the Greater Tacoma Community Foundation (GTCF). She sits on the executive board of the Central Puget Sound Economic Development District. These seats connect Port power to Pierce County's civic network. (Sources 1, 2, 3) - **Excluded but related:** Don Meyer (former Port Commissioner she defeated in 2019). Other Port Commissioners (John McCarthy, Dick Marzano, Deanna Keller, JT Wilcox) are profiled separately. Puyallup Tribal Council is profiled separately too — even though the Tribe endorsed Ang, it is a sovereign nation.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** Kristin Ang holds tier-3 power as one of five elected Port of Tacoma commissioners. She governs major industrial property and marine cargo. She was the first person of color elected to the Port Commission. She was first elected in 2019 by a 3% margin and was re-elected in 2023. Her current term runs through Dec. 31, 2027. As Commission President in 2024, she automatically served as Co-chair of the Northwest Seaport Alliance (NWSA) Managing Members — that is the joint body over the largest container gateway on the U.S. West Coast. In 2026, she is Commission Secretary, which under NWSA bylaws maps to NWSA Co-Secretary. She sits on many Port committees (environmental, Tribal Liaison, Workforce Development, Strategic Business Initiatives, Local Economic Development Investment Fund) and many outside boards (Central Puget Sound EDD, Washington Council on International Trade, City Club of Tacoma board president, Tacoma Rotary 8, GTCF ambassador). She was the first Port Commission candidate ever endorsed by the Puyallup Tribe of Indians (in 2019 and again in 2023). She is best read as a high-profile, agenda-setting commissioner whose power is focused on environmental, tribal, and workforce-development policy at the Port. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags (defense counter-argument):** A defense would say the Port Commission role is constitutionally independent and nonpartisan. Her outside board positions are public. Her legal practice is separate. Her environmental and tribal focus is policy choice, not a conflict.
