## 0. Why this person or group is on the map <!-- id:boundary --> - **Why Chatham is on the map.** Chatham Asset Management owns The McClatchy Company. McClatchy publishes The News Tribune in Tacoma — the main daily paper for Pierce County. Chatham is based in New Jersey. But its decisions about money, layoffs, and use of AI directly shape what Pierce County residents read about their local government. - **What that gives Chatham.** Two kinds of power. One is ownership of a regional newspaper monopoly. The other is the impact of newsroom cuts on accountability reporting at the local-government level. - **Who is NOT on the map.** McClatchy itself has its own profile. Postmedia is a Canadian chain owned by Chatham but is outside the case area. A360media (also called American Media) merged into McClatchy in December 2024 and is now part of the McClatchy operation. ## 1. Who they are <!-- id:identity tier:1 --> - **Legal name:** Chatham Asset Management, LLC. - **Type of business:** A private hedge-fund manager set up as a New Jersey LLC. - **Headquarters:** 26 Main Street, Chatham Borough, NJ 07928. - **Founded:** 2000 (about 26 years ago, per Wikipedia). - **Founder and owner:** Anthony Melchiorre. He owns about 70% of the firm. He was born around 1968 (per 2023 reports). He grew up near Chicago and now lives in Short Hills, NJ. - **Other key principal:** Larry Buchalter. - **Regulatory history:** In April 2023, the SEC charged Chatham and Melchiorre with improper trading in fixed-income securities tied to American Media / AMI stock. They paid a $19.3 million civil penalty to settle (per NJ Globe and Reuters, April 2023). - **Major companies Chatham owns:** - **The McClatchy Company, LLC.** Private; bought September 4, 2020, out of Chapter 11 bankruptcy for $312 million. Headquartered in Sacramento, California. Runs 30 daily papers across 14 states. Merged with A360media in December 2024 and is now called **McClatchy Media**. - **Postmedia Network Canada Corp.** About 66% control since October 2016. It is Canada's largest newspaper chain. - **A360media, LLC.** Formerly American Media, Inc. Merged into McClatchy in December 2024. - **RR Donnelley.** Printing and distribution company. - **Governing law:** Delaware and New Jersey LLC rules. SEC-registered investment adviser. Where required, it files reports under Section 13 of the Securities Exchange Act (Postmedia is publicly listed in Canada). ## 3. Where their power comes from <!-- id:power-basis --> - **What power does this company hold?** 1. **Control of media markets.** Through McClatchy, Chatham owns Washington's second-largest newspaper (The News Tribune in Tacoma) plus The Olympian, The Bellingham Herald, and the Tri-City Herald. The four biggest McClatchy papers in Washington cover the I-5 corridor from Bellingham down through Pierce County (per NW Labor Press and the Seattle Times). 2. **Power over the newsroom.** Since buying McClatchy in 2020, Chatham has pushed repeated newsroom cuts. The Miami Herald had layoffs (per WFLA). In 2025, The News Tribune, The Olympian, and the Bellingham Herald all had layoffs (per Seattle Times). This affects what gets reported on Pierce County government, schools, Joint Base Lewis-McChord (JBLM), and the Port of Tacoma. 3. **Capital and political economy.** A hedge fund owning regional newspapers plays into the broader decline of local news in America (covered by PEN America and the Notre Dame Local News Index). In 2025 and 2026, Northwest journalists went on strike against McClatchy over the use of AI in place of reporters and over starting pay of about $45,000. - **Key facts about the media side:** - **Ownership chain:** Chatham (NJ) → McClatchy Co. LLC (Sacramento, post-Chapter 11 since September 4, 2020) → The News Tribune (Tacoma). - **Reach:** The News Tribune is "the second-largest newspaper in Washington, covering the local news market of Pierce County in print and online" (per McClatchy's own markets page). It also covers JBLM (one of the largest U.S. military bases) and the Port of Tacoma. - **Editorial direction:** Local, not partisan-national. Investigative capacity has dropped (per Seattle Times reporting on 2025 layoffs and the 2026 strike, which note reporters covering Pierce County government have been cut). ## 3. Who they are <!-- id:assessment --> - **The bottom line.** Chatham is based out of the region but still matters a lot for Pierce County. Their ownership of The News Tribune is the single biggest private factor in whether Pierce County has strong local-government reporting. The 2025 layoffs at The News Tribune (which cut the Tacoma City Hall and Pierce County government beat) and the 2026 strike over AI substitution are direct, documented Chatham-era decisions. The composite power score (0.010) understates that real-world influence. - **Conflicts to watch.** First, the 2023 SEC settlement for improper trading shows Chatham had governance problems. Second, hedge-fund ownership of a regional newspaper of record is a structural mismatch with the civic-journalism mission. Third, the December 2024 merger of McClatchy and A360media (a tabloid publisher) further cut into hard-news capacity. The defense reading: Chatham bought McClatchy out of bankruptcy and saved jobs that would have been lost otherwise. That is partly true. It does not erase the structural critique. - **The counter-argument.** McClatchy might have failed anyway given the long decline of the newspaper industry. Chatham's money stopped an immediate collapse. But the Seattle Times reporting and the 2026 strike suggest that Chatham-era cuts went deeper than the industry-wide decline would predict. - **Confidence:** Medium — corporate ownership, SEC settlement, McClatchy acquisition price, and 2025 layoffs / 2026 strike are primary-sourced; the *counterfactual* (what would have happened absent Chatham) is necessarily INFERENCE.
