## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why he is here:** Mike Griffus holds the top non-elected public-transit executive job in Pierce County. He runs a $154 million budget and about 960 employees. His agency runs buses, paratransit (door-to-door rides for people who cannot use regular buses), and vanpools across 292 square miles of Pierce County (per the United Way of Pierce County bio). He also leads contract talks with Sound Transit and labor talks with ATU 758 (the bus-drivers' union). Note: the assignment text calls him "former Pierce County Exec." Primary sources say he is the Pierce Transit CEO, not the elected County Executive. The elected County Executive since 2025 is Ryan Mello, on a different page. Treat the assignment's wording as a graph-data error. - **Who is NOT on this page:** Ryan Mello (Pierce County Executive) is on a different page. Bruce Dammeier (former Pierce County Executive) is on a different page. Sue Dreier (Griffus's predecessor at Pierce Transit) is on a different page.

## 1. Who they are - **Legal name:** Michael "Mike" Griffus. - **Home:** Based in Kirkland, WA (per RocketReach business directory). Works in Pierce County. - **Schooling:** B.A. in Business Administration from the University of Washington. ## 3. Where their power comes from - **How he got authority:** Appointed CEO of a Public Transportation Benefit Area (PTBA), a special kind of transit district set up under state law (RCW 36.57A). He is not elected. A nine-member Board of Commissioners hires him — eight elected officials and one non-voting labor union representative. He runs day-to-day operations, labor talks, contracts (Sound Transit express bus), and capital grants. - **Budget and contracts:** $154 million a year operating budget (per the United Way bio; the $345 million graph figure likely includes multi-year capital spending). Pierce Transit operates Sound Transit's ST Express bus service between Pierce and King counties. That accounts for about 40% of its bus-operator jobs. - **Key facts:** - **Office:** Pierce Transit CEO (appointed). - **Budget:** About $154 million operating; about 960 full-time employees. - **Veto / appointment power:** None. The Board hires and fires the CEO. - **Where he operates:** A 292-square-mile PTBA in Pierce County, plus Sound Transit contract work into King County.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** Mike Griffus runs a $154 million / 960-employee public-transit authority. He is the chief operational contact for the biggest non-Sound-Transit bus operator in Pierce County. His power is real but limited: the Board is publicly appointed, and he is an executive, not an elected official. The original task description's "former Pierce County Exec" wording appears to be a graph-label error. Many primary sources confirm he is the Pierce Transit CEO, a separate job from the elected Pierce County Executive. - **Conflicts of interest (defense-attorney view):** None found in primary sources. One thing to watch: he came from Keolis and Veolia, both big private transit contractors. If Pierce Transit ever bids Sound Transit work, that past could look like favoritism. No public allegation has been made. - **Other view:** A skeptic would call a transit CEO a functionary, not a power player. The view that elevates Griffus points to two things: (a) the November 2026 Pierce Transit sales-tax ballot measure (a proposed jump from 0.6% to 0.9%) — he is the public face of that campaign; (b) Pierce Transit's contract role with Sound Transit, where he shapes a key regional link. The evidence supports tier3 placement at rank 147. - **Confidence:** High on role, career path, and powers (three independent primary/secondary sources); Medium on personal-political network (no documented civic board seats in primary sources reviewed).
