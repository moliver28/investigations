## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - Deanna Keller is one of five elected commissioners who run the Port of Tacoma. The Port runs marine terminals, a grain elevator, Foreign Trade Zone #86, and Port-owned industrial land. It also runs the South Harbor half of the Northwest Seaport Alliance, a joint port authority. The Port supports over 41,000 jobs, $3.4B in wages, and $10.8B in business output. Keller sits inside the decision-making room on maritime trade, jobs, and the environment. - People NOT on the map: John McCarthy is Commission President on a different seat. Dick Marzano, Don Meyer, Kristin Ang, and JT Wilcox are fellow commissioners, tracked separately. Eric Johnson is the Executive Director, a hired staffer. Tacoma City Council members on the Tideflats Subarea Plan Steering Committee are also adjacent and excluded.

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** Keller is a very well-networked port commissioner. She sits on the WPPA Executive Committee (as Secretary). She also sits on PSRC's two policy boards. She sits on the Pierce County Regional Council and Workforce Central. She sits on the Gig Harbor Rotary. She sits on the Tacoma Tideflats Subarea Plan Steering Committee. She is Vice-Chair of the Joint Municipal Action Committee. Her Audit Committee chair role gives her direct oversight over the Port's books. Her NWSA Audit co-chair role does the same for the Seaport Alliance. That kind of oversight power is rare for public port commissions in Washington. Her Marine Corps service in the "President's Own" Marine Band is rare too. Her time as Kel-Tech Plastics CEO adds business credibility. Her career arc (schoolteacher → principal → K-12 administrator → small-business CEO → port commissioner) is a classic Pierce County bipartisan mix. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** - Kel-Tech Plastics supplier ties from her CEO years could overlap with current Port deals. Her 2008-2020 tenure predates her 2024-27 term, but the gap is small. - As a Marine veteran and advocate for "Place for Jobs" (workforce development), Keller is a natural ally of the shipyard and industrial bloc. She could shape deals at the Port's marine terminals. - Counter-argument: one port commissioner out of five is not the deciding voice. Her audit role is a watchdog job, not a strategy job. - **Analysis of alternatives:** A skeptical reading treats Keller as one vote among five. Big decisions (capital projects, executive director hires, Seaport Alliance policy) are usually unanimous or near-unanimous. The opposing reading (favored here) is that her many board seats make her a connector who passes decisions and information across the region's biggest bodies. The Audit Committee chair role is the most underrated lever: in a budget this size, the audit chair sees every contract before it goes public. - **Confidence:** High — name, term, board seats, and military service verified across the Port of Tacoma commissioner page and the WPPA About page.
