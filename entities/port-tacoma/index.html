## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - Port of Tacoma is on the map because it holds power through office, makes big decisions, and drives the local economy. It is a public port district under state law (RCW) Title 53. It works as an independent city-like government with elected commissioners. It runs container terminals, industrial property, and rail-truck interchanges. Through the Northwest Seaport Alliance (NWSA), it shares control of one of the largest container ports in North America. Its commissioners are elected county-wide, which gives it direct democratic legitimacy. Its board members also sit on the Chamber of Commerce and the Economic Development Board (EDB). It hires ILWU Local 23 longshore workers. - **Groups not profiled here.** Port of Seattle — separate port in King County, but connected through NWSA. NWSA is profiled as its own node (`nwsa`) — it is a Port Development Authority (PDA), not a port district. The Washington Public Ports Association is a trade group, not a power node. Foss Waterway Development Authority is a subsidiary.

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Legal authority:** RCW 53.08 gives port districts broad powers. They can buy and run terminals, build industrial sites, levy taxes, issue bonds, set rates, and manage real estate. The Port of Tacoma uses these powers on the Tacoma Tideflats — one of the largest industrial waterfronts on the West Coast. [Source #3] - **Economic engine:** Through NWSA, the Port co-runs cargo operations that handled nearly $68 billion in waterborne trade with 175 trading partners in 2025. NWSA facilities support over 52,000 jobs and $14 billion in business output for Washington state. [Source #11] - **Taxing authority:** Port districts can levy property taxes under RCW 53.36 and 53.40. The graph shows a property-tax-levy edge with the County Council (weight 5). The Port's 2025 budget includes the legal budget and tax levy adopted Nov 19, 2024. [Source #1, #4] - **Land-use power:** The Port controls industrial land use on the Tideflats — zoning, leasing, and environmental permits. The graph edge to Communities for a Healthy Bay (oversees, weight 4) points to environmental review work. [graph] - **Key facts:** - Offices and authority: 5 elected commissioners plus an appointed executive director; independent city-like government [Source #1] - Budget: 2025 budget adopted Nov 19, 2024; includes a 2024 staffing budget of $45.8 million ($12M salaried + $17.8M hourly + benefits) [Source #4] - Average salary: $129,469 (excluding the Executive Director) [Source #4] - Powers: The Commission hires the executive director, sets policy, and approves major spending [Source #1] - Jurisdictions: Port district covers Pierce County; commissioners are elected at-large countywide [Source #1]

## 1. Who they are - **Verdict:** Port of Tacoma controls one of the largest container-port complexes on the U.S. West Coast. Its power comes from five elected commissioners, elected at-large countywide. It also comes from an appointed executive director. All work under RCW Title 53 as an independent city-like government. Its power rests on four sources. (1) Legal power over port operations, real estate, and taxation. This is under RCW 53.08, 53.36, and 53.40. (2) Economic scale. Through NWSA, the Port moves $68B in waterborne trade, supports 52,000+ jobs, and generates $14B in state business output. (3) Property-tax levy authority. The Port is one of the few Pierce County bodies that can tax property directly. (4) Board overlaps. The ED sits on Chamber and EDB boards. The CFO came from Weyerhaeuser, where she worked 23 years. Commissioner Marzano is a former ILWU Local 23 president. Commissioner Wilcox is a former state House Minority Leader. The NWSA structure splits governance with Port of Seattle. It creates both shared power and potential conflict over resources. - **Conflicts of interest (defense view):** The defense would say port operations are open. Commissioners are elected. Meetings are public under the Open Public Meetings Act. Budgets are published. NWSA is a rational regional model, not a power grab. Marzano's ILWU background gives labor a voice in port governance. That is democratic representation, not conflict. Property-tax levies are set by law and reviewed through commissioner elections. The Port's economic contribution ($14B output, 52K jobs) supports its tax authority. - **Counter-reading:** Port of Tacoma could be seen as unaccountable economic governance — an independent city-like body with taxing power that is not under Pierce County government. At-large elections mean commissioners answer to all ~900K county residents, most of whom have no direct stake in Tideflats operations. The ILWU-Marzano link creates a structural labor bias. The NWSA structure means Tacoma's port revenue is shared with Seattle. The mix of former legislators (Wilcox), labor leaders (Marzano), and corporate alumni (Galeno/Weyerhaeuser) on Port governance creates an insider network that may resist outside scrutiny. - **Confidence:** High.
