## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - **Why included:** Holds office + makes decisions + well-connected — East Pierce Fire & Rescue (EPFR / Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 22) is one of the two largest career fire districts in Pierce County (the other is Central Pierce Fire & Rescue). It has legal power over firefighting, emergency medical services, marine rescue on Lake Tapps, and fire code enforcement for 115,000+ residents across 153 square miles. It is run by an elected Board of Fire Commissioners. It collects voter-approved property tax levies ($1.50 per $1,000 fire levy approved by voters in 2024 + $0.41 per $1,000 EMS levy). (Sources 1, 2, 3, 6) - **Adjacent but NOT included:** Central Pierce Fire & Rescue (peer district serving the urban core). Tacoma Fire Department (city fire dept). West Pierce Fire & Rescue (a separate district covering west county). Buckley Fire Department (Wilkeson left East Pierce in 2014 to contract with Buckley). ## 1. Who they are - **Legal name:** Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 22, doing business as "East Pierce Fire & Rescue" (EPFR). (Sources 1, 2) - **Type:** Washington fire protection district. This is a special-purpose district set up under state law RCW Title 52. RCW 52.04 covers fire districts. RCW 52.33 covers Standards of Cover. An elected Board of Fire Commissioners runs it. (Sources 1, 2, 5) - **When it formed:** Merged in 2000 (effective Jan 1, 2000) from City of Bonney Lake Fire Department + Pierce County Fire District 22 (Lake Tapps) + Pierce County Fire District 24. On Nov 2, 1999, voters approved the merger (7,636 yes to 1,332 no). The three agencies had already been consolidated under the name "East Pierce Fire & Rescue" since 1995. (Sources 1, 2) - **Later annexations:** - 2006: Pierce County Fire District 12 (South Prairie/Fire District 20) - 2008: Sumner/Fire District 1 - 2010: Edgewood/Fire District 8 - 2013: Milton Fire Department - 2014: Wilkeson ended its contract (now with Buckley FD) (Sources 1, 2) - **HQ address:** 8601 Main Street East, Bonney Lake, WA 98391. (Sources 2, 3) - **Operating budget (2024):** $59,865,333 budgeted. $58,022,515 in actual revenues. $51,991,391 in actual expenditures (and $52,109,920 in a second count — slight underrun per the 2024 Annual Report). (Source 4) - **Funding model:** EPFR stays within the state-law maximum levy rate of $2.00 per $1,000 of assessed property value (Fire Levy $1.50 + EMS Levy $0.50 max). As of 2024, EPFR is the only major Pierce County fire district operating WITHOUT a Maintenance & Operations (M&O) levy or a Benefit Charge that would push rates above $2.00. Most career fire districts in WA collect over $3.00 per $1,000. (Sources 4, 6) - **Fire levy history:** 2024 — voters approved restoring the fire levy to $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value after several years of compression below the state maximum. EMS levy $0.41 per $1,000 of assessed value, renewed in 2021 for 10 years. (Source 6) ## 3. Where their power comes from - **State law power (government side):** Provides firefighting, EMS, marine rescue on Lake Tapps, fire code enforcement, and hazardous materials response for 115,000+ residents across 153 sq mi. Run by an elected Board of Fire Commissioners (RCW 52.14). (Sources 1, 2) - **Budget power:** About $59 million in annual operating budget (2024). Most of it comes from two voter-approved property tax levies (Fire + EMS). (Sources 4, 6) - **Levy power (taxing):** Voters approved the fire levy restoration to $1.50 per $1,000 in 2024. This is state-law power given by Pierce County voters to the District. It does not come from the County Council — it is direct voter power. (Source 6) - **Workforce size:** EPFR cross-trains all full-time firefighters as either EMTs (emergency medical technicians) or paramedics. There are 9 fire stations (8 staffed + 1 marine rescue). The exact uniformed staff count is UNVERIFIED. (Source 2) - **Where power crosses domains:** Government (special-purpose district) + economic (levy collection, capital facilities) + institutional (mutual aid agreements with nearby fire and EMS agencies). - **Relationship with the Pierce County Council:** EPFR's taxing power comes directly from district voters, NOT from the Pierce County Council. The Council's relationship with EPFR is indirect (boundary annexations, interlocal agreements). The map edge "taxing" between EPFR and the Pierce County Council is INACCURATE — EPFR's main taxing power is direct from voters under RCW 52. (Source 6) — see Section 12 alternative reading. ## 3. Who they are - **Verdict:** East Pierce Fire & Rescue has state-law power as Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 22. It provides firefighting, EMS, marine rescue on Lake Tapps, and code enforcement for 115,000+ residents across 153 sq mi. It runs 9 fire stations and a $59 million annual budget. The District stays within the state-law combined levy cap of $2.00 per $1,000 — making it the only major Pierce County fire district without a third revenue stream (M&O levy or Benefit Charge). Voters approved restoring the fire levy to $1.50 per $1,000 in 2024. They renewed the EMS levy in 2021 for 10 years. - **Conflicts-of-interest flags:** The map edge between EPFR and the Pierce County Council labeled "taxing" is INACCURATE in the strict state-law sense. EPFR's taxing power comes directly from district voters under RCW 52, not from the County Council. The Council's role is limited to interlocal agreements and boundary matters. An opposing reading might note that the Council can still affect District operations through budget cycles, regional coordination, and boundary annexation review — but the direct taxing power is voter-controlled. (FACT — Source 6) - **Analysis of alternatives:** A defense reading: EPFR is a high-service, low-cost district. A restrained levy rate plus full staffing delivers services below the cost of peer districts. An opposing reading: running at the legal minimum levy cap with no M&O revenue makes EPFR always vulnerable to revenue compression. State law caps levy growth at 1% per year unless voters approve a higher rate. That pushes the district into periodic ballot-measure battles when costs outstrip revenue. The 2024 fire-levy restoration is exactly this dynamic. - **Population discrepancy (Source 2 lists ~100,500; Sources 1 and 3 list "over 115,000"):** The 100,500 figure appears on the Contact page. The 115,000 figure appears on About and Fire Stations pages. This likely reflects recent expansion or methodology (residents vs. service population).
