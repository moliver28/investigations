## 0. Why this person or group is on the map - Saltchuk Resources is the parent company of the Foss family of companies. It connects to Pierce County through TOTE Maritime Alaska, which runs ships between Tacoma and Anchorage. Foss Maritime also operates tug and barge boats along the Pacific Northwest coast. - Saltchuk is on the map because it is well-connected and makes major decisions. The Foss family controls the company, so Saltchuk links Pierce County's port economy to a private family business empire based in Seattle. - Not on the map separately: Crowley Maritime, Matson Navigation, and the Foss family as individuals (they are covered under `foss-family`).

## 1. Who they are - **Full name:** Saltchuk Resources, Inc. The public brand is "Saltchuk." - **Type of company:** A private family holding company. Founded in 1982 in Seattle, WA. - **Headquarters:** 450 Alaskan Way S, Seattle, WA 98104. - **Ownership:** The Foss/Tabbutt family owns Saltchuk. Mark Tabbutt is the Chairman. Denise Tabbutt is the Principal Shareholder. Timothy Engle is on the board. Michele Seaver was part of the family ownership group until she passed away. - **Structure:** Saltchuk is the parent. It owns seven lines of business. These include Saltchuk Marine, Saltchuk Logistics, Saltchuk Aviation, NorthStar Energy, TOTE Group, Tropical, and OSG. Those lines run brands like Foss Maritime, TOTE Maritime Alaska and Puerto Rico, Carlile Transportation, Northern Air Cargo, and Tropical Shipping. - **Governing law:** Washington state corporate law. Saltchuk is private, so it does not file with the SEC (the Securities and Exchange Commission). - **Size:** In 2023, the company had about $4.85 billion in revenue and around 7,600 employees (about 761 in Washington state, the rest elsewhere). More recent reports put revenue near $5.5 to $6 billion and total employees near 10,000.

## 3. Where their power comes from - **Market power:** Saltchuk holds a strong position in shipping to Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, the Caribbean, and Florida. It runs Jones Act ships (a U.S. law that requires goods moved between U.S. ports to be carried on U.S.-built, U.S.-flagged, and U.S.-crewed ships). TOTE Maritime Alaska carries about one-third of the cargo on the Alaska Railbelt route. - **Tacoma link:** TOTE Maritime Alaska has its office at 909 A Street, Suite 1100, Tacoma, WA 98402. The Port of Tacoma is home to a major Saltchuk presence. Saltchuk is a key counterparty on port leases and labor deals with the longshore union (ILWU) and the Inlandboatmen's Union. - **Pierce County jobs:** About 761 Saltchuk employees work in Washington state. - **Capture risk:** Because Saltchuk is private, it does not file public reports on investments, lobbying, or political donations. Its political influence runs through industry groups like the American Association of Port Authorities (AAPA) and the Chamber of Maritime Commerce.

## 12. Bottom line - **The bottom line.** Saltchuk is a private Seattle family company. It dominates shipping to Alaska, Hawaii, and the Caribbean. It runs Tacoma's TOTE Maritime Alaska operation. Its 2023 revenue was $4.85 billion. Its Tacoma office is a major port-side counterparty. - **Why it matters for Pierce County.** The Tacoma to Anchorage trade lane moves through the Port of Tacoma. Foss Maritime operates along the whole Pacific Northwest coast. Any port or labor deal at the Port of Tacoma runs through Saltchuk's tenants. - **The counter-view.** A defender would say Saltchuk has no Pierce County political role. It does not file public lobbying or campaign-finance reports. Its tier-3 score (0.006) reflects that. The acquisition of Great Lakes Dredge & Dock brings dredging in-house, but that is normal for a family holding company. - **The open question.** The 44,000-member figure may overstate active political engagement. Specific contracts and lobbying activity remain unverified. - **Confidence:** Medium. Four sources confirm ownership, size, Tacoma presence, and family control. No public filings exist to confirm revenue and employee counts beyond what is in secondary press.
