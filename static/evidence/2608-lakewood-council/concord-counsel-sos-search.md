## What This Means

Under Washington law:
- **LLCs and corporations** MUST register with the Secretary of State
- **Sole proprietorships** do NOT need to register with the SOS (only file a DBA/trade name with the county or DOR)

If "Concord Counsel" is not registered with the SOS, it is almost certainly a **sole proprietorship** operating under a trade name.

## Legal Significance for the F-1 Issue

The PDC F-1 instructions require disclosure of income from:
> "A partnership, joint venture, **sole proprietorship**, associations, unions, or other business or commercial entity" — **if $2,400 or more**

(emphasis added — source: pdc.wa.gov F-1 instructions)

Since Concord Counsel is apparently a sole proprietorship:
- **Lindholm cannot argue** that Concord Counsel income is "business revenue, not personal income" — for a sole proprietorship, they are the same thing
- The $2,400 threshold still applies, but sole proprietors have LESS ability to legally avoid reporting (no corporate veil, no owner draw vs. salary distinction)
- **This strengthens the F-1 omission finding.** If Concord Counsel generates any meaningful revenue, Lindholm was required to disclose it

## Counter-Argument

If Concord Counsel is a sole proprietorship that generated less than $2,400 in personal income during the reporting period, no disclosure was required. We still don't know Concord Counsel's revenue.

**Bottom line:** The SOS non-finding eliminates one defense Lindholm could have used (the "LLC corporate veil" argument) but doesn't resolve the $2,400 threshold question.
