## Why this recount exists

The investigation's original count included Bocchi (Pierce County), Belle (City of Kent), and Talbo (City of Renton) — but excluded **Pearson** (also Pierce County) and **Lauricella** (South Sound 911) without any documented rationale, while the peer-city comparison counted school-district employees, state-agency employees, and a state legislator in other cities. Pearson's omission is internally inconsistent (same employer class as Bocchi), and a count that omits classes of dual employment in one city while peers include them cannot support a comparison. This file pins the rule and recounts **both sides** under it.

## The counting rule (written; applied to both sides)

A council member counts as **dual-government employed** if they currently hold a **paid position — employment or elected office — with a government entity other than the city they serve**, including: counties, other cities, state agencies, school districts, and special-purpose public agencies (public authorities, transit/911 agencies). **Excluded:** former or retired roles; unpaid board and commission seats (tracked separately); private-sector employment.

## The one judgment call — stated explicitly

**South Sound 911** is a tax-funded public authority with a member-city board — a special-purpose government. Under the inclusive rule already applied to peers (which counted school districts and state agencies), it counts. If a strict variant excludes special-purpose agencies **on both sides**, Lakewood is **4 of 7 (57%)** and the peer maximum falls to 14%. **The outlier verdict is unchanged under every variant** (see below).
