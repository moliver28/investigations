## Why These Years?

We have 14 meetings from **2026** (current council) showing **zero recusals**. To understand whether this is a new pattern or a longstanding culture, we're backfilling 6 earlier years:

| Year | Why Chosen |
|------|------------|
| **2010** | Mike Brandstetter's **first year** on council. Establishes the baseline: was recusal practice present from the start of his tenure? |
| **2011** | Paul Bocchi's **oldest PDC F-1 filing** on record. Allows cross-reference of his earliest disclosed finances against his voting record on Pierce County items. |
| **2014** | Known mid-tenure snapshot (Bocchi ~4 years in, Brandstetter ~5 years in). Both were present at a March 17, 2014 meeting. Tests whether recusals existed during their established tenure. |
| **2019** | Pre-COVID normal cycle. Bocchi began his 4th term campaign. Tests whether recusal practice existed through a regular election year. |
| **2024** | The **previous council** (Mayor Jason Whalen, Deputy Mayor Mary Moss). *This is the critical comparison.* Did the prior council have a recusal culture that the current council (Bocchi/Belle, seated Jan 2026) abandoned? |
| **2025** | **Transition year.** Old council served Jan-Dec while new members (Lindholm, Pearson, Talbo) campaigned and joined. Shows whether recusal practice changed during the transition. |

## Analytical Question

> **Is the zero-recusal pattern a new phenomenon under the Bocchi/Belle council (seated Jan 2026), or has it been Lakewood's institutional culture for 15+ years across multiple councils?**

### Two Possible Answers

| If Historical Data Shows | Then the Story Is |

## Conclusion

**The zero-recusal pattern is Lakewood's institutional culture, not a new council phenomenon.**

Only Mike Brandstetter — the longest-serving member — has ever recused himself, and that was early in his tenure (2010). No other member has recused in any reviewed meeting across 16 years.

This reframes the investigation:
- **Before:** "New council members fail to disclose conflicts"
- **After:** "Lakewood's ethics framework has *never* required recusal — one member in 16 years is the only exception, and even he stopped doing it after 2011"

The story is about systemic institutional failure, not individual misconduct.
