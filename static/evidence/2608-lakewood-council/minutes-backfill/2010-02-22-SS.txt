Review of t he proposed Driving Wit h License Suspended Program.

Officer Scott Novasky and Assistant City Attorney Mike McKenzie reviewed the proposed driving with license suspended program with
regard to towing of vehicles.

Discussion ensued relative to retaining a vehicle for those without insurance; how many vehicles would be impounded if this ordinance
was adopted and how would it impact Court and Police staff; why did Lakewood not implement this earlier and why the State Patrol no
longer implements this program; possibly changing the proposed ordinance from ?posting a five-day notice? to ?five days from receipt? of
the notice before the sale of the vehicle at auction; what is the priority order of recouping the costs for removal, towing and storage;
how difficult is it to obtain an occupational driving license; what strategy would be used to inform the public; and the option to impose this
program for driving with license suspended for second and third degree offenses only.

Speaking before the Council was:

Dennis Haugen, Lakewood resident, spoke in support of the proposed driving with license suspended program.

City Council Study Session Minutes -3- February 22, 2010

City Manager Neiditz reported that interviews will be held for the Court Administrator position.

City Manager Neiditz reported that a training ?burn? on a City-owned property on Pacific Highway will be conducted by the Fire
Department on February 24, 2010.

A volunteer workshop, ?Pain behind the badge,? sponsored by the Police Department was held last week.

City Manager Neiditz indicated that the National Law Enforcement memorial will be held in May and that he is planning to honor the
request for the families of the four fallen officers, and the officers and honor guards who escorted the families, to attend the memorial in
Washington, D.C.

City Manager Neiditz reported that the Lakewood Water District is requesting that the City maintain over 1,900 hydrants at the cost of
$100 per hydrant, amounting to approximately $195,000 annually.

City Manager Neiditz spoke about reducing $500,000 of General Funding spending in 2010 by eliminating a couple of positions in
administration and Community Development, cutting back on professional services, computer expenditures, reducing Public Works
spending, and the vehicle replacement fund and, with the four vacancies in the Police Department, two of the four positions would
continue with hiring and the remaining two will be delayed until the third quarter of 2010.

Deputy Mayor Anderson suggested that the City engage in a discussion with the Fire District, Lakewood Water District and the City
relative to the maintenance costs of fire hydrants.

Councilmember Neary asked for a list of the budget reductions and their amounts. He then expressed concern about the delayed hiring
of two police officers.

Mayor Richardson asked if furloughs were considered.

City Manager Neiditz commented that a meeting was held with the Washington State Department of Transportation (WSDOT) and senior
staff from the Rail Department. He added that there was also discussion with the Utilities Transportation Commission (UTC) and that, of
the seven rail crossings, four involves the Sounder Service. He indicated that the UTC would hold a public hearing and that there would
be a vehicle for discussion rather than outright litigation. He explained that the WSDOT indicated that they need to do more public
outreach in Lakewood.
