2014 10 27 Study Session Minutes
LAKEWOOD CITY COUNCIL
STUDY SESSION MINUTES
Monday, October 27, 2014
City of Lakewood
City Council Chambers
6000 Main Street SW
Lakewood, WA 98499
CALL TO ORDER
Mayor Anderson called the meeting to order at 7:02 p.m.
ROLL CALL
Councilmembers Present: 7 – Mayor Don Anderson; Deputy Mayor Jason Whalen; Councilmembers Mary Moss, Mike
Brandstetter, John Simpson, Marie Barth and Paul Bocchi.
Lakewood’s Promise Advisory Board Members Present: 6- Chair Clayton DeNault; Board Members Elvin Bucu, Mary
Dodsworth, Lonnie Howard, Judi Weldy and Ellie Wilson.
ITEMS FOR DISCUSSION:
Joint Lakewood’s Promise Advisory Board meeting.
City Council and Lakewood’s Promise Advisory Board Members introduced themselves.
Chair DeNault reviewed the major accomplishments of the Board. Lakewood’s Promise Coordinator Kurt Sample provided an
overview of the Lakewood’s Promise website showing a map depicting where early learning programs are offered in
Lakewood, where 6-11 age children’s activities, teen activities, volunteer opportunities, and youth activities are occurring in
Lakewood. Board Member Bucu spoke about last year’s efforts being focused on safe places by creating an inventory and
afterschool late night programs. Board Member Wilson spoke about the “caring adult” promise by providing a caring adult for
serving as mentors and tutors. Board Member Weldy spoke about the healthy start task force who are focusing on healthy
eating and eliminating food waste. Board Member Howard spoke about educational outcomes at the secondary level, and how
to blend work force development with economic development. Board Member Dodsworth spoke about utilizing the Youth
Council in the Five Promises.
Discussion ensued on how does the work force development with economic development program work; creating a web page
open to youth as an introduction to invite other youths to higher education sites; providing for the ability to track “safe places” on
the Promise website; involving the Police Department in the safe place programs; what synergy will the new Harrison
Preparatory School have with being in close proximity to Clover Park Technical College; potentially using public buildings (ie
schools, etc.) as safe place facilities; and what is considered a successful safe place.
**********
Council recessed at 8:10 p.m. and reconvened at 8:16 p.m.
**********
Review of the 2014 Comprehensive Plan amendments.
Assistant City Manager Bugher provided the Council with copies of the 2014 Comprehensive Plan details. He reviewed the
Planning Advisory Board’s seven recommendations consisting of the Ruby Drive Apartments, Pacific Highway zoning
changes, Department of Transportation property, Wards Lake Park, Springbrook Park expansion, Pacific Highway SW
property, and Chapters 2, 3, 5, 7 of the Comprehensive Plan. He reviewed the key points of the proposed Comprehensive Plan
amendments.
Discussion ensued on how the 2014 Comprehensive Plan and visioning process helps to build on the 2015 Comprehensive

Plan; what kind of agencies might raise concerns about the housing section of the Comprehensive Plan; possibly including an
affirmative statement for maintaining low income housing; and is there liability when a developer has a vested building permit
and its zoning is changed.
Review of the 2014 budget amendments.
Assistant City Manager for Finance & Administrative Services Kraus reviewed the 2014 budget amendments including
revenue increases of $23,000 and an increase of $181,000 in expenditures. She then highlighted the General Fund, Fund 102
Street capital, Fund 180 Narcotics Seizure, Fund 181 Felony Seizure; Fund 195 Public Safety grants, Fund 401 Surface Water
Management, and Fund 502 City Hall service changes.
Discussion ensued on what is the basis for the Bridgeport Way Street capital fund project.
Review of the 2015 property tax levy.
Assistant City Manager for Finance & Administrative Services Kraus reviewed the 2015 property tax levy that includes a 1.5
percent increase.
Discussion ensued on whether the property tax increase was reflected in the proposed 2015-2016 biennial budget; what
constitutes the State assessed property estimates by the Department of Revenue; and what can the City propose for an AWC
legislative agenda strategy relative to property tax revenues.
2015-2016 Proposed Biennial Budget Department Presentations.
• Police
Assistant City Manager for Finance & Administrative Services Kraus provided an overview of the Police Department budget.
Assistant Police Chief Zaro reviewed the proposed changes to the Community Safety Resource Team (CSRT) which is
proposed to be led by Lt. Hoffman.
Discussion ensued on what metrics are being used for maintaining the same level of service with reductions in CSRT staff;
what are the approved metrics that the CSRT performance measurements will be based on; concerns that have been
expressed at neighborhood association meetings about the loss of Community Service Officers (CSOs) and the knowledge
CSOs have about their neighborhoods; Tillicum’s concerns about increases in property crimes; adding a performance
measurement on the number of citizens’ contact made and measuring the response time and mechanism for providing
feedback; is the $35,000 General Fund dollars for the CDBG Program Manager a transfer or enhancement of services; and are
there grants that are sunsetting in 2014.
Assistant City Manager for Finance & Administrative Services Kraus reviewed the position inventory/personnel costs.
Further discussion ensued on the Police overtime budget and if off duty police services provided by third parties are reflected
in the budget; is the administrative charge for off duty police services reflected in the budget; is there an “apples to apples”
comparison of Police benefits from last year; and what does the professional services budget include (photo enforcement,
Humane Society contract, background checks, fingerprinting, etc.).
Assistant Police Chief reviewed the command section, patrol section, criminal investigations, specialty units, and professional
standards budgets.
Further discussion ensued on what is the staffing level for each patrol unit; what is the financial burden to Lakewood for nonmutual aid services; and is there a budgeted item for hospital/medical expenses when medical services are rendered for
someone in police custody.
* Municipal Court
Assistant City Manager for Finance & Administrative Services Kraus provided an overview of the Municipal Court budget.
Judge Blinn summarized the Court’s accomplishments this past year and key projects for 2015-2016.
Discussion ensued on the data for a veterans court; and the types of defendants serving in the work crew program.
Assistant City Manager for Finance & Administrative Services Kraus reviewed the position inventory/personnel costs.
Discussion ensued on the cost for translation services; what are the job responsibilities for Court Specialist I and II; and can

Lakewood provide assistance with video arraignments with Nisqually Jail that can be used by other jurisdictions.
