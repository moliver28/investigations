# Expanded Peer City Comparison: What "Good" Looks Like

> **RESOLVED (2026-10-04, L016 → E046):** recounted under a written counting rule — Lakewood 5 of 7 (71%) vs a 0–29% peer range. "43%" figures herein are superseded; see `dual-government-recount.md`.
## Tacoma, Federal Way, and Kirkland vs. Lakewood's Ethics Vacuum

**Prepared**: August 17, 2026  
**Investigation**: INV-2026-001 (2608-lakewood-council)  
**Purpose**: Establish the "good" end of the ethics spectrum by comparing Lakewood to cities that DO have independent ethics enforcement, then answer: is Lakewood's ethics vacuum a choice or an inevitability for a city its size?

---

## Executive Summary

**ANSWER: LAKEWOOD'S ETHICS VACUUM IS A CHOICE, NOT AN INEVITABILITY.**

The evidence is conclusive. Cities of comparable size and governance form (code cities) have voluntarily adopted independent ethics enforcement — Kirkland (pop 92K) has an independent Ethics Officer; Federal Way (pop 99K) has a 3-member Board of Ethics with a formal Code of Ethics. Even Tacoma (pop 222K, a charter city with more structural flexibility) demonstrates the baseline: a functioning Board of Ethics that receives and processes complaints, even if many are dismissed.

The variation is NOT a function of city size, government form, or available resources. It is a function of political will.

**Key finding**: Among all 9 cities examined (the original 5 peers + these 3 expanded), Lakewood is unique in a combination that no other city shares: 
1. **Zero independent ethics enforcement body** — unlike Lynnwood, Federal Way, Kirkland, and Tacoma
2. **Highest dual-government employment rate (71%; 5 of 7)** — versus a peer range of 0–29%
3. **Longest documented zero-recusal record (16+ years, only 1 known recusal)** — even cities without ethics boards have documented recusals

The cost of establishing a basic ethics board is negligible (Lynnwood's uses citizen volunteers; Kirkland's independent Ethics Officer is a contracted position; Federal Way's board meets as-needed). The absence is therefore political, not financial.

## 1. TACOMA (~222K population)

**Government form**: Charter city (first-class), council-manager form, 9 members (5 district + 3 at-large + mayor)  
**Comparability**: MEDIUM — charter city (more structural flexibility than code city), but the closest large-city comparison in Pierce County

### Ethics Enforcement Body: Board of Ethics
- **Established**: June 27, 2006 (Ordinance 27504)
- **Structure**: 5 regular members (City residents), 3-year terms, appointed by City Council on recommendation of Government Performance and Finance Committee
- **Legal basis**: Tacoma Municipal Code Chapter 1.46
- **Staff support**: City Clerk's Office
- **Meets**: Quarterly (second Wednesday), with special meetings as needed
- **Authority**: Receives, investigates, and makes recommendations on complaints against the City Manager, Director of Public Utilities, PUB members, board/commission members, and ALL city elected officials. Can issue advisory opinions.
- **Judicial review**: Board decisions appealable to Pierce County Superior Court (TMC 1.46.045)
- **Required annual report**: Yes — Board must report summary of decisions and opinions to City Council each year (TMC 1.46.045(C)(4))

### Recusal Culture
- Tacoma's voting records show a consistent pattern of **recorded abstentions** (the Voting Record PDFs track "Ayes, Nays, Absent, and Abstaining" for every vote). In the sampled voting records (Feb 2025, Dec 2024), abstentions were 0 on routine votes.
- However, the Lonergan case (2011) shows that when a conflict was discovered POST-VOTE (rather than self-identified as a recusal), the Board investigated and found a violation. This means Tacoma has after-the-fact enforcement even if pre-vote recusal culture is not strong.
- No evidence of systematic recusal practice. Tacoma appears closer to the peer norm (low recusal frequency) but with the important difference that violations get investigated.

5. **Lower dual-government rate** (11% vs 71%)
6. **BUT** — The Board often lacks quorum (multiple meeting cancellations in 2024-2026), suggesting the structure exists but is under-resourced or under-participated.

## 2. FEDERAL WAY (~99K population)

**Government form**: Mayor-council code city, 7 members  
**Comparability**: HIGH — code city (same as Lakewood), comparable population, nearby (King County, south of Seattle)

### Ethics Enforcement Body: Board of Ethics
- **Established**: As part of the Federal Way Code of Ethics (2020) — a comprehensive standalone document applying to councilmembers, mayor, AND all employees
- **Structure**: 3 regular members + 1 first alternate + 1 second alternate, appointed by City Council
- **Current membership**: 
  - Position 1: **VACANT**
  - Position 2: Karen Kirkpatrick
  - Position 3: Douglas Stensby
  - Alternate 1: John Cameron
  - Alternate 2: Kristine Gray
- **Meetings**: "As needed" — no regular meeting schedule
- **Authority**: Issue advisory opinions on Code of Ethics provisions; investigate and report to City Council on alleged violations
- **Subpoena power**: Yes — the Code of Ethics grants the Board subpoena power for investigations (Section 7)
- **Complaint process** (Section 7.4): Written complaint to City Clerk with specific facts, signed under penalty of perjury. Board determines sufficiency, then investigates if warranted.
- **Independent counsel**: The Board may retain independent legal counsel outside the City Attorney's office.

### Code of Ethics Content (Key Provisions)
- **Section 4 — Conflict of Interest**: Expressly prohibits activities in conflict with official duties. Includes influencing contract selection, using position for personal gain, accepting improper gifts.
- **Section 4 — Remote Interest**: If an elected official has only a "remote interest" (defined per RCW 42.23.040), they must disclose it in official minutes AND recuse themselves from voting. "All action taken...shall be by a vote sufficient for the purpose without counting the vote of the Elected Official having the remote interest."
- **Section 5 — Disclosure**: Annual financial disclosure required.
- **Section 7.8**: Board members reimbursed for reasonable expenses.
- **Section 3 — Recusal**: Explicitly codified — council members must recuse for remote interests; the vote proceeds without them.

### Complaint Activity
- No publicly available docket of complaints found on city website. The Ethics Board page shows meeting packets/agendas but links to an empty repository.
- The Board has had at least one **vacant position** (Position 1), suggesting the Board may not be fully active.
- The "as needed" meeting schedule and vacancy suggest **minimal or no recent complaint activity**.

### Recusal Culture
- **The Code explicitly requires recusal** for remote interests — this is stronger than Lakewood's silence on the matter.
- **February 2026**: Councilmember Martin Moore was removed as Council President in a 4-3 vote. The stated reasons included concerns about "conflicts of interest." The Federal Way Mirror reported: "Section 4 of the Federal Way City Council Code of Ethics describes how to respond to a potential conflict of interest and does not prohibit council members from retaining positions of leadership in local organizations."
- The debate over Moore's removal shows active public and council-level discussion about conflicts of interest — a level of scrutiny absent in Lakewood.

### Key Differences from Lakewood
1. **HAS a Board of Ethics** (3 members, with subpoena power) (Lakewood: none)
2. **HAS a detailed, standalone Code of Ethics** (2020) with explicit recusal requirements (Lakewood: relies on generic state law)
3. **HAS had active public debate about conflicts of interest** (Moore removal, 2026) (Lakewood: no comparable public debate)
4. **BUT** — One board position is vacant (functionality may be limited)
5. **BUT** — Dual-government rate (29%) is also high — 2x the peer average — suggesting the ethics code hasn't solved the underlying governance design issue
6. **BUT** — No documented formal ethics complaints processed (Board may be dormant)

## 3. KIRKLAND (~92K population)

**Government form**: Council-manager code city, 7 at-large members  
**Comparability**: HIGH — code city (same as Lakewood), similar population, King County

### Ethics Enforcement Structure: Independent Ethics Officer
- **Structure**: Not a board — an **independent, contracted Ethics Officer**
- **Current Ethics Officer**: Gary McLean (GMMD Consulting) — independent contractor, not a city employee
- **Legal basis**: Kirkland Municipal Code Chapter 3.14 (Code of Ethics)
- **Complaint process**: Formal written complaint to City Clerk, visibly labeled "For the Ethics Officer." Must set forth specific facts with precision and detail, signed under penalty of perjury (RCW 5.50.050).
- **Authority**: Issues advisory opinions, investigates complaints, determines dismissal or resolution
- **Dismissal authority**: Ethics Officer shall dismiss if violation is "inadvertent and minor" or if no violation occurred
- **Backup system**: City Ombud program (Deputy City Manager James Lopez) handles general complaints about city personnel; could serve as entry point for ethics concerns
- **Annual disclosures**: KMC 3.14.040 requires ALL city officials to file annual disclosures of investments, real property, income, and creditors

### Key Content of KMC Chapter 3.14
- **3.14.030**: Prohibits use of public resources not available to the general public; allows infrequent use at little/no cost to city
- **3.14.060-.080**: Creates the Ethics Officer position and complaint process
- **3.14.040**: Annual financial disclosure requirement for all city officials
- Coverage: Applies to councilmembers, boards, and commissions (KMC 3.14)
- The code notably does NOT cover city employees (separate process via Ombud)

## ANSWER: IS LAKEWOOD'S ETHICS VACUUM A CHOICE OR AN INEVITABILITY?

### Verdict: IT IS A CHOICE — AND A DELIBERATE ONE

**Argument for "inevitability" (rejected):**
- "Lakewood is a small code city with limited resources"
- "Most WA code cities don't have ethics boards"
- "The state law (RCW 42.23) provides enough baseline"

**Counter-evidence for "choice":**

1. **Cost is negligible.** Lynnwood's 5-member citizen board costs nothing (volunteers). Kirkland's independent Ethics Officer is a single contracted position. Federal Way's board meets "as needed." The claim of resource constraints is not credible when cities of 40K-60K population have found the resources.

2. **Three comparable code cities chose differently.** Kirkland (92K, code city), Federal Way (99K, code city), and Lynnwood (40K, code city) are all code cities with elected councils and similar resource bases. All three chose to create ethics enforcement. Lakewood is the same type of city making a different choice.

3. **Lakewood's problems are objectively worse and would benefit most from oversight.** At 71% dual-government employment (5 of 7), Lakewood has MORE conflict-of-interest surface area than any peer — and LESS oversight. This is the worst possible combination: high risk + zero mitigation.

4. **The recusal denial pattern in Lakewood is structural.** Single-council districts mean a conflicted council member's recusal leaves their district unrepresented — creating pressure NOT to recuse. This is a design flaw that an ethics board could address through advisory opinions and clear procedures.

5. **No evidence of any council proposal to create an ethics board.** In Lynnwood, the ethics board was created by council ordinance. In Tacoma, by council ordinance. In Federal Way, by council ordinance. The absence of any such proposal in Lakewood over 16+ years of documented dual-government conflict is a pattern of non-action that cannot be explained by ignorance.

### The Real Answer

**Lakewood's ethics vacuum is a policy choice sustained by the council itself.** Creating an ethics board would require a council vote — the same council with the highest dual-government rate in the region. The same council that has not recused itself in 16+ years. The same council that would be subject to the board's oversight.

This is the core political economy problem: **the people who would need to create ethics oversight are the people who would be overseen by it.** In a system with high dual-government employment, the incentives to maintain the status quo are higher, not lower.

### What "Good" Looks Like — A Benchmark

Drawing from the three expanded cities, a functional municipal ethics system has:

| Component | Best Practice | Example |
|-----------|--------------|---------|
| **Independent enforcement** | Not controlled by council or city attorney | Kirkland (contracted independent officer), Tacoma (citizen board) |
| **Clear complaint process** | Written, sworn complaint; clear sufficiency standard | Federal Way (Section 7), Kirkland (KMC 3.14.060-.080) |
| **Subpoena power** | Ability to compel evidence | Federal Way Ethics Code Section 7 |
| **Annual reporting** | Public transparency on activity | Tacoma (TMC 1.46.045(C)(4)) |
| **Judicial review** | Appeal path ensures accountability | Tacoma (Superior Court review) |
| **Recusal codification** | Explicit requirement, not discretionary | Federal Way Code Section 4 |
| **Proactive recusal culture** | Conflict-of-interest as standing agenda item | Lacey |
| **Financial disclosure** | Annual filings required | Kirkland (KMC 3.14.040) |

**Lakewood has exactly zero of these eight components.**
