# Pierce County Power Matrix — Top 25 Ranked Actors

## Methodology

Each actor (person or organization) scored across 6 power domains on a 0-10 scale:

1. **Government** — Elected/appointed authority, budget control, veto/decision power
2. **Economic** — Capital, employment, contracts, development control  
3. **Institutional** — Military, healthcare, tribal, educational authority
4. **Political** — Party machinery, fundraising, endorsements, labor ties
5. **Legal/Judicial** — Prosecutorial discretion, bench power, sentencing
6. **Media/Narrative** — News ownership, editorial control, narrative reach

**Composite Score** = Weighted average (Gov 25%, Econ 25%, Inst 20%, Political 15%, Legal 10%, Media 5%)
**Network Score** = From NetworkX eigenvector + betweenness + weighted degree analysis
**Final Rank** = Blend of Domain Power + Network Position

## Key Patterns

1. **Government + Money = Power.** The highest scores belong to those who control both public authority AND economic resources (Mello, Council, Tribe, MultiCare, Port).

2. **The Sheriff Paradox.** Swank ranks #7 despite having the 2nd largest budget — because his power is concentrated in one domain (law enforcement/safety) and he's in active conflict with the executive. His autonomy makes him dangerous, but the isolation limits network influence.

3. **The Party Money Pipeline.** Both parties score high on political power, but the **GOP has more concentrated money** ($175K to Chambers from party) while the **Dems have broader coalition** (unions, HDCC, progressive PACs, tribe).

4. **Media is a hollow power.** The News Tribune has narrative control (only daily paper) but is owned by an out-of-state hedge fund and its editor edits 3 other papers. No locally owned TV exists. Media as an institution is **extracted from the community** rather than embedded.

5. **The Interlock Economy.** John Wiborg (MultiCare/Stellar/Chamber/GTCF/UW Tacoma) and John McCarthy (Port/FMSIB/PSRC) show how a handful of people bridge institutional power.

6. **Tribal Sovereignty = Structural Power.** The Puyallup Tribe is the only actor that can *opt out* of county jurisdiction. That structural independence, combined with gaming revenue and political spending, makes them the most powerful non-governmental entity.
