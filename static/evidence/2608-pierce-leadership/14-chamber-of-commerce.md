## 4. CANDIDATE ACADEMY

### Overview
The Candidate Academy is a program of the Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber described as "a unique opportunity for local, civic-minded individuals who are considering running for office." It is explicitly designed to recruit, train, and vet potential candidates for elected office.

### Format
- **Duration:** Intensive 1-day primer
- **Curriculum:**
  1. Civics 101: How Governments Interact and Work
  2. Panel: The Good, Bad, and Ugly of Politics — How to Survive being a Candidate and Elected Official
  3. Campaign Strategy and Operations: Putting the Pieces Together
  4. Election Filing Guide
  5. Fundraising and Event Planning
  6. Getting Out Your Message: Media, Mailings, and Doorbelling
  7. Managing Your Campaign: People, Process, and Budget

### Key Features
- Participants meet local elected officials and community advocates
- Learn differences between levels of government and elected positions
- Become "better equipped to serve as a candidate and elected or appointed official"
- Program assists in determining "whether to throw their hat in the ring"
- Networking with "local leaders and elected officials who can offer support, advice, and guidance"

### Known Alumni
The Chamber does not publish a public alumni list for the Candidate Academy on its website. This is a significant information gap — the program's alumni are not transparently disclosed. However, based on investigative reporting context:

- The phrase "Chamber candidate" is a known political epithet in Tacoma politics, suggesting the Candidate Academy has produced identifiable candidates who are perceived as Chamber-aligned (per Matt Driscoll, Tacoma News Tribune columnist, 2015 column).

### Strategic Significance
The Candidate Academy functions as an **informal candidate vetting and training pipeline** — the Chamber trains aspiring candidates on the mechanics of running for office while simultaneously building relationships and networks. This gives the Chamber disproportionate influence over who enters the candidate pool for local offices, especially at the City of Tacoma and Pierce County levels.

**Source:** https://www.tacomachamber.org/candidateacademy.html

## 6. POLITICAL ENDORSEMENTS & ELECTION ACTIVITY

### Official Position
The Chamber **officially states it does not endorse candidates**. Its Voters Guide page says: *"The Chamber does not endorse candidates. Our objective is to promote information on the election cycle and candidates within it so that voters can make informed decisions."*

### Reality: The "Chamber Candidate" Phenomenon
Despite the official non-endorsement policy, the Chamber has a well-documented history of being perceived as a political kingmaker:

1. **Matt Driscoll Column (The News Tribune, ~2015):** In a column titled *"Is fear over a 'Chamber candidate' for Tacoma City Council warranted?"*, Driscoll documented that "Chamber candidate" is a recognized political label in Tacoma. He noted that since Tom Pierson's arrival as CEO in 2011, the Chamber "has taken a more active approach in local politics." The column reported that the phrase "Chamber candidate" is used by critics to describe candidates perceived as business-aligned.

2. **Candidates Forum:** The Chamber hosts an annual Candidates Forum featuring finalists for Tacoma Mayor and City Council. In 2025, this was held at University of Puget Sound featuring candidates for Mayor and Council positions 2, 4, 5, and 6.

3. **Voter Guide:** The Chamber publishes a "Vote for Business Voters Guide" that candidates respond to with 600-word answers. It is widely distributed to members. Candidates who don't respond are noted. This effectively functions as a de facto endorsement signal — favorable coverage for responsive candidates.

4. **Advocacy Fund:** The Chamber operates an "Advocacy Fund" that "powers the education, engagement, and influence needed to advance policies." While described as issue-focused, the fund directly supports the Chamber's political engagement infrastructure.

5. **Instagram Political Activity:** The Chamber's Instagram account (@tacoma_chamber, 5,242 followers) actively promotes candidate content, including candidate videos (e.g., Latasha Palmer for Tacoma Council-At-Large Position 6) and election reminders.

### Key Insight
The Chamber's power operates through **indirect political influence** rather than direct PAC-style endorsements. By controlling the candidate training pipeline (Candidate Academy), the voter information ecosystem (Voters Guide), and the candidate visibility platform (Candidates Forum), the Chamber shapes the political landscape without formally endorsing.

### Comparison: Labor Endorsements
By contrast, the Pierce County Central Labor Council (AFL-CIO) publishes explicit endorsements. For example, in the 2025 general election, labor endorsed Anders Ibsen for Tacoma Mayor, Latasha Palmer for Council-at-Large Position 6, and Silong Chhun for District 4. The Chamber does not publish equivalent endorsements but its informal influence arguably rivals labor's formal endorsement power.

| Activity | Description |

| **Legislative Advocacy Day** | Annual trip to Olympia; Chamber members meet with state legislators directly |
| **Washington-to-Washington, D.C. Conference** | Federal advocacy trip to Washington D.C. |

| **Public Officials Holiday Reception** | Networking event for elected officials and Chamber members |
| **Advocacy Fund** | Dedicated fund for policy education, engagement, and influence |

### 2026 Legislative Agenda (Published)
1. Protect small businesses from new taxes and fees
2. Improve public safety for businesses, customers, and employees
3. Oppose new employment mandates that increase costs / create administrative burdens
4. Protect Puget Sound Gateway, Connecting Washington, and Move Ahead Washington transportation funding
5. Advance Washington's goal of doubling manufacturing employment base by 2031
6. Strengthen talent pipeline for technology-advanced manufacturing
7. Support policy solutions to address housing shortage — **while opposing price/market controls**

### The Chamber will support legislation that:
- Positively impacts Pierce County's economy and business climate
- Promotes private sector job growth and capital investment (especially manufacturing)
- Provides enhanced funding for education and workforce development
- Builds attainable housing of all types
- Decreases regulatory burden on businesses
- Improves public safety
- Supports legislative priorities of Chamber's partner entities

### Manufacturing Industrial Council (MIC)
The Chamber runs the South Sound Manufacturing Industrial Council, which:
- Is funded through grants including City of Tacoma and state Commerce funding
- Has a dedicated Program Director (Ryan Spence)
- Reports to the City of Tacoma on manufacturing policy
- Has driven the Tacoma Manufacturing Incubator — adaptive reuse of 225,000 sq ft historic facility
- Combines $200,000 Evergreen Manufacturing Grant (WA Dept of Commerce), $85,000 City of Tacoma matching funds, and $5.8M in private capital
- Aims to create 15–20 new manufacturing businesses over three years

### Public-Private Partnership Infrastructure
The Chamber is embedded in Tacoma's formal economic development ecosystem:
- **Spaceworks Tacoma:** Joint initiative of the City of Tacoma and the Chamber
- **Manufacturing Industrial Council:** Chamber-led, reported to City Council
- **Pierce County Business Accelerator:** Chamber program for BIPOC, veteran, and women-owned businesses
- **Economic Development Board:** Chamber has board representation (Michael Catsi sits on Chamber board)
- **Kiva Microloans:** Chamber facilitated $1.3M in 0% interest microloans

## 11. POWER ANALYSIS

### Institutional Power Score: CRITICAL (9/10)

The Tacoma-Pierce County Chamber of Commerce is a dominant power institution in Pierce County for the following reasons:

1. **Shadow Political Party Function:** Despite officially stating it does not endorse candidates, the Chamber operates a Candidate Academy that trains and vets aspiring politicians, publishes a widely-distributed Voters Guide, and hosts a Candidates Forum. The term "Chamber candidate" is a recognized label in Tacoma politics.

2. **Government Integration:** The Chamber is formally embedded in City of Tacoma and Pierce County economic development planning. County and city agencies hold seats on its board. Its former CEO simultaneously served as Acting Director of Economic Development for Pierce County.

3. **Financial Capacity:** With ~$3M annual revenue, ~1,400 members, and sophisticated advocacy infrastructure (Advocacy Fund, Legislative Advocacy Day, D.C. conference), the Chamber has resources far beyond a typical civic organization.

4. **Institutional Longevity:** 141 years of continuous operation gives the Chamber unmatched institutional memory and relationship networks.

5. **Cross-Sector Board:** The board includes representatives from healthcare (MultiCare, Virginia Mason), banking (KeyBank), energy (Puget Sound Energy), tech (Comcast, DoorDash), aerospace (Boeing), higher education (UW Tacoma, CPTC), public transit (Pierce Transit), port (Port of Tacoma), and utilities (TPU) — creating a unified business voice across all major sectors.

6. **Data Authority:** The Chamber's Pierce County Economic Index report positions it as the authoritative source on regional economic conditions — a form of narrative control.

### Key Vulnerabilities / Information Gaps
- Candidate Academy alumni list is not publicly published
- Advocacy Fund donors are not disclosed
- The relationship between the Chamber's Voters Guide and de facto endorsement patterns needs further investigation
- The FY2025 audit finding of "significant deficiency in internal controls" warrants attention
- Tom Pierson's dual role as Acting Director of Economic Development for Pierce County while serving as Interim Chamber CEO creates an unprecedented conflict of interest that merits deeper investigation
