## Overview: The Unmapped Power Layer

Pierce County has 20+ fire protection districts, each an independent municipal corporation under **RCW Title 52** with its own elected Board of Fire Commissioners (3–7 members, 6-year terms) and **independent property taxing authority**. Commissioners set budgets, levy property taxes, impose Fire Benefit Charges (FBC), approve IAFF labor contracts, and direct capital projects — with no county-level oversight. This is a CRITICAL unmapped power layer per the supplementary gap analysis (evidence/20-supplementary-gaps.md).

**2025–26 consolidation wave** is rapidly concentrating this power into fewer, larger districts:
- **Central Pierce Fire & Rescue** absorbed Districts 18 (Orting) & 21 (Graham) — effective Jan 1, 2026 → now 2nd-largest fire district in WA
- **Pierce 23** merged with Lewis County District 17 (Big Creek) → Lewis-Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 49 (2025)
- **Central Pierce + South Pierce** entered an administrative contract for services (July 1, 2026) and held a joint board meeting (Sept 2, 2026) — a de facto pre-merger step
- **East Pierce (Dist 22)** has absorbed Districts 1, 8, 12, 20, 24 over 1999–2013

- WSRB Class 2 rating (only fire dept in Pierce County with Class 2; <2.5% statewide)

## 9. The 2025–26 Consolidation Wave (Key Findings)

1. **Central Pierce mega-merger (Dist 6+18+21):** Approved April 22, 2025; effective Jan 1, 2026. Created WA's 2nd-largest fire district (331K residents, 178 sq mi, 20 stations, ~550 firefighters, ~$180M budget). Board grew to 12 commissioners.

2. **Pierce 23 + Lewis 17:** Approved Feb 11, 2025 → Lewis-Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 49.

3. **Central Pierce + South Pierce:** Administrative contract for services effective July 1, 2026; joint board meeting Sept 2, 2026. Public comments (Facebook) explicitly note "West Pierce and South Pierce could join also" — further consolidation is anticipated.

4. **East Pierce (Dist 22)** is the historical consolidator on the east side (absorbed Dists 1, 8, 12, 20, 24, 1999–2013) and now runs a 7-member board.

5. **West Pierce (Dist 3)** absorbed Dist 2 (Lakewood) in 2011 and contracts with Steilacoom; evaluating a 7th station.

**Net effect:** Pierce County's ~20 fire districts are consolidating into a handful of regional giants (Central, West, East, Gig Harbor, South, Key Peninsula), each with independent taxing authority and elected boards — concentrating fire-service political and fiscal power with no county-level check.

## Power-Map Implications (for INV-2026-002)

- **Independent taxing authority:** Each board sets property levies + FBC without county approval — a direct, voter-accountable fiscal lever.
- **Labor power:** IAFF locals (e.g., East Pierce Local 3520, Pierce County Professional Firefighters) negotiate directly with boards; personnel = 84% of West Pierce budget.
- **Capital concentration:** $80M+ bond programs (East Pierce, Gig Harbor), $9.85M non-voter-approved bonds (West Pierce) — boards direct major infrastructure spending.
- **Consolidation = power concentration:** Merger wave is reducing the number of elected commissioner seats while enlarging the budgets each remaining board controls.
- **Commissioner overlap with other power centers:** Dellinger (West Pierce) is a PSRFA Captain; Casebolt (West Pierce) is a former Gig Harbor commissioner; several commissioners serve on business associations, parks boards, and community councils.
